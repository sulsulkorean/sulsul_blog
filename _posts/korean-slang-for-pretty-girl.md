The everyday Korean word for "pretty" is **예쁘다** (*ye-ppeu-da*). Say **예뻐요** (*ye-ppeo-yo*) to tell someone "You're pretty," or **예쁘다!** when the word just slips out. When Koreans want to say a girl is *really* pretty, they reach for slang: **존예** (*jon-ye*, "stunning"), **여신** (*yeo-sin*, "goddess"), and **얼짱** (*eol-jjang*, "the best face").

> - **예쁘다 / 예뻐요** — pretty — safe for anyone
> - **진짜 예쁘다** — really pretty — the most common compliment
> - **존예** — drop-dead gorgeous — friends only, very casual
> - **여신** — goddess — fans about actresses and idols
> - **얼짱** — "face champion," the prettiest face around
> - **미모** — looks, beauty — "her looks are unreal"

Below: the plain words first, then the slang by how strong it is, how K-drama characters use these lines, and the mistakes that make a compliment land wrong.

## 예쁘다 is the word you hear most

Before any slang, learn **예쁘다**. It covers about 80 percent of the compliments you hear in K-dramas, on variety shows, and between friends.

| Korean | Romanization | Use it with |
|---|---|---|
| **예뻐** | *ye-ppeo* | a close friend, a partner, a younger sibling |
| **예뻐요** | *ye-ppeo-yo* | almost anyone, polite and warm |
| **예쁘세요** | *ye-ppeu-se-yo* | someone older, a shop owner, a stranger |
| **예쁘다!** | *ye-ppeu-da!* | talking to yourself, reacting to a photo |

The fourth one matters. **예쁘다!** with a falling tone is not really aimed at the person — it is a reaction. You say it when a friend sends a selfie, when an actress walks onto the screen, or when the male lead sees the heroine in a dress for the first time.

Two close cousins:

- **귀엽다** (*gwi-yeop-da*) — cute. Pretty with a playful, younger feel.
- **아름답다** (*a-reum-dap-da*) — beautiful. Sounds like poetry or a speech. Nobody says it to a friend over coffee, but you hear it in wedding scenes and OST lyrics.

## The slang, from mild to over the top

Korean slang for a pretty girl is mostly about **turning the volume up**. Each word below is a little stronger than the one before.

**미인** (*mi-in*) — a beauty. Not slang, but older people use it as a compliment: **미인이시네요** (*mi-i-ni-si-ne-yo*), "You're a real beauty." A grandma at a market might say this to you.

**미모** (*mi-mo*) — good looks. Fans love it in comments: **미모 미쳤다** (*mi-mo mi-chyeot-da*), "Her looks are insane." The word **미쳤다** (crazy) is a compliment here, not an insult.

**얼짱** (*eol-jjang*) — from **얼굴** (face) + **짱** (the best). It began in the early internet days to name the prettiest girl in school, and it still shows up in dramas set in high school. Today it sounds a little retro, which is why characters sometimes say it as a joke.

**여신** (*yeo-sin*) — goddess. Fans attach it to a name or a moment: **비주얼 여신** (visual goddess), **무대 여신** (stage goddess). You will read it in captions more often than you hear it in conversation.

**존예** (*jon-ye*) — short for a rude intensifier + **예쁘다**. It means "ridiculously pretty." It is very common among people in their teens and twenties, but it contains a swear-word root, so keep it for close friends and group chats. Never say it to a teacher, a boss, or a stranger.

**예쁨 주의** (*ye-ppeum ju-ui*) — "Warning: pretty." Playful caption slang for a photo post.

## How K-drama characters say it

The words matter less than the timing. In dramas, the compliment usually comes in one of three scenes.

**The surprised reaction.** The heroine comes down the stairs in a new outfit. The male lead freezes, then mutters:

- **예쁘네.** (*ye-ppeu-ne*) — "Huh. You look pretty."

The ending **-네** shows he just noticed. It sounds honest, not rehearsed, which is why it hits harder than a big speech.

**The best friend hype.** Two friends get ready for a date:

- A: **나 어때?** (*na eo-ttae?*) — "How do I look?"
- B: **대박, 존예. 오늘 여신이다.** (*dae-bak, jon-ye. o-neul yeo-si-ni-da*) — "Wow, gorgeous. You're a goddess today."
- A: **진짜? 거짓말 아니지?** (*jin-jja? geo-jit-mal a-ni-ji?*) — "Really? You're not lying?"
- B: **완전 예뻐. 빨리 가.** (*wan-jeon ye-ppeo. ppal-li ga*) — "Totally pretty. Go, hurry."

**The shy confession.** The line is short on purpose:

- **너 오늘 좀 예쁘다.** (*neo o-neul jom ye-ppeu-da*) — "You're kind of pretty today."

That little **좀** (a bit) softens it, because saying it straight would be too embarrassing for him. Korean viewers know it means "very."

## Making it polite, or making it stronger

You can build almost any compliment with **예쁘다** plus one helper word.

| Add this | Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **진짜** (really) | **진짜 예뻐요.** | You're really pretty. |
| **너무** (so) | **너무 예뻐요!** | You're so pretty! |
| **완전** (totally) | **완전 예뻐.** | Totally pretty. (casual) |
| **오늘** (today) | **오늘 예뻐 보여요.** | You look pretty today. |
| a thing | **원피스 예뻐요.** | Your dress is pretty. |

The last row is the safest compliment in Korea. Praising the **dress**, the **hair** (**머리 예뻐요**), or the **nails** (**손톱 예뻐요**) is friendly and never awkward, even with a coworker or a shop clerk.

## Mistakes to avoid

**Using 존예 with the wrong person.** It is fun with friends, rude with elders. When in doubt, say **진짜 예뻐요**.

**Calling a man 예쁘다 without meaning it.** Koreans do call male idols **예쁘다**, and fans mean it kindly. But to a man you just met, **잘생겼어요** (*jal-saeng-gyeo-sseo-yo*, "you're handsome") is the expected word.

**Commenting on someone's face at work.** In an office scene, characters praise clothes or style, not faces. **스타일 좋아요** ("nice style") works better than **얼굴 예뻐요**.

**Mixing up 예뻐요 and 예뻐서요.** **예뻐서요** means "because it's pretty." If someone asks why you bought a mug, that is the right answer. As a compliment, use **예뻐요**.

**Not answering a compliment.** If someone says **예뻐요** to you, a simple **고마워요** (*go-ma-wo-yo*) is perfect. A playful **에이, 아니에요** ("Oh, no, I'm not") is also very Korean.
