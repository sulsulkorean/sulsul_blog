**마라탕** (*malatang*) shops do not really have a menu. You are handed a metal bowl and a pair of tongs, pointed at a wall of bins, and expected to build the dish yourself before anyone speaks to you. The two sentences that carry the whole visit are **여기 담으면 돼요?** (*yeogi dameumyeon dwaeyo?*), "Do I put it in here?" and **고수 빼 주세요** (*gosu ppae juseyo*), "No cilantro, please" — the second one only works if you say it before the pot goes on.

> - Check the system: **여기 담으면 돼요?** (*yeogi dameumyeon dwaeyo?*), "Do I put it in here?"
> - Ask about noodles: **면은 뭐 있어요?** (*myeoneun mwo isseoyo?*), "What noodles do you have?"
> - Keep it mild: **안 맵게 해 주세요** (*an maepge hae juseyo*), "Please make it not spicy."
> - Skip the cilantro: **고수 빼 주세요** (*gosu ppae juseyo*), "No cilantro, please."
> - Ask for more soup: **국물 더 주세요** (*gungmul deo juseyo*), "More broth, please."

## How does the bar actually work?

Nobody explains it, because every Korean customer in the room has done it since middle school.

1. **여기 담으면 돼요?** holding up the bowl, which sorts out the one thing signs never make obvious.
2. Fill it from the bins with the tongs — vegetables, mushrooms, fish cake, sliced meat, whatever you recognise.
3. **면은 뭐 있어요?** at the noodle section, since the chewy wide **넓적당면** and the thin **중국당면** behave completely differently in broth.
4. Hand the bowl over the counter, then **안 맵게 해 주세요** and **고수 빼 주세요** in that order, before they start cooking.
5. Take the number, sit down, and use **국물 더 주세요** when it arrives thicker than you expected.

| Moment | Say this |
|---|---|
| Picking up the bowl | 여기 담으면 돼요? (*yeogi dameumyeon dwaeyo?*) |
| At the noodle bin | 면은 뭐 있어요? (*myeoneun mwo isseoyo?*) |
| Handing it over | 안 맵게 해 주세요 (*an maepge hae juseyo*) |
| Before it cooks | 고수 빼 주세요 (*gosu ppae juseyo*) |
| At the table | 국물 더 주세요 (*gungmul deo juseyo*) |

## What do Korean speakers say back?

The counter is fast and the staff ask everything in one breath, so the skill is catching which question just landed.

You: **여기 담으면 돼요?** — *yeogi dameumyeon dwaeyo?* — "Do I put it in here?"  
Them: **네, 담아서 저울에 올려 주세요** — *ne, damaseo jeoure ollyeo juseyo* — "Yes, fill it and put it on the scale."  
You: **면은 뭐 있어요?** — *myeoneun mwo isseoyo?* — "What noodles do you have?"  
Them: **중국당면이랑 넓적당면 있어요. 맵기는요?** — *junggukdangmyeonirang neopjeokdangmyeon isseoyo. maepgineunyo?* — "Glass noodles and wide noodles. And the spice?"  
You: **안 맵게 해 주세요. 고수 빼 주세요** — *an maepge hae juseyo. gosu ppae juseyo* — "Not spicy, and no cilantro."  
Them: **번호표 들고 앉아 계세요** — *beonhopyo deulgo anja gyeseyo* — "Take the number tag and have a seat."

**저울** (*jeoul*) is the scale — malatang is sold by the weight of what you picked, so the bowl you build is the order. For the other place Korean sells heat in numbered steps, see [Korean phrases for picking a spice level at a tteokbokki shop](/korean-phrases-tteokbokki-spice-level).

## What usually goes wrong at a malatang shop?

The most common one is filling the bowl like a buffet plate. Everything on that bar is raw and the leafy things collapse to nothing, so a bowl that looks modest at the bins can arrive as far more food than you wanted. If you have overdone it, **덜어도 돼요?** (*deoreodo dwaeyo?*), "Can I take some back out?" is a normal thing to ask before it is weighed, and nobody minds.

The second is the cilantro. Many shops add **고수** by default, and once it is in the broth it cannot come out, which is why **고수 빼 주세요** belongs at the counter and not at the table. The third is the name: **마라탕** is the soup, while **마라샹궈** (*malasyanggwo*) is the same bar of ingredients stir-fried dry with no broth at all. If a real allergy rather than a preference is in play, the sentences for that are in [Korean phrases for food allergies and diet restrictions](/korean-phrases-food-allergies-korea).
