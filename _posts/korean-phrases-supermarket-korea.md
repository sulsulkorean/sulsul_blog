Korean phrases for shopping at a supermarket in Korea come down to Pattern 01 from the SULSUL workbook — noun plus **주세요** (*juseyo*), "please give me." Point at an item and say **이거 주세요** (*igeo juseyo*), "This one, please," then at checkout ask **봉투 주세요** (*bongtu juseyo*) when you need a bag.

> - Point and buy: **이거 주세요** (*igeo juseyo*), "This one, please."
> - Ask for a bag: **봉투 주세요** (*bongtu juseyo*), "Bag, please."
> - Check stock: **이거 있어요?** (*igeo isseoyo?*), "Do you have this?"
> - Find checkout: **계산대 어디예요?** (*gyesandae eodiyeyo?*), "Where is the checkout counter?"

## How do you grab something from a shelf?

Large Korean supermarkets — **마트** (*mateu*, mart) — label most aisles in Korean and English, but staff on the floor still answer quick questions in Korean only. Hold the item up and use the textbook request line.

You: **저기요, 이거 있어요?** — *jeogiyo, igeo isseoyo?* — "Excuse me, do you have this?"  
Them: **네, 저쪽 냉장고에 있어요.** — *ne, jeojjok naengjanggo-e isseoyo.* — "Yes, it is in the refrigerator over there."  
You: **감사합니다.** — *gamsahamnida.* — "Thank you."

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Buy what you are pointing at | 이거 주세요 (*igeo juseyo*) |
| Buy two of the same item | 이거 두 개 주세요 (*igeo du gae juseyo*) |
| Ask if they stock it | 이거 있어요? (*igeo isseoyo?*) |
| Need a shopping bag | 봉투 주세요 (*bongtu juseyo*) |
| Pay by card | 카드 돼요? (*kadeu dwaeyo?*) |

## What happens at the checkout counter?

Self-checkout kiosks are common, but staffed lanes still handle questions about bags, receipts, and membership cards. Listen for **봉투** (*bongtu*, bag) and **영수증** (*yeongsujeung*, receipt).

You: **봉투 주세요.** — *bongtu juseyo.* — "Bag, please."  
Them: **500원 추가예요.** — *obaek won chugayeyo.* — "That adds 500 won."  
You: **네, 괜찮아요.** — *ne, gwaenchanayo.* — "Yes, that is fine."

Korean stores often charge a small fee for plastic bags. If you brought your own, say **봉투 필요 없어요** (*bongtu piryo eopseoyo*), "I do not need a bag." For finding the register in a department store, see [Korean phrases for finding the checkout counter](/korean-phrases-finding-checkout-counter) — the same **계산대 어디예요?** (*gyesandae eodiyeyo?*) line works there too.

## What usually goes wrong before you leave?

Travelers often freeze at the weigh-your-own produce section. Look for a scale near the fruit — staff expect you to bag and label items yourself before checkout. If you are unsure, ask **이거 어떻게 해요?** (*igeo eotteoke haeyo?*), "How do I do this?"

You: **계산대 어디예요?** — *gyesandae eodiyeyo?* — "Where is the checkout counter?"  
Them: **저쪽 끝이에요.** — *jeojjok kkeut-ieyo.* — "At the far end over there."  
You: **영수증 주세요.** — *yeongsujeung juseyo.* — "Receipt, please."

A second mistake is saying **사고 싶어요** (*sago sipeoyo*), "I want to buy it," at a staffed counter when **이거 주세요** (*igeo juseyo*) is faster and more natural. Save **고 싶어요** (*go sipeoyo*) for when you are browsing and chatting — at checkout, point and request.
