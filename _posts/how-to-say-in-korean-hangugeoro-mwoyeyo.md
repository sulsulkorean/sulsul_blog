To ask how to say something in Korean, say the word in English (or point at the thing) and ask **한국어로 뭐예요?** (*han-gu-geo-ro mwo-ye-yo?*): "What is it in Korean?" It works with any Korean person, in any shop, at any age gap, because it ends in polite 요. If you want to sound a little more natural when asking about a word or phrase, use **한국어로 뭐라고 해요?** (*han-gu-geo-ro mwo-ra-go hae-yo?*), "What do you call it in Korean?" These two questions are the most useful sentences a beginner can carry, because every other word you need can be pulled out with them.

> - What is it in Korean?: **한국어로 뭐예요?** — *han-gu-geo-ro mwo-ye-yo?*
> - What do you call it in Korean?: **한국어로 뭐라고 해요?** — *han-gu-geo-ro mwo-ra-go hae-yo?*
> - What is this?: **이거 뭐예요?** — *i-geo mwo-ye-yo?*
> - What does it mean?: **무슨 뜻이에요?** — *mu-seun tteu-si-e-yo?*
> - Please say it again: **다시 말해 주세요** — *da-si mal-hae ju-se-yo*
> - Could you write it down?: **써 주세요** — *sseo ju-se-yo*

## Break the question into three pieces

The sentence looks long, but it is only three small parts glued together, and once you see them you can rebuild it any time.

- **한국어** (*han-gu-geo*) means the Korean language.
- **로** (*ro*) means "in" or "by way of." Stuck onto 한국어 it becomes 한국어로, "in Korean."
- **뭐예요?** (*mwo-ye-yo?*) means "What is it?"

So 한국어로 뭐예요? is literally "In Korean, what is it?" Swap the first piece and the same question works for other languages: **영어로 뭐예요?** (*yeong-eo-ro mwo-ye-yo?*) is "What is it in English?" That reverse version is handy when a Korean friend says a word you don't know and you want the English to anchor it.

Raise your voice at the end, on 요. In Korean the same words can be a statement or a question, and the rising tone is what tells the listener you are asking.

## Put the English word in front

If you can't point at the thing, say the English word first and add the particle **이/가**. Use **이** after a word that ends in a consonant sound and **가** after a vowel sound. Beginners do not need to be perfect here, because people understand you either way.

- **Spoon이 한국어로 뭐예요?** — "What is 'spoon' in Korean?"
- **Receipt가 한국어로 뭐예요?** — "What is 'receipt' in Korean?"
- **"Excuse me"가 한국어로 뭐예요?** — "How do you say 'excuse me' in Korean?"

The answer usually comes back short: **숟가락이에요** (*sut-ga-ra-gi-e-yo*), "It's 숟가락." Repeat it once out loud. Koreans are used to learners doing this and will often say it again, slower, without being asked.

When it's a whole sentence you want, not one word, **뭐라고 해요?** fits better: **"Can I sit here?" 한국어로 뭐라고 해요?** The person will often answer with the full line, like 여기 앉아도 돼요?, which you can use right away.

## When the thing is right in front of you

At a market stall, a bakery counter, or a menu with pictures, you don't need the English at all. Point and ask **이거 뭐예요?** (*i-geo mwo-ye-yo?*), "What is this?" It is one of the first patterns in the SULSUL textbook for exactly this reason: it turns everything you can see into vocabulary.

![A learner chatting with a Korean speaker and asking what a word is](/assets/blog/scenes/small-talk.jpg)

Pair it with **이거 이름이 뭐예요?** (*i-geo i-reu-mi mwo-ye-yo?*), "What is this called?", when the shop owner answers with a description instead of a name. If you hear something like "떡이에요, 쌀로 만든 거예요," you've just learned that 떡 is rice cake and 쌀 is rice, all from one question.

## Asking what a word means

The opposite problem is hearing a Korean word and not knowing it. For that, say the word you heard and add **무슨 뜻이에요?** (*mu-seun tteu-si-e-yo?*), "What does it mean?"

- **"포장" 무슨 뜻이에요?** — "What does 포장 mean?"
- **그게 무슨 뜻이에요?** — "What does that mean?"

If you only caught part of it, don't guess. Use the textbook pattern for understanding:

- **잘 모르겠어요.** (*jal mo-reu-ge-sseo-yo*) — "I don't quite get it."
- **잘 알겠어요.** (*jal al-ge-sseo-yo*) — "Got it, I understand."

잘 모르겠어요 is much softer than a flat "I don't know," and it invites the other person to explain again instead of giving up on you.

## Getting it said again, slower, or written down

Even when someone answers kindly, Korean words go by fast. These three requests keep the conversation alive:

- **다시 말해 주세요.** (*da-si mal-hae ju-se-yo*) — "Please say it again."
- **천천히 말해 주세요.** (*cheon-cheon-hi mal-hae ju-se-yo*) — "Please say it slowly."
- **여기 써 주세요.** (*yeo-gi sseo ju-se-yo*) — "Please write it here." Hold out your phone's notes app.

The last one is the secret weapon. Once a word is written in Hangul, you can read it, save it, and look it up later. Shop staff and taxi drivers are usually happy to type a word on your phone.

## A short scene at a street stall

> **You:** (pointing) 저기요, 이거 뭐예요?
> *jeo-gi-yo, i-geo mwo-ye-yo?* — Excuse me, what is this?
>
> **Owner:** 호떡이에요. 달아요.
> *ho-tteo-gi-e-yo. da-ra-yo.* — It's hotteok. It's sweet.
>
> **You:** 죄송해요, 다시 말해 주세요.
> *joe-song-hae-yo, da-si mal-hae ju-se-yo.* — Sorry, please say it again.
>
> **Owner:** 호-떡.
>
> **You:** 아, 호떡! "Sweet"이 한국어로 뭐예요?
> *a, ho-tteok! "sweet"-i han-gu-geo-ro mwo-ye-yo?* — Ah, hotteok! What is "sweet" in Korean?
>
> **Owner:** 달아요. 달다.
>
> **You:** 아, 잘 알겠어요. 호떡 하나 주세요!
> *a, jal al-ge-sseo-yo. ho-tteok ha-na ju-se-yo!* — Oh, got it. One hotteok, please!

Two questions, one repeat, and you walked away with two words and a snack.

## Slips that make people switch to English

- **Saying "in Korea" instead of "in Korean."** 한국에서 (*han-gu-ge-seo*) means "in Korea," the place. 한국에서 뭐예요? sounds like you're asking what something is in the country. The language is 한국어, so keep 한국어로.
- **Dropping 요.** 한국어로 뭐야? is casual speech for close friends. With a stranger or shop owner, keep 뭐예요.
- **Asking with a flat tone.** Without the rise at the end, 한국어로 뭐예요 can sound like you're muttering to yourself. Lift the last syllable.
- **Apologizing too much first.** A long "sorry, my Korean is bad" makes people reach for English to help you. A bright 저기요 followed straight by the question keeps them in Korean.
- **Not repeating the answer.** If you just nod, the person doesn't know if you got it. Say the word back. It shows you're learning, and they'll correct you if it's off.

## Make it automatic before you need it

These questions only work if they come out without a pause. Say them out loud now, five times each, with the rise at the end: 한국어로 뭐예요? 이거 뭐예요? 무슨 뜻이에요? 다시 말해 주세요. Then pick three things on your desk and ask about each one in English plus 이/가 한국어로 뭐예요.
