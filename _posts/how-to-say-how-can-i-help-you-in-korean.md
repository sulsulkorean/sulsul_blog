"How can I help you?" in Korean is **뭘 도와드릴까요?** (*mwol dowadeurilkkayo?*). It is polite, it is what a hotel front desk or an information counter will say, and it is also what you can say when you are the one offering. In a shop, though, staff rarely say it word for word. You will far more often hear **찾으시는 거 있으세요?** (*chajeusineun geo isseuseyo?*), "Are you looking for something?" — the Korean shop version of "Can I help you?"

> - **뭘 도와드릴까요?** — How can I help you? (counters, front desks)
> - **도와드릴까요?** — Can I help you? / Shall I help?
> - **찾으시는 거 있으세요?** — Looking for anything in particular? (shops)
> - **어서 오세요** — Welcome (said as you walk in)
> - **필요하신 거 있으면 말씀하세요** — Let me know if you need anything
> - **그냥 구경하는 거예요** — I'm just looking (your answer)

So when you search "how can I help you in Korean," you are really looking for two things: the line staff say, and the line you say back. Most travelers only learn the first half. This post covers both, starting with how the phrase is built.

## Why 도와드릴까요 sounds so polite

The plain verb "to help" is **돕다** (*dopda*). "Help me" becomes **도와주세요** — the textbook pattern **(V)아/어 주세요**, "please do it for me." When the help goes the other way, from the speaker to you, Korean swaps 주다 ("give") for **드리다**, its humble form. That gives **도와드리다**, "to humbly help."

Then comes the ending **-ㄹ까요?**, the same one in **같이 갈까요?** ("Shall we go together?"). On its own it means "Shall I…?" Put it all together:

| Piece | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 돕다 → 도와 | help |
| + 드리다 | (humbly, for you) |
| + -ㄹ까요? | shall I…? |
| **도와드릴까요?** | Shall I help you? |
| **뭘** + 도와드릴까요? | What shall I help you with? → How can I help you? |

That is why the phrase sounds warm rather than stiff. The speaker lowers themselves a little and raises you. With a friend, it shrinks to **도와줄까?** (*dowajulkka?*), "Want me to help?"

## What staff actually say, place by place

Korean service language changes with the counter. Here is what to expect.

| Where | What you hear | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Clothing or cosmetics shop | **찾으시는 거 있으세요?** | Looking for something? |
| Hotel front desk | **무엇을 도와드릴까요?** | How may I help you? (very formal) |
| Information desk, station | **뭘 도와드릴까요?** | How can I help you? |
| Phone customer service | **네, 고객님, 무엇을 도와드릴까요?** | Yes, valued customer, how may I help? |
| Café counter | **주문하시겠어요?** | Ready to order? |
| Anywhere, as you enter | **어서 오세요 / 어서 오십시오** | Welcome |

**무엇을** is the full, written form of **뭘**. Hotels and call centers use it because it sounds more formal; in normal speech, 뭘 is what comes out. You do not need to answer at the same level — a plain **네** and a short sentence with 요 at the end is always polite enough.

## How to answer in three or four words

This is the half that matters. When someone asks you **찾으시는 거 있으세요?**, you have four easy replies.

**You are just browsing:**
**그냥 구경하는 거예요.** (*geunyang gugyeonghaneun geoyeyo*) — "I'm just looking."
Add **감사합니다** and the staff will usually leave you to browse.

**You want something specific** — use **(N) 있어요?**, "Do you have (N)?":
**이거 다른 색 있어요?** — "Do you have this in another color?"
**선크림 있어요?** — "Do you have sunscreen?"

**You need them to do something** — use **(V)아/어 주세요**:
**이거 보여 주세요.** — "Please show me this."
**좀 도와주세요.** — "Please help me a little."

**You do not need help right now:**
**괜찮아요, 필요하면 부를게요.** (*gwaenchanayo, piryohamyeon bureulgeyo*) — "I'm okay, I'll call you if I need something."

If the staff say **필요하신 거 있으면 말씀하세요**, there is nothing to answer except **네, 감사합니다**.

## A real conversation at a cosmetics shop

You walk into a cosmetics store in Myeongdong.

**Staff:** 어서 오세요! 찾으시는 거 있으세요?
*Welcome! Looking for anything?*

**You:** 네, 선크림 있어요?
*Yes, do you have sunscreen?*

**Staff:** 네, 이쪽이에요. 피부 타입이 어떻게 되세요?
*Yes, this way. What's your skin type?*

**You:** 음… 건성이에요. 하나 추천해 주세요.
*Um… dry. Please recommend one.*

**Staff:** 이거 많이 쓰세요. 테스트해 보시겠어요?
*A lot of people use this one. Would you like to test it?*

**You:** 네, 좋아요. 그리고 그냥 좀 더 구경할게요.
*Yes, great. And I'll just look around a bit more.*

**Staff:** 네, 필요하신 거 있으면 말씀하세요.
*Sure, let me know if you need anything.*

Three textbook patterns carried that whole exchange: **(N) 있어요?**, **(V)아/어 주세요**, and **-ㄹ게요** for "I'll…".

## Offering help yourself

The same phrase works when you are the helper. If someone is struggling with a ticket machine or a heavy suitcase on the subway stairs, a quick **도와드릴까요?** is perfect — it is polite enough for any stranger, any age. To a friend, say **도와줄까?** And when you are offering to help a host clear the table, **뭐 도와드릴까요?** — "Anything I can help with?" — is the natural line.

The common replies you will get back:

- **네, 감사합니다** — Yes, thank you.
- **괜찮아요** — I'm fine (no thanks).
- **아, 정말요? 고마워요** — Oh, really? Thanks.

## Mistakes that change the meaning

**Saying 도와주세요 when you mean to offer.** 도와주세요 means "Please help *me*." Said to someone carrying boxes, it sounds like you want them to carry yours too. Offering is 도와드릴까요.

**Answering 찾으시는 거 있으세요? with 아니요 alone.** A flat 아니요 sounds cold. Soften it: **아니요, 그냥 구경하는 거예요.**

**Using 도와드릴게요 as a question.** **-ㄹ게요** is a promise — "I'll help you" — and Koreans use it once the other person has said yes. Ask first with -ㄹ까요?, then follow up with 도와드릴게요.

**Mishearing 찾으시는.** It is fast, and to many ears it sounds like *chajeushinun*. If you catch "찾으시…" and "있으세요" at the end, it is the shop "Can I help you?" — no need to understand every syllable.

## Say it out loud

Practice these until they come out without thinking:

1. **그냥 구경하는 거예요, 감사합니다.** — the browsing answer.
2. **이거 다른 색 있어요?** — the "do you have" answer.
3. **도와드릴까요?** — offering help to a stranger.
