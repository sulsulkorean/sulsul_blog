Korean phrases at a shoe repair shop in Korea lean on Pattern 23 from the SULSUL workbook — verb stem plus **아/어 주세요** (*a/eo juseyo*), "please do this for me." Hand over your shoes and say **수선해 주세요** (*suseonhae juseyo*), "Please repair this," or **굽을 고쳐 주세요** (*gubeul gocheo juseyo*), "Please fix the heel." Pattern 03 — **있어요?** (*isseoyo?*) — covers pickup timing: **언제 되어요?** (*eonje doeeoyo?*), "When will it be ready?"

> - Repair these: **수선해 주세요** (*suseonhae juseyo*), "Please repair this."
> - Fix the heel: **굽을 고쳐 주세요** (*gubeul gocheo juseyo*), "Please fix the heel."
> - When ready: **언제 되어요?** (*eonje doeeoyo?*), "When will it be ready?"
> - Ask the price: **얼마예요?** (*eolmayeyo?*), "How much is it?"
> - Speak slowly: **천천히 말해 주세요** (*cheoncheonhi malhae juseyo*), "Please speak slowly."

## How do you drop off shoes at a Korean repair shop?

**수선집** (*suseonjip*, repair shop) counters sit near subway exits, university streets, and traditional markets. Many handle **신발** (*sinbal*, shoes), **가방** (*gabang*, bags), and **옷** (*ot*, clothes) in one small storefront. Start with Pattern 23 and point to the broken part.

You: **안녕하세요. 이 신발 굽이 떨어졌어요. 수선해 주세요.** — *annyeonghaseyo. i sinbal gubi tteoreojyeosseoyo. suseonhae juseyo.* — "Hello. The heel came off these shoes. Please repair them."  
Them: **내일 오후에 되요.** — *naeil ohu-e dwaeyo.* — "They will be ready tomorrow afternoon."  
You: **얼마예요?** — *eolmayeyo?* — "How much is it?"

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Repair shoes | 수선해 주세요 (*suseonhae juseyo*) |
| Fix the heel | 굽을 고쳐 주세요 (*gubeul gocheo juseyo*) |
| When ready | 언제 되어요? (*eonje doeeoyo?*) |
| Ask the price | 얼마예요? (*eolmayeyo?*) |
| Speak slowly | 천천히 말해 주세요 (*cheoncheonhi malhae juseyo*) |

## What do you say when you pick up your shoes?

Clerks often ask for your phone number or a receipt slip when you drop off. Keep the **영수증** (*yeongsujeung*, receipt) they hand back — you will need it at pickup.

You: **수선 맡긴 거 찾으러 왔어요.** — *suseon matgin geo chajeureo wasseoyo.* — "I came to pick up what I left for repair."  
Them: **영수증 있어요?** — *yeongsujeung isseoyo?* — "Do you have the receipt?"  
You: **네, 여기 있어요. 잘 됐어요?** — *ne, yeogi isseoyo. jal dwaesseoyo?* — "Yes, here it is. Did it turn out well?"

The **얼마예요?** line from [buying clothes in Korea](/buying-clothes-in-korea) works the same way at the counter when you want the total before you pay.

## What usually goes wrong at a Korean shoe repair shop?

Travelers often point at the damage but forget to say **언제 되어요?** (*eonje doeeoyo?*, when will it be ready?) — the shop defaults to two or three days and you miss your flight. Ask the pickup date every time you drop off.

A second mistake is assuming every **수선집** (*suseonjip*, repair shop) takes card payment. Many are cash-only. If the clerk shakes their head at your card, ask **현금만 돼요?** (*hyeongeumman dwaeyo?*), "Cash only?" before you walk to an ATM.
