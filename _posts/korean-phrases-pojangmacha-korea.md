A pojangmacha (*pojangmacha*) is a canvas-covered tent stall that appears on Seoul sidewalks and riverside paths after dark. You sit on plastic stools around a low table, share fishcake skewers or tteokbokki, pour soju into small cups, and stay until the owner starts folding up. The whole experience runs on about five phrases.

> - Flag staff with **여기요** (*yeogiyо*) — louder than a wave, politer than a shout.
> - Order **소주 한 병 주세요** (*soju han byeong juseyo*) for one bottle of soju.
> - Ask **이거 뭐예요?** (*igeo mwoyeyo?*) when a dish has no label.

## What do you order when you first sit down?

Most tent stalls post a short board in Korean only. Point at it and say the dish name plus **하나 주세요** (*hana juseyo*), "one, please." For drinks, name the bottle: **소주 한 병 주세요** (*soju han byeong juseyo*) or **맥주 한 캔 주세요** (*maekju han kaen juseyo*) for a can of beer.

| Order | Say this |
|---|---|
| One bottle of soju | 소주 한 병 주세요 (*soju han byeong juseyo*) |
| One can of beer | 맥주 한 캔 주세요 (*maekju han kaen juseyo*) |
| Tteokbokki | 떡볶이 하나 주세요 (*tteokbokki hana juseyo*) |
| Fishcake skewer | 어묵 하나 주세요 (*eomuk hana juseyo*) |

**하나 주세요** (*hana juseyo*) works for almost anything with a count of one — it is the polite "one, please" shortcut at any Korean food stall.

## How do you ask for more drinks or food?

When your cups run dry, raise your hand to catch the owner's eye and say **여기요**, then add what you need. A common second round sounds like this:

You: **소주 한 잔 더 주세요.** — *soju han jan deo juseyo.* — "One more shot of soju, please."  
Them: **네, 금방 갖다 드릴게요.** — *ne, geumbang gatda deurilgeyo.* — "Sure, I'll bring it right away."  
You: **감사합니다.** — *gamsahamnida.* — "Thank you."

**한 잔 더** (*han jan deo*) means "one more glass." Swap **잔** for **병** (*byeong*, bottle) if the table needs a full bottle instead of a single cup. For a side dish refill, say **이거 더 주세요** (*igeo deo juseyo*), "More of this, please," while pointing at the bowl.

## What do you say when you are ready to leave?

Signal the owner from your seat: **계산해 주세요** (*gyesanhae juseyo*), "Please bring the bill." Most pojangmacha owners come directly to your table — you do not need to walk to a register. After paying, say **잘 먹었어요** (*jal meogeosseoyo*), "That was a great meal," on your way out. Owners genuinely love hearing it.

Before you order the first round, ask **카드 돼요?** (*kadeu dwaeyo?*), "Do you take card?" Many stalls are cash-only, and knowing early saves the scramble at the end of the night.
