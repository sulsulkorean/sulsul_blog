A **포장마차** (*pojangmacha*, "tent cart") is a plastic-walled street stall wrapped in an orange or blue tarp, open until late, selling fishcake soup, tteokbokki, and soju at elbow-to-elbow tables. Nobody expects you to be fluent. They do expect you to order, eat, and settle the bill without too much confusion — and **자리 있어요?** (*jari isseoyo?*, "Is there a seat?") is the phrase that gets you started.

> - Ask for a seat: **자리 있어요?** (*jari isseoyo?*), "Is there a seat?"
> - Order one item: **이거 하나 주세요** (*igeo hana juseyo*), "One of this, please."
> - Order soju: **소주 한 병 주세요** (*soju han byeong juseyo*), "One bottle of soju, please."
> - Get the bill: **계산해 주세요** (*gyesan hae juseyo*), "Check, please."

## How do you sit down and order at a pojangmacha?

Walk up to the open side of the tent, catch the cook's eye, and say:

**자리 있어요?** — *jari isseoyo?* — "Is there a seat?"

If they wave you in, take a low stool or a plastic chair at the counter. Most pojangmacha do not have a written menu — the food is in the steaming trays and pots in front of you. Point at what looks good and say:

**이거 하나 주세요.** — *igeo hana juseyo.* — "One of this, please."

For two portions, swap **하나** (*hana*, one) for **둘** (*dul*, two): **이거 둘 주세요.** For a different drink, just name it:

**맥주 한 캔 주세요.** — *maekju han kaen juseyo.* — "One can of beer, please."

The cook typically brings everything at once and keeps a running tab.

## What do you say when the food arrives and the soju pours?

Korean drinking culture has a few phrases that are short, friendly, and expected at a pojangmacha table. Lead with the toast and the rest of the night falls into place:

**건배!** — *geonbae!* — "Cheers!"  
**맛있다!** — *masitda!* — "This is delicious!"  
**더 주세요.** — *deo juseyo.* — "More, please."  
**이거 뭐예요?** — *igeo mwoyeyo?* — "What is this?"

If a fellow diner offers to pour for you, let them — and pour back afterward. When your bottle runs low:

You: **소주 한 병 더 주세요.** — *soju han byeong deo juseyo.* — "One more bottle of soju, please."  
Them: **네, 잠깐만요.** — *ne, jamkkanmanyo.* — "Yes, one moment."  
You: **감사합니다!** — *gamsahamnida!* — "Thank you!"

## How do you find out the price and pay?

Pojangmacha are cash-heavy — many do not accept card. Ask before you order a second round:

**얼마예요?** — *eolmayeyo?* — "How much is it?"

When you are ready to leave, catch the cook's eye and say:

**계산해 주세요.** — *gyesan hae juseyo.* — "Check, please."

They will either tell you the total verbally or hold up fingers. If you did not quite catch the number, ask:

**다시 한 번만 말해 주세요.** — *dasi han beonman mal hae juseyo.* — "Could you say that one more time, please?"

| Item | Korean name |
|---|---|
| Fishcake skewer | 어묵 (*eomuk*) |
| Spicy rice cakes | 떡볶이 (*tteokbokki*) |
| Soju | 소주 (*soju*) |
| Beer | 맥주 (*maekju*) |
