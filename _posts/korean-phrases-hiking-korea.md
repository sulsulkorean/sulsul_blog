Korean phrases for hiking in Korea start with Pattern 04 from the SULSUL workbook — **어디예요?** (*eodiyeyo?*), "Where is…?" At a **국립공원** (*gungnipgongwon*, national park) entrance, ask **등산로 어디예요?** (*deungsanro eodiyeyo?*), "Where is the hiking trail?" Pattern 56 — verb plus **기로 했어요** (*giro haesseoyo*), "decided to" — is how locals plan a trip: **주말에 등산하기로 했어요** (*jumare deungsanhagirop haesseoyo*), "We decided to hike this weekend."

> - Find the trail: **등산로 어디예요?** (*deungsanro eodiyeyo?*), "Where is the hiking trail?"
> - Plan with friends: **주말에 등산하기로 했어요** (*jumare deungsanhagirop haesseoyo*), "We decided to hike this weekend."
> - Ask about difficulty: **여기 어려워요?** (*yeogi eoryeowoyo?*), "Is this part difficult?"
> - Need water: **물 있어요?** (*mul isseoyo?*), "Is there water?"
> - Check time left: **정상까지 얼마나 걸려요?** (*jeongsangkkaji eolmana geollyeoyo?*), "How long to the summit?"

## How do you start at the trailhead?

Park rangers and volunteer guides at popular peaks like **북한산** (*Bukhansan*) or **설악산** (*Seoraksan*) expect short Korean questions. Show your map and use Pattern 04 before you follow the wrong fork.

You: **안녕하세요. 등산로 어디예요?** — *annyeonghaseyo. deungsanro eodiyeyo?* — "Hello. Where is the hiking trail?"  
Them: **저쪽 표지판 따라가세요.** — *jeojjok pyojipan ttaragaseyo.* — "Follow the sign over there."  
You: **정상까지 얼마나 걸려요?** — *jeongsangkkaji eolmana geollyeoyo?* — "How long to the summit?"

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Where is the trail? | 등산로 어디예요? (*deungsanro eodiyeyo?*) |
| Plan a weekend hike | 주말에 등산하기로 했어요 (*jumare deungsanhagirop haesseoyo*) |
| Is this section hard? | 여기 어려워요? (*yeogi eoryeowoyo?*) |
| Water available? | 물 있어요? (*mul isseoyo?*) |
| Time to summit | 정상까지 얼마나 걸려요? (*jeongsangkkaji eolmana geollyeoyo?*) |

## What do you say when you meet other hikers on the path?

Trail culture in Korea is friendly — a quick **안녕하세요** (*annyeonghaseyo*) and **수고하세요** (*sugohaseyo*), "Keep up the good work," go a long way on steep stairs.

You: **정상까지 얼마나 남았어요?** — *jeongsangkkaji eolmana namasseoyo?* — "How much is left to the summit?"  
Them: **30분쯤이에요.** — *samsip bun jjeum-ieyo.* — "About thirty minutes."  
You: **감사합니다. 수고하세요.** — *gamsahamnida. sugohaseyo.* — "Thank you. Keep it up."

For asking directions when you are still in the city before the mountain, **어디 있어요?** (*eodi isseoyo?*) from [asking for directions on the Korean subway](/ask-directions-korean-subway) works the same way at a bus stop bound for the trailhead.

## What usually goes wrong on a Korean hiking trail?

Travelers often skip the **등산로** (*deungsanro*, hiking trail) sign and follow a paved road meant for service vehicles — if the path looks like a driveway, double-check with **여기 등산로 맞아요?** (*yeogi deungsanro majayo?*), "Is this the hiking trail?"

A second mistake is starting too late in summer. Ask at the booth **몇 시까지 올라갈 수 있어요?** (*myeot sikkaji ollagal su isseoyo?*), "Until what time can we go up?" so you are not turned back near sunset.
