To ask for a to-go box in Korean, say **남은 거 포장해 주세요** (*na-meun geo po-jang-hae ju-se-yo*): "Please pack the leftovers." There is no separate word for a "to-go box" or "doggy bag." Koreans use **포장** (*po-jang*, packing / to-go) for everything from takeout orders to the half plate of chicken you couldn't finish. If you're not sure the restaurant does it, ask first with **남은 거 포장돼요?** (*na-meun geo po-jang-dwae-yo?*), "Can I take the leftovers?" Most casual restaurants will say yes and either pack it for you or hand you a container.

> - Please pack the leftovers: **남은 거 포장해 주세요** — *na-meun geo po-jang-hae ju-se-yo*
> - Can I take the leftovers to go?: **남은 거 포장돼요?** — *na-meun geo po-jang-dwae-yo?*
> - Please wrap this up (homey): **이거 싸 주세요** — *i-geo ssa ju-se-yo*
> - A container, please: **포장 용기 주세요** — *po-jang yong-gi ju-se-yo*
> - A bag, please: **봉투 주세요** — *bong-tu ju-se-yo*
> - Separately, please: **따로 담아 주세요** — *tta-ro da-ma ju-se-yo*

## What 포장 (pojang) actually means

포장 is a noun that means "packing" or "wrapping." Gift wrapping at a department store is 포장. So is a takeout order at a gimbap shop, and so is the box for your leftover tteokbokki. When you hear "pojang" in a Korean restaurant, it simply means "this is leaving the building."

To turn it into a request, you use the pattern from the SULSUL workbook for asking someone to *do* something: verb stem plus **아/어 주세요**. 포장하다 (to pack) becomes **포장해 주세요**, "please pack it." Add what you want packed in front: **이거** (this), **남은 거** (what's left), **치킨** (the chicken). For an extra-soft version, put 좀 in the middle: **이거 좀 포장해 주세요**. Want to sound a bit more careful? Say **포장해 주시겠어요?**, "Could you pack it for me?"

The word 남은 comes from 남다, "to be left over," so 남은 거 is literally "the thing that is left." It's the most useful two-word phrase for this situation, and you can point at the plate as you say it.

## Asking first: 남은 거 포장돼요?

If you don't know whether a restaurant packs leftovers, ask before you assume. **포장돼요?** (*po-jang-dwae-yo?*) means "Is packing possible?" It's polite and neutral, and staff hear it all day.

You can also ask with **싸 갈 수 있어요?** (*ssa gal su i-sseo-yo?*), "Can I wrap it up and take it?" Both work. The first sounds a little more like a customer at a counter, and the second a little more like someone at a family meal.

If the answer is **네, 돼요** (*ne, dwae-yo*), "Yes, you can," you're set. If it's **포장은 안 돼요** (*po-jang-eun an dwae-yo*), "We don't do to-go," just say **아, 네** and leave it there.

## 싸 주세요: the homey way to say "wrap it up"

You'll also hear **싸 주세요** (*ssa ju-se-yo*). It comes from 싸다, "to wrap," the same verb in 쌈 (ssam, food wrapped in lettuce) and 도시락 싸다 (to pack a lunchbox). It feels warmer and more everyday than 포장해 주세요, the kind of thing you'd say to the 이모 at a neighborhood restaurant.

Both are fine. If you only learn one, learn 포장해 주세요. It works in every kind of place, from a chain restaurant to a market stall.

## What the server might say back

The request is short. The questions that come back are where people freeze. These are the common ones:

- **포장 용기 갖다 드릴게요.** (*po-jang yong-gi gat-da deu-ril-ge-yo*) — "I'll bring you a container." In many places the server brings a box and you pack it yourself at the table.
- **직접 담으시면 돼요.** (*jik-jeop da-meu-si-myeon dwae-yo*) — "You can pack it yourself." Same idea. Look for a sign that says **셀프 포장** (*sel-peu po-jang*), self-packing.
- **국물도 담아 드릴까요?** (*gung-mul-do da-ma deu-ril-kka-yo?*) — "Should I pack the soup too?" Answer **네, 같이 담아 주세요** for yes, or **아니요, 건더기만요** (*geon-deo-gi-man-yo*, "just the solid bits") if you don't want a leaky bag.
- **봉투 필요하세요?** (*bong-tu pi-ryo-ha-se-yo?*) — "Do you need a bag?" Bags often cost a little extra in Korea, so staff ask instead of just handing one over. **네, 주세요** or **괜찮아요** (no thanks).
- **반찬은 포장이 안 돼요.** (*ban-chan-eun po-jang-i an dwae-yo*) — "Side dishes can't be packed." More on that below.

## Which foods usually go home, and which don't

Leftovers are normal in Korea, especially with shared dishes. Fried chicken, pizza, jokbal, braised dishes like 찜 (*jjim*), and stews are all commonly packed. Uncooked meat left over at a barbecue place can often go home too. Ask **고기 남은 거 포장돼요?** before it hits the grill.

A few things usually stay behind:

- **Free side dishes (반찬).** Refills are free because they stay in the restaurant. Many places won't pack them, and asking for a box of kimchi to take home can feel a bit cheeky.
- **Buffets and all-you-can-eat places (무한리필, *mu-han-ri-pil*).** Taking food out is generally not allowed. Some places even charge if you leave a lot on the plate.
- **Food that won't travel.** Cold noodles (냉면) go soggy, and some restaurants politely refuse to pack them for that reason.

At a takeout-friendly place, it helps to ask early. Say **반은 포장해 주세요** (*ba-neun po-jang-hae ju-se-yo*), "Please pack half of it," when you order something huge.

## A short scene at the end of a meal

> **You:** 저기요! 남은 거 포장돼요?
> *jeo-gi-yo! na-meun geo po-jang-dwae-yo?* — Excuse me! Can I take the leftovers?
>
> **Server:** 네, 돼요. 용기 갖다 드릴게요.
> *ne, dwae-yo. yong-gi gat-da deu-ril-ge-yo.* — Yes, you can. I'll bring a container.
>
> **Server:** 국물도 담아 드릴까요?
> *gung-mul-do da-ma deu-ril-kka-yo?* — Should I pack the soup too?
>
> **You:** 아니요, 건더기만요. 그리고 봉투도 주세요.
> *a-ni-yo, geon-deo-gi-man-yo. geu-ri-go bong-tu-do ju-se-yo.* — No, just the solids. And a bag too, please.
>
> **Server:** 네, 여기 있어요.
> *ne, yeo-gi i-sseo-yo.* — Sure, here you go.
>
> **You:** 감사합니다. 잘 먹었습니다!
> *gam-sa-ham-ni-da. jal meo-geo-sseum-ni-da!* — Thank you. That was delicious!

## Slips that make it awkward

- **Saying "doggy bag."** Most staff won't know it. Even "take out" can be heard as 테이크아웃, which in Korea usually means coffee. 포장 is the word.
- **Packing the shared banchan into your box.** If the server didn't offer, don't. Pack only the dish you ordered.
- **Dropping the 요.** **포장해** on its own is casual and sounds like an order. Keep **주세요**.
- **Waiting until the plate is cleared.** Staff move fast in busy places. Call 저기요 while the food is still on the table.

## Say it before your next big meal

The whole thing comes down to three lines. Say them out loud now, a few times each: **남은 거 포장돼요?** — **포장 용기 주세요.** — **봉투도 주세요.** Then try swapping the first word: 치킨 포장해 주세요. 이거 반만 포장해 주세요. 이거 싸 주세요. Once 포장해 주세요 comes out without thinking, it works for takeout orders, leftovers, and even gift wrapping at a shop.
