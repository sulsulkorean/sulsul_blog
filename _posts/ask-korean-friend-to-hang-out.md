To ask a Korean friend to hang out in Korean, add **같이** (*gachi*, together) before the verb and finish with **ㄹ래요?** (*llaeyo?*) or **ㄹ까요?** (*lkkayo?*). **주말에 같이 영화 볼래요?** (*jumare gachi yeonghwa bollaeyo?*) means "Do you want to watch a movie together this weekend?" Pattern 25 in the SULSUL workbook covers this warm invitation without sounding pushy.

> - Suggest watching together: **같이 볼래요?** (*gachi bollaeyo?*), "Do you want to watch it together?"
> - Suggest eating together: **같이 먹을까요?** (*gachi meogeulkkayo?*), "Shall we eat together?"
> - Offer coffee after class: **커피 한잔할래요?** (*keopi hanjanhallaeyo?*), "Do you want to grab a cup of coffee?"
> - Set the day first: **토요일에 같이 갈래요?** (*toyoire gachi gallaeyo?*), "Do you want to go together on Saturday?"

## How do you build a hang-out invitation in Korean?

The reusable frame is time or place + **같이** + verb stem + **ㄹ래요?** or **ㄹ까요?**. **ㄹ래요?** (*llaeyo?*) asks what the other person wants — good between peers. **ㄹ까요?** (*lkkayo?*) asks for a joint decision and feels slightly softer at work or with someone you just met.

If the verb stem ends in a consonant, use **을래요?** or **을까요?** instead: **같이 먹을래요?** (*gachi meogeullaeyo?*), "Do you want to eat together?"

## What does a real invitation exchange sound like?

You: **수업 끝나고 같이 커피 한잔할래요?** — *su-eop kkeutnago gachi keopi hanjanhallaeyo?* — "Do you want to grab coffee together after class?"  
Them: **좋아요! 몇 시에요?** — *joayo! myeot sieyo?* — "Sounds good! What time?"  
You: **세 시쯤 어때요?** — *se sijjeum eottaeyo?* — "How about around three?"

If they are busy, you will hear **미안해요, 그때 시간 없어요** (*mianhaeyo, geuttae sigan eopseoyo*), "Sorry, I am not free then." Reply with **그럼 다음에요** (*geureom daeumeyo*), "Then next time," and the door stays open.

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Watch a movie | 주말에 같이 영화 볼래요? (*jumare gachi yeonghwa bollaeyo?*) |
| Eat lunch | 오늘 점심 같이 먹을까요? (*oneul jeomsim gachi meogeulkkayo?*) |
| Grab coffee | 커피 한잔할래요? (*keopi hanjanhallaeyo?*) |
| Go somewhere | 내일 같이 갈래요? (*naeil gachi gallaeyo?*) |
| Pick a place | 어디에서 만날까요? (*eodiseo mannalkkayo?*) |

## What usually goes wrong?

The most common mistake is skipping **같이** and sounding like a solo plan. **영화 볼래요?** (*yeonghwa bollaeyo?*) can read as "Do you want to watch a movie?" without implying you go together. Add **같이** every time until it is automatic.

A second slip is using **ㄹ래요?** with a boss or a professor when **ㄹ까요?** fits better. Both are polite **해요체** (*haeyoche*); **ㄹ까요?** just leaves more room for the other person to say no gracefully. For lines after you already exchanged numbers, see [Korean phrases for KakaoTalk texting](/korean-phrases-kakaotalk-texting). If lunch is the plan, **같이 먹을까요?** (*gachi meogeulkkayo?*) is the line to practice first.
