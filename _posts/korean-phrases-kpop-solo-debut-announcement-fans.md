The notice goes up on a Tuesday with no warning, and the thread splits in half within a minute — one side celebrating, the other side quietly counting how long the group has left. **솔로 데뷔** (*sollo debwi*) is the phrase on the poster, and **팀 활동** (*tim hwaldong*), group activity, is the phrase everyone is actually scanning for. **솔로 데뷔한대요** (*sollo debwihandaeyo*), "I heard they're debuting solo," is how the news travels, and **팀 활동도 계속한대요** (*tim hwaldongdo gyesokhandaeyo*), "They're keeping up group activities too," is the sentence that settles the room.

> - Share the news: **솔로 데뷔한대요** (*sollo debwihandaeyo*), "I heard they're debuting solo."
> - Point at the early track: **선공개곡 나왔어요** (*seon-gonggaegok nawasseoyo*), "The pre-release song is out."
> - Credit the writing: **자작곡이래요** (*jajakgogiraeyo*), "I heard they wrote it themselves."
> - Calm the thread: **팀 활동도 계속한대요** (*tim hwaldongdo gyesokhandaeyo*), "They're continuing group activities too."
> - Ask for the date: **첫 방송 언제예요?** (*cheot bangsong eonjeyeyo?*), "When's the first broadcast stage?"

## How do you join the thread the hour it goes up?

The conversation runs in a fixed order, and it is the same order every time — news, then the music, then the group.

1. **솔로 데뷔한대요** with the poster attached, because half the timeline sees the photo before anyone tells them what it is for.
2. **선공개곡 나왔어요** once a track lands ahead of the album, since that is usually the first thing anyone can actually listen to.
3. **자작곡이래요** if the credits have their name on them — in Korean fan threads this is the compliment that carries the most weight.
4. **팀 활동도 계속한대요** the moment someone starts worrying out loud, which in these threads takes about four replies.
5. **첫 방송 언제예요?** once the panic has passed and people move on to planning what to watch.

| Moment | Say this |
|---|---|
| Sharing the announcement | 솔로 데뷔한대요 (*sollo debwihandaeyo*) |
| A track drops early | 선공개곡 나왔어요 (*seon-gonggaegok nawasseoyo*) |
| They wrote it | 자작곡이래요 (*jajakgogiraeyo*) |
| Someone panics about the group | 팀 활동도 계속한대요 (*tim hwaldongdo gyesokhandaeyo*) |
| Asking about the first stage | 첫 방송 언제예요? (*cheot bangsong eonjeyeyo?*) |

## What do Korean speakers say back?

Replies come in fragments, half of them questions, and nobody finishes a sentence before the next photo lands.

You: **솔로 데뷔한대요** — *sollo debwihandaeyo* — "I heard they're debuting solo."  
Them: **진짜요? 언제요?** — *jinjjayo? eonjeyo?* — "Really? When?"  
You: **선공개곡 나왔어요** — *seon-gonggaegok nawasseoyo* — "The pre-release song is out."  
Them: **저 벌써 들었어요. 곡도 직접 썼대요?** — *jeo beolsseo deureosseoyo. gokdo jikjeop sseotdaeyo?* — "I already listened. Did they write the song themselves too?"  
You: **자작곡이래요** — *jajakgogiraeyo* — "I heard they wrote it themselves."  
Them: **그럼 팀은요?** — *geureom timeunyo?* — "Then what about the group?"  
You: **팀 활동도 계속한대요** — *tim hwaldongdo gyesokhandaeyo* — "They're continuing group activities too."

## What usually goes wrong in a solo debut thread?

The first slip is the word **탈퇴** (*talttoe*). It means leaving the group for good, and using it about a solo release reads as a rumour rather than a question — Korean fans will correct it fast. The neutral way to ask is **팀 활동은요?** (*tim hwaldongeunyo?*), "And group activities?" which asks the same thing without accusing anyone of anything.

The second is calling every solo release a **솔로 데뷔**. A track sung alone on a group album is a **솔로곡** (*sollogok*); a debut is a release put out under their own name, with its own promotion cycle. The related word worth keeping is **완전체** (*wanjeonche*), the full group together, which is what fans start asking about the moment solo schedules stack up.
