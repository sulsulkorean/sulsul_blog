Around a bias birthday, Seoul stations fill up with fan-funded ads, and the trip to see one in person is half the point. **광고 어디에 있어요?** (*gwanggo eodie isseoyo?*), "Where is the ad?" is how you stop circling a station with three levels, and **사진 좀 찍어 주실래요?** (*sajin jom jjigeo jusillaeyo?*), "Could you take a photo for me?" is how you go home with the one shot you came for.

> - Find it: **광고 어디에 있어요?** (*gwanggo eodie isseoyo?*), "Where is the ad?"
> - Narrow it down: **몇 번 출구예요?** (*myeot beon chulguyeyo?*), "Which exit is it near?"
> - Ask for the photo: **사진 좀 찍어 주실래요?** (*sajin jom jjigeo jusillaeyo?*), "Could you take a photo for me?"
> - Offer one back: **저도 찍어 드릴게요** (*jeodo jjigeo deurilgeyo*), "I'll take one for you too."
> - Check the run: **언제까지 걸려 있어요?** (*eonjekkaji geollyeo isseoyo?*), "How long is it up?"

## How do you actually find the ad inside the station?

Big transfer stations have several floors and a dozen ad walls, and the photo in the fan post never shows which one.

1. **광고 어디에 있어요?** to station staff — say the group name and they will usually know the one, because fans have been asking since morning.
2. **몇 번 출구예요?** turns a vague direction into a number you can follow on the signs.
3. **사진 좀 찍어 주실래요?** to another fan standing there, not to a passing commuter.
4. **저도 찍어 드릴게요** right after, which is why nobody at these walls ever says no.
5. **언제까지 걸려 있어요?** if a friend is arriving later in the week.

## What do Korean speakers say back?

Station staff answer with an exit number, and fans answer by already reaching for your phone.

You: **광고 어디에 있어요?** — *gwanggo eodie isseoyo?* — "Where is the ad?"  
Them: **3번 출구 쪽에 있어요. 계단 내려가면 바로 보여요** — *sambeon chulgu jjoge isseoyo. gyedan naeryeogamyeon baro boyeoyo* — "It's over by exit 3. You'll see it right after the stairs."  
You: **감사합니다** — *gamsahamnida* — "Thank you."

Once you are there, **사진 좀 찍어 주실래요?** to the person with the same lightstick keyring as yours works every time. For the other stop on the same birthday route, see [Korean phrases at an idol birthday cafe in Seoul](/korean-phrases-idol-birthday-cafe-seoul).

## What usually goes wrong on an ad run?

The most common one is asking for the ad by the idol's name only. Staff sort the station by exits and ad walls, not by fandoms, so **광고 어디에 있어요?** followed by **몇 번 출구예요?** gets you a real answer in one exchange.

The other is timing. These ads come down on a fixed date, and a trip planned around an old fan post lands at a blank wall — **언제까지 걸려 있어요?** asked on the spot saves the friend flying in on Saturday.
