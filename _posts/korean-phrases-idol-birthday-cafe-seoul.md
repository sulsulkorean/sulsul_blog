**Birthday cafes** for K-pop idols pop up across Seoul every summer weekend in 2026 — decorated corners, free photocards, and a line out the door before noon. **생일 축하해요** (*saengil chukahaeyo*), "Happy birthday," is what fans write on the message wall, and **포토카드 있어요?** (*photokadeu isseoyo?*), "Do you have photocards?" is the first question at the counter when the tray display runs low.

> - Wish the idol: **생일 축하해요** (*saengil chukahaeyo*), "Happy birthday."
> - Ask about freebies: **포토카드 있어요?** (*photokadeu isseoyo?*), "Do you have photocards?"
> - Order a drink: **아이스 아메리카노 한 잔 주세요** (*aiseu amerikano han jan juseyo*), "One iced Americano, please."
> - Ask about a photo spot: **여기서 사진 찍어도 돼요?** (*yeogiseo sajin jjigeodo dwaeyo?*), "May I take a photo here?"
> - Thank the staff: **감사합니다** (*gamsahamnida*), "Thank you."

## How do you order and join the fan line at a birthday cafe?

Birthday cafe crowds mix English fandom slang with short Korean at the counter — a few clear lines keep you moving when the photocard tray is almost empty.

1. Check the freebie table and ask **포토카드 있어요?** before you sit down.
2. Order with **아이스 아메리카노 한 잔 주세요** if the menu is all in Korean.
3. Before setting up a shot, ask **여기서 사진 찍어도 돼요?** — staff appreciate the ask.
4. Write **생일 축하해요** on the message board even if your handwriting wobbles.
5. Hand the cup back with **감사합니다** when you leave.

| Moment | Say this |
|---|---|
| Message wall | 생일 축하해요 (*saengil chukahaeyo*) |
| Free photocards | 포토카드 있어요? (*photokadeu isseoyo?*) |
| At the counter | 아이스 아메리카노 한 잔 주세요 (*aiseu amerikano han jan juseyo*) |
| Photo corner | 여기서 사진 찍어도 돼요? (*yeogiseo sajin jjigeodo dwaeyo?*) |
| Leaving | 감사합니다 (*gamsahamnida*) |

## What does cafe staff or another fan say back?

Replies stay quick because the next group is already waiting at the door.

You: **포토카드 있어요?** — *photokadeu isseoyo?* — "Do you have photocards?"  
Them: **네, 아직 있어요.** — *ne, ajik isseoyo.* — "Yes, we still have some."  
You: **감사합니다.** — *gamsahamnida.*

If someone moves so you can photograph the wall, **고마워요** (*gomawoyo*) works with peers; **감사합니다** is safer with staff behind the counter.
