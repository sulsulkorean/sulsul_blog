Most of the Korean ordering phrases you need today are ones you **read**, because in a lot of burger shops, cafes and noodle places the first thing you meet is a touchscreen. Five words get you through almost any of them: **매장** (*maejang*), "eat in"; **포장** (*pojang*), "to go"; **담기** (*damgi*), "add to cart"; **주문하기** (*jumunhagi*), "place order"; and **결제하기** (*gyeoljehagi*), "pay." When the machine won't cooperate, one spoken line moves you to a human: **여기서 주문해도 돼요?** (*yeogiseo jumunhaedo dwaeyo?*), "Can I order here?"

> - Eat in: **매장** (*maejang*) or **먹고 가기** (*meokgo gagi*)
> - To go: **포장** (*pojang*)
> - Next / back: **다음** (*daeum*) / **이전** (*ijeon*)
> - Sold out: **품절** (*pumjeol*)
> - Pay: **결제하기** (*gyeoljehagi*)
> - Help: **키오스크 좀 도와주세요.** (*kioseukeu jom dowajuseyo*) — "Please help me with the kiosk."

## The screen, in the order it appears

Kiosks differ in design, but the sequence hardly changes. Look first for a small **English** or **언어** (*eoneo*, "language") button, usually in a corner of the start screen. If there isn't one, this is the path:

| Step | What you see | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 화면을 터치해 주세요 (*hwamyeoneul teochihae juseyo*) | Please touch the screen |
| 2 | 매장 / 포장 | Eat in / to go |
| 3 | 세트 · 단품 · 음료 · 사이드 | Set meal · single item · drinks · sides |
| 4 | 옵션 선택 (*opsyeon seontaek*) | Choose options |
| 5 | 담기 · 장바구니 (*jangbaguni*) | Add · cart |
| 6 | 주문하기 · 결제하기 | Place order · pay |
| 7 | 카드를 넣어 주세요 (*kadeureul neoeo juseyo*) | Please insert your card |
| 8 | 주문번호 (*jumun beonho*) | Your order number |

Two words in step 4 stop people. **필수** (*pilsu*) means "required": the button to continue stays gray until you pick something. **선택** (*seontaek*) next to an option group means "optional," so you can skip it.

Watch the clock too. Leave the screen alone and you may get **주문을 계속하시겠습니까?** (*jumuneul gyesokhasigesseumnikka?*), "Would you like to continue your order?" Tap **예** (*ye*, "yes") or **계속** (*gyesok*, "continue"), or your cart empties.

## Options you will want: spice, ice and size

Food kiosks usually ask about heat. Cafe kiosks ask about temperature and ice. The words repeat everywhere:

- **순한맛** (*sunhanmat*) — mild. **매운맛** (*maeunmat*) — spicy. **보통맛** (*botongmat*) — regular.
- **곱빼기** (*gopppaegi*) — an extra-large portion, mostly noodles.
- **추가** (*chuga*) — add. **치즈 추가** is "add cheese"; **샷 추가** is "add a shot."
- **빼기** (*ppaegi*) — leave out. **양파 빼기** (*yangpa ppaegi*) is "no onion."
- **얼음 적게** (*eoreum jeokge*) — less ice. **덜 달게** (*deol dalge*) — less sweet.
- **수량** (*suryang*) — quantity, next to the plus and minus buttons.

If the option you need isn't on the screen, finish ordering and ask the counter. Pattern 23 in the SULSUL workbook, (V)아/어 주세요, does that job: **양파 빼 주세요.** (*yangpa ppae juseyo*), "Please leave out the onion." For a real allergy, skip the kiosk and tell a person from the start, using the lines in [food allergies and diet restrictions](/korean-phrases-food-allergies-korea).

## The payment page is where most people get stuck

You found the food, and now the screen asks three more questions before it takes a card.

1. **포인트 적립하시겠습니까?** (*pointeu jeongniphasigesseumnikka?*) — "Would you like to collect points?" It wants a Korean phone number. Tap **건너뛰기** (*geonneottwigi*, "skip"), **적립 안 함** (*jeongnip an ham*, "don't collect") or **아니요** (*aniyo*, "no").
2. **결제 수단** (*gyeolje sudan*) — "Payment method." **신용카드** (*sinyongkadeu*) is credit card. **쿠폰** or **기프티콘** are vouchers you won't have. **간편결제** (*ganpyeon gyeolje*) means phone payment apps.
3. **영수증 출력** (*yeongsujeung chullyeok*) — "Print receipt." Choose **예** if the order number is printed on it, which is common.

Then wait for **카드를 빼 주세요** (*kadeureul ppae juseyo*), "Please remove your card." Pull it early and the payment can fail. Some kiosks also reject overseas cards that work fine at the register, and many take no cash, so a failed payment doesn't mean your card is broken. Walk to the counter.

## What to say when the kiosk wins

Nobody minds. Plenty of Koreans get stuck on these screens too. Start with **저기요** (*jeogiyo*), "excuse me," then pick one:

- **키오스크 잘 모르겠어요.** (*kioseukeu jal moreugesseoyo*) — "I can't figure out the kiosk." (Pattern 21)
- **여기서 주문해도 돼요?** (*yeogiseo jumunhaedo dwaeyo?*) — "Can I order here?" (Pattern 44)
- **카드가 안 돼요.** (*kadeuga an dwaeyo*) — "My card isn't working."
- **현금 돼요?** (*hyeongeum dwaeyo?*) — "Can I pay in cash?"
- **영어 메뉴 있어요?** (*yeongeo menyu isseoyo?*) — "Is there an English menu?" (Pattern 03)
- **잘못 눌렀어요.** (*jalmot nulleosseoyo*) — "I pressed the wrong thing." (Pattern 08)
- **취소해 주세요.** (*chwisohae juseyo*) — "Please cancel it."

If someone is waiting behind you and you need a moment, step aside and say **먼저 하세요.** (*meonjeo haseyo*), "Go ahead."

## At the counter instead: a short exchange

> **You:** 저기요, 카드가 안 돼요. 여기서 주문해도 돼요?
> *jeogiyo, kadeuga an dwaeyo. yeogiseo jumunhaedo dwaeyo?* — Excuse me, my card isn't working. Can I order here?
>
> **Staff:** 네, 말씀하세요.
> *ne, malsseumhaseyo.* — Yes, go ahead.
>
> **You:** 불고기버거 세트 하나 주세요.
> *bulgogibeogeo seteu hana juseyo.* — One bulgogi burger set, please.
>
> **Staff:** 드시고 가세요?
> *deusigo gaseyo?* — Is that for here?
>
> **You:** 아니요, 포장이요.
> *aniyo, pojang-iyo.* — No, to go.
>
> **Staff:** 네, 번호 부르면 오세요.
> *ne, beonho bureumyeon oseyo.* — Okay, come when we call your number.

The order of words in the third line is the one to copy: item, then number, then **주세요**. It's Pattern 01, (N) 주세요, and it works in every shop in the country.

## Hearing your number called

A kiosk order ends with a slip and a number, and then somebody shouts it. Order numbers use the Sino-Korean set (일, 이, 삼), not 하나, 둘, 셋. So 42 is **사십이 번** (*sasibi beon*), and you'll hear:

- **42번 고객님!** (*sasibi beon gogaengnim!*) — "Customer number 42!"
- **주문하신 메뉴 나왔습니다.** (*jumunhasin menyu nawatseumnida*) — "Your order is ready."

Before you sit down, say your number out loud twice, so it sounds familiar when it comes. If you miss it, show the slip and ask **이거 나왔어요?** (*igeo nawasseoyo?*), "Is this one ready?" Some places hand you a buzzer, **진동벨** (*jindongbel*), instead. The full number set is in [Korean numbers for paying and counting](/korean-numbers-paying-counting-korea).

## Slips that cost you a second try

- **Tapping 처음으로 to go back one page.** **처음으로** (*cheoeumeuro*) means "to the beginning" and may wipe your cart. **이전** goes back one step.
- **Reading 단품 as a deal.** **단품** (*danpum*) is the item alone. If you want fries and a drink, tap **세트** (*seteu*).
- **Waiting at the table.** At most kiosk places food is picked up at **픽업대** (*pigeopdae*) or **음식 나오는 곳** (*eumsik naoneun got*), and trays go back to **퇴식구** (*toesikgu*).
- **Asking staff "take out?"** People will follow, but the word on the screen and at the till is **포장**.

Tablets bolted to restaurant tables use the same vocabulary, plus one gift: **직원 호출** (*jigwon hochul*), "call staff."

## Say the rescue line before you're standing in line

Reading the buttons is the easy half. The hard half is turning to a busy cashier and getting **여기서 주문해도 돼요?** out in one breath. Say it five times now, then follow it with a full order: item, number, **주세요**, and **포장이요** or **먹고 갈게요** (*meokgo galgeyo*), "I'll eat here."
