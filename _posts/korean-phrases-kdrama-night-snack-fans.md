A shared K-drama binge rarely ends on schedule — someone gets hungry around the cliffhanger, and the group chat drifts into Korean while you decide what to order. **야식 시킬까요?** (*yasik sikilkkayo?*), "Should we order a late-night snack?" is the line that starts the food debate, and **다음 화 바로 볼까요?** (*daeum hwa baro bolkkayo?*), "Should we watch the next episode right away?" is what you send before anyone drifts off to sleep.

> - Pick the show: **오늘 뭐 볼까요?** (*oneul mwo bolkkayo?*), "What should we watch today?"
> - Order food: **야식 시킬까요?** (*yasik sikilkkayo?*), "Should we order a late-night snack?"
> - React to a scene: **이 장면 진짜 좋아요** (*i jangmyeon jinjja joayo*), "This scene is really good."
> - Keep going: **다음 화 바로 볼까요?** (*daeum hwa baro bolkkayo?*), "Should we watch the next episode now?"
> - Protect the ending: **스포 하지 마세요** (*seupo haji maseyo*), "No spoilers, please."

## How do you run a K-drama snack night without stalling the group?

A binge night lives on quick decisions — what to watch, what to eat, whether to push through one more episode. Short Korean lines keep the plan moving instead of stuck in a wall of "?" reactions.

1. Open with **오늘 뭐 볼까요?** so the show gets picked before the mood cools off.
2. When the first cliffhanger hits, send **야식 시킬까요?** to start the food order.
3. During a big moment, drop **이 장면 진짜 좋아요** to keep the room reacting together.
4. Before anyone logs off, ask **다음 화 바로 볼까요?** to lock in one more episode.
5. If someone read ahead, warn them with **스포 하지 마세요** to save the ending.

| Moment | Say this |
|---|---|
| Choose the show | 오늘 뭐 볼까요? (*oneul mwo bolkkayo?*) |
| Order food | 야식 시킬까요? (*yasik sikilkkayo?*) |
| React to a scene | 이 장면 진짜 좋아요 (*i jangmyeon jinjja joayo*) |
| Next episode | 다음 화 바로 볼까요? (*daeum hwa baro bolkkayo?*) |
| Block spoilers | 스포 하지 마세요 (*seupo haji maseyo*) |

## What do Korean speakers say back?

Replies on a snack night stay casual and quick — the group is half-watching, half-typing.

You: **야식 시킬까요?** — *yasik sikilkkayo?* — "Should we order a late-night snack?"  
Them: **좋아요, 뭐 먹을까요?** — *joayo, mwo meogeulkkayo?* — "Sounds good, what should we eat?"  
You: **다음 화 바로 볼까요?** — *daeum hwa baro bolkkayo?*

When the episode ends on a twist, **스포 하지 마세요** keeps the friend who binged ahead from ruining it. For ordering the actual food once the group decides, see [Korean phrases for food delivery in Korea](/korean-phrases-food-delivery-korea).

## What usually goes wrong on a binge night?

Learners follow the whole episode fine but freeze on the tiny logistics chat — what to order, whether to keep watching — which is exactly where friends switch to Korean.

A second mistake is answering every plan with an emoji instead of a short line, so you end up watching the decisions happen instead of making them.
