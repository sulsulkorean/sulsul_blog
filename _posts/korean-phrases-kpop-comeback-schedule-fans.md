August 2026 comeback season means **K-pop schedule posters** land in group chats before the shop page even loads — and the Korean fans type still works even if you read the announcement in English. **컴백 일정 나왔어요?** (*keombaek iljeong nawasseoyo?*), "Did the comeback schedule come out?" opens the thread when someone posts the first image, and **언제예요?** (*eonjeyeyo?*), "When is it?" is what you send when the teaser date is clear but the full timeline is still scrolling.

> - Ask about the drop: **컴백 일정 나왔어요?** (*keombaek iljeong nawasseoyo?*), "Did the comeback schedule come out?"
> - Pin down a date: **언제예요?** (*eonjeyeyo?*), "When is it?"
> - Show excitement: **기대돼요** (*gidaedwaeyo*), "I am looking forward to it."
> - Save the date: **달력에 적어 둘게요** (*dallyeoge jeogeo dulgeyo*), "I will mark it on my calendar."
> - Plan together: **같이 가요** (*gati gayo*), "Let us go together."

## How do you react when a comeback schedule starts scrolling?

Schedule drops mix English platform names with short Korean dates — a few clear lines signal you are planning, not just screenshotting the poster.

1. When the first image appears, ask **컴백 일정 나왔어요?** before anyone else types.
2. If only one date is visible, follow with **언제예요?** to pull the full timeline.
3. After the MV date locks in, send **기대돼요** — safe excitement, no spoiler theories.
4. Before the thread moves on, type **달력에 적어 둘게요** so friends know you are locked in.
5. When a fan meeting date appears, close with **같이 가요** to keep the group together.

## What do Korean speakers say back?

Replies in schedule-drop threads stay short, practical, and date-focused.

You: **컴백 일정 나왔어요?** — *keombaek iljeong nawasseoyo?* — "Did the comeback schedule come out?"  
Them: **응, 금요일이야!** — *eung, geumyoilirya!* — "Yeah, it is Friday!"  
You: **기대돼요.** — *gidaedwaeyo.*
