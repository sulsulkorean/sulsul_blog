tvN's **네 손의 소나타** (*Ne Sonui Sonata*, Four Hands, Two Sonatas) aired its first episode on August 29, 2026, with Song Kang as piano prodigy Kang Pio. Korean fans flooded fan cafes the moment the credits rolled. The phrases they used were short and past-tense: **1화 봤어요?** (*il-hwa bwasseoyo?*), "Did you watch Episode 1?" and **다음 화가 기대돼요** (*daeum hwa-ga gidaedwaeyo*), "I can't wait for the next episode."

> - Did you watch it: **1화 봤어요?** (*il-hwa bwasseoyo?*), "Did you watch Episode 1?"
> - React to a great scene: **피아노 장면이 진짜 멋있어요!** (*piano jangmyeon-i jinjja meosisseoyo!*), "The piano scene is genuinely amazing!"
> - Can't wait for more: **다음 화가 기대돼요.** (*daeum hwa-ga gidaedwaeyo.*), "I can't wait for the next episode."
> - Watch together: **같이 봐요!** (*gachi bwayo!*), "Let's watch together!"

## What do you say right after Episode 1?

The first twenty minutes after a new episode airs is the hottest window in any Korean drama fandom. Most reactions use Pattern 09 past tense — **봤어요** (*bwasseoyo*, watched), **울었어요** (*ureosseoyo*, cried), **멈출 수 없었어요** (*meomchul su eopseosseoyo*, couldn't stop) — short and sent fast.

You: **1화 봤어요? 피아노 장면이 진짜 멋있어요!** — *il-hwa bwasseoyo? piano jangmyeon-i jinjja meosisseoyo!* — "Did you watch Episode 1? The piano scene is genuinely amazing!"  
Them: **네! 엔딩이 대박이에요. 다음 화 빨리 보고 싶어요.** — *ne! ending-i daebak-ieyo. daeum hwa ppalli bogo sipeoyo.* — "Yes! The ending is incredible. I want to see the next episode quickly."  
You: **맞아요. 다음 화가 너무 기대돼요!** — *majayo. daeum hwa-ga neomu gidaedwaeyo!* — "Exactly. I can't wait for the next episode!"

## How do you talk about the drama with Korean fans?

Discussing a drama in Korean means reacting to scenes, not summarizing the plot. Keep each sentence short and attach one adjective: **슬펐어요** (*seulpeosseoyo*, sad), **무서웠어요** (*museoweosseoyo*, scary), **설렜어요** (*seolleosseoyo*, heart-fluttering).

You: **송강 연기 어때요? 저는 진짜 좋아요.** — *Song Kang yeongi eottaeyo? jeoneun jinjja joayo.* — "What do you think of Song Kang's acting? I really love it."  
Them: **저도요! 두 주인공 케미가 너무 좋아요.** — *jeodoyo! du ju-ingong kemi-ga neomu joayo.* — "Me too! The chemistry between the two leads is so good."  
You: **맞아요. 같이 봐요 다음 주에!** — *majayo. gachi bwayo daeum ju-e!* — "Right. Let's watch together next week!"

## What usually trips up international viewers?

The most common mistake is using **재미있어요** (*jaemi-isseoyo*, fun/interesting) for every reaction. Korean fans also use **좋아요** (*joayo*, emotionally warm) and **멋있어요** (*meosisseoyo*, visually impressive) for different occasions. A piano scene in a drama earns **멋있어요** more than **재미있어요**.

A second slip is mixing up **기대돼요** (*gidaedwaeyo*, I'm looking forward to it) — for next-episode anticipation — with **기대했어요** (*gidaehaesseoyo*, I had expectations), which judges the past.
