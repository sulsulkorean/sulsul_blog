Korean phrases for food allergies combine a clear warning and a specific limit. Say **알레르기가 있어요** (*allereugiga isseoyo*), "I have an allergy," then name the ingredient with **못 먹어요** (*mot meogeoyo*), "I can't eat it." Pattern 07 in the SULSUL workbook — **못** (*mot*) for inability — is the line staff act on fastest.

> - Warn the server first: **알레르기가 있어요** (*allereugiga isseoyo*), "I have an allergy."
> - Name what you skip: **해산물 못 먹어요** (*haesanmul mot meogeoyo*), "I can't eat seafood."
> - Ask about an ingredient: **땅콩 들어가요?** (*ttangkong deureogayo?*), "Does it contain peanuts?"
> - Request a change: **고기 빼 주세요** (*gogi ppae juseyo*), "Please leave out the meat."

## How do you tell a server before you order?

Restaurants move fast. Lead with the allergy, not the dish name. **알레르기가 있어요** (*allereugiga isseoyo*) sets the frame; follow immediately with **견과류 못 먹어요** (*gyeonggallyu mot meogeoyo*), "I can't eat nuts," or whatever applies.

1. Catch the server's eye with **실례합니다** (*sillyehamnida*), "Excuse me."
2. State your allergy before pointing at the menu.
3. Name the ingredient you must avoid.
4. Ask **이거 괜찮아요?** (*igeo gwaenchanayo?*), "Is this okay?" while pointing.
5. Close with **감사합니다** (*gamsahamnida*) once they confirm.

Vegetarian travelers can swap **못 먹어요** for **안 먹어요** (*an meogeoyo*), "I don't eat it," when the restriction is a choice rather than a medical reaction — both are polite **해요체** (*haeyoche*).

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| I have an allergy | 알레르기가 있어요 (*allereugiga isseoyo*) |
| No seafood | 해산물 못 먹어요 (*haesanmul mot meogeoyo*) |
| No dairy | 우유 못 먹어요 (*uyu mot meogeoyo*) |
| Leave it out | 빼 주세요 (*ppae juseyo*) |
| Is it in this dish? | 들어가요? (*deureogayo?*) |

## What does the staff say back?

Servers often repeat the ingredient to confirm they understood.

You: **알레르기가 있어요. 땅콩 못 먹어요.** — *allereugiga isseoyo. ttangkong mot meogeoyo.* — "I have an allergy. I can't eat peanuts."  
Them: **땅콩 빼 드릴게요.** — *ttangkong ppae deurilgeyo.* — "I'll leave out the peanuts."  
You: **감사합니다.** — *gamsahamnida.* — "Thank you."

If the kitchen cannot adjust the dish, they may say **어려울 것 같아요** (*eoryeoul geot gatayo*), "That might be difficult." Ask **다른 메뉴 추천해 주세요** (*dareun menyu chucheonhae juseyo*), "Please recommend another menu item," instead of guessing from the photo alone.

## What if you only realize mid-meal?

Pattern 03 — **있어요?** (*isseoyo?*) — works when you need to check one ingredient on a plate already in front of you. **이 소스에 새우 들어가요?** (*i soseue saewu deureogayo?*), "Does this sauce contain shrimp?" is clearer than describing the allergy in English.

Travelers sometimes whisper **매운 거 못 먹어요** (*maeun geo mot meogeoyo*) when they mean spice sensitivity — staff hear it as a preference, not an emergency. For a true allergy, always lead with **알레르기** (*allereugi*). For general ordering lines, see [Korean phrases for ordering at a restaurant](/korean-phrases-ordering-restaurant-korea).
