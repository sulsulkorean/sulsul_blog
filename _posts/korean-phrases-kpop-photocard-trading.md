When Billboard reported on SEVENTEEN's Right Here world tour filling arenas from Seoul to São Paulo, one scene repeated itself at every venue: fans spreading out their photocard binders on a folding table and switching to rapid-fire Korean the moment they spotted a card they needed. A **포카** (*poka*) — short for 포토카드 (*photokadeu*, "photocard") — is a small laminated card printed with an idol's photo, and **교환** (*gyohwan*, "trade/exchange") is the single word that opens every negotiation.

> - Ask if someone has cards to trade: **교환 있어요?** (*gyohwan isseoyo?*), "Do you have any for trade?"
> - Ask if they have a specific card: **이거 있어요?** (*igeo isseoyo?*), "Do you have this one?"
> - Ask the price for a sale: **이거 얼마예요?** (*igeo eolmayeyo?*), "How much is this one?"
> - Greet a fellow fan: **반가워요!** (*ban-gaweoyo!*), "Nice to meet you!"

## How do you start a trade at a fan zone?

Walk up to someone who has a binder or a spread of cards on a table and open with:

**교환 있어요?** — *gyohwan isseoyo?* — "Do you have any for trade?"

If they are open to trading they will nod or hand you the binder. Flip through and point at any card you want, then ask:

**이거 있어요?** — *igeo isseoyo?* — "Do you have this one?"

Show your own cards to signal what you can offer. Most traders are patient with language learners — a smile and pointing goes a long way while your Korean warms up.

If you want to buy rather than swap, add:

**이거 살 수 있어요?** — *igeo sal su isseoyo?* — "Can I buy this?"

Then ask the price. Many traders answer in English numbers, but knowing the Korean is a respectful opener.

## What do you say during the negotiation?

Once you have both shown your cards, these short phrases cover every moment of the back-and-forth:

**잠깐만요.** — *jamkkanmanyo.* — "One moment, please."  
**생각해 볼게요.** — *saenggak hae bolgeyo.* — "Let me think about it."  
**이걸로 할게요.** — *igeolro halgeyo.* — "I'll go with this one."  
**감사합니다!** — *gamsahamnida!* — "Thank you!"

A quick exchange at the table:

You: **교환 있어요?** — *gyohwan isseoyo?* — "Do you have any for trade?"  
Them: **네, 어떤 거 찾으세요?** — *ne, eotteon geo chajeuseyo?* — "Yes, what are you looking for?"  
You: **이거 있어요?** — *igeo isseoyo?* — "Do you have this one?"

## How do you connect with Korean fans beyond the trade?

Photocard trading is also a fast way to make friends. Once a swap is done, most Korean fans are happy to chat. A few phrases take the interaction further:

**이름이 뭐예요?** — *ireumi mwoyeyo?* — "What's your name?"  
**어느 나라에서 왔어요?** — *eoneu naraeseo wasseoyo?* — "Where are you from?"  
**같이 사진 찍어요?** — *gachi sajin jjigyeoyo?* — "Shall we take a photo together?"

End every trade or conversation with **행운을 빌어요** (*haenguneul bireoyo*), "Good luck to you," and you will be remembered as one of the polite international fans.

| Phrase | Use it when |
|---|---|
| 교환 있어요? | Opening a trade conversation |
| 이거 얼마예요? | Asking the price before buying |
| 잠깐만요 | Buying yourself time to decide |
| 감사합니다 | Closing any trade or greeting |
