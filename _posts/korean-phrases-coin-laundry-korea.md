Korean coin laundries — **빨래방** (*ppallaebang*) — sit on almost every residential block in Seoul and run around the clock. They are self-service and fast, but the machines use Korean-only menus. Two phrases carry the whole visit: **세탁기 어떻게 써요?** (*setakgi eotteoke sseoyo?*), "How do I use the washing machine?", and **얼마나 남았어요?** (*eolmana namassseoyo?*), "How much time is left?"

> - Start the machine: **세탁기 어떻게 써요?** (*setakgi eotteoke sseoyo?*) — "How do I use the washing machine?"
> - Check time remaining: **얼마나 남았어요?** (*eolmana namassseoyo?*) — "How much time is left?"
> - Buy detergent: **세제 있어요?** (*seje isseoyo?*) — "Do you have detergent?"
> - Ask the price: **얼마예요?** (*eolmayeyo?*) — "How much is it?"

## How do you start a washing machine at a Korean coin laundry?

Most 빨래방 have a small vending machine next to the washers that sells **세제** (*seje*, laundry detergent) and **섬유유연제** (*seomyu yueonje*, fabric softener). Insert **동전** (*dongjeon*, coins) or tap a transit card, choose a cycle, and press **시작** (*sijak*, start).

If the panel looks unfamiliar, find the attendant or a nearby resident and ask:

**세탁기 어떻게 써요?** — *setakgi eotteoke sseoyo?* — "How do I use the washing machine?"

Most people will walk you through it on the spot. To ask about a specific button, point and say:

**이게 뭐예요?** — *ige mwoyeyo?* — "What is this?"

## What do you say if something goes wrong?

Korean coin laundries are self-service, but an attendant or building owner is usually close by. If the machine stops mid-cycle or the door sticks:

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Machine is broken | 고장났어요 (*gojangnaseoyo*) |
| Door won't open | 문이 안 열려요 (*muni an yeollyeoyo*) |
| Clothes still wet | 빨래가 젖어 있어요 (*ppallaega jeojeo isseoyo*) |
| Need a refund | 환불해 주세요 (*hwanbul hae juseyo*) |

**고장났어요** (*gojangnaseoyo*) works for any broken appliance — machine, dryer, door latch. Say it first so the attendant understands the situation immediately.

You: **세탁기 고장났어요. 문이 안 열려요.**  
— *setakgi gojangnaseoyo. muni an yeollyeoyo.*  
— "The washing machine is broken. The door won't open."  
Them: **잠깐만요, 제가 볼게요.**  
— *jamkkanmanyo, jega bolgeyo.*  
— "One moment, I'll take a look."  
You: **감사합니다. 안에 빨래가 있어요.**  
— *gamsahamnida. ane ppallaega isseoyo.*  
— "Thank you. My laundry is inside."

## How do you use the dryer at a Korean coin laundry?

The **건조기** (*geonjogi*, dryer) usually takes separate payment. Before loading, check the price:

**건조기 얼마예요?** — *geonjogi eolmayeyo?* — "How much is the dryer?"

To ask how long the cycle runs:

**몇 분이에요?** — *myeot bunieyo?* — "How many minutes is it?"

Most dryers run 30–40 minutes for a standard load. Set a timer on your phone, step next door for a snack, and come back when it beeps.

To confirm your load is done before you unload:

**다 됐어요?** — *da dwaesseoyo?* — "Is it done?"

## Why coin laundry Korean matters more than hotel laundry

Hotel laundry is point and sign. The coin laundry is different — an attendant may speak to you directly in Korean and wait for a reply. That is when you need to respond, not search your phone.
