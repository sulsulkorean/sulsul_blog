Korean fried chicken shops — **치킨집** (*chikinijip*) — turn every menu decision into the same two words: sauced or plain? **양념** (*yangnyeom*), the glossy sweet-spicy coating, and **후라이드** (*huraidu*), the original crispy batter, are the two flavors you will say on every visit. Add **하나 주세요** (*hana juseyo*) — "One, please" — and most orders are handled in under ten seconds.

> - Order spicy-sauce: **양념 치킨 하나 주세요** (*yangnyeom chikin hana juseyo*), "One spicy-sauce chicken, please."
> - Order original: **후라이드로 주세요** (*huraiduro juseyo*), "The original fried, please."
> - Ask for half-and-half: **반반으로 주세요** (*banbanuro juseyo*), "Half and half, please."
> - Check the heat: **많이 매워요?** (*mani maewoyo?*), "Is it very spicy?"
> - Tone it down: **덜 맵게 해주세요** (*deol maepge haejuseyo*), "Please make it less spicy."

## How do you pick a fried chicken flavor in Korea?

Most 치킨집 menus list several sauces. The four you will see on almost every board are:

| Flavor (*romanization*) | What it tastes like |
|---|---|
| **양념** (*yangnyeom*) | Spicy-sweet red sauce, sticky coating |
| **후라이드** (*huraidu*) | Original crispy, no sauce |
| **간장** (*ganjang*) | Soy-garlic glaze, savory and mild |
| **반반** (*banban*) | Half spicy-sauce, half original |

If you are not sure which heat level suits you, **반반으로 주세요** is the safest first order — half the pieces arrive sauced, half come plain, and you can taste both before committing next time.

## How do you order Korean fried chicken for delivery?

Most people order 치킨 through an app or by phone. If you call directly, staff will ask for your building number and a contact name. Keep it short:

**여기로 배달 돼요?** — *yeogiro baedal dwaeyo?* — "Can you deliver here?"

If they ask about payment, **카드 되요?** (*kadeu dwaeyo?*) — "Do you accept card?" — covers the most common option. When the doorbell rings, the driver may say **배달 왔어요** (*baedal wasseoyo*), "Delivery is here." Reply with **감사합니다** (*gamsahamnida*) — "Thank you."

## What do you say at the table?

Whether you are in a restaurant or opening a delivery box at home, two phrases come up every time. Fried chicken in Korea always comes with **무** (*mu*) — small cubes of pickled radish — to cut through the grease. If yours did not arrive or ran out, ask:

**무 더 주세요** — *mu deo juseyo* — "More pickled radish, please."

To flag down staff for anything else — a refill, an extra plate, more napkins — say:

**여기요!** — *yeogiyo!* — "Excuse me!"

You: **반반으로 주세요.** — *banbanuro juseyo.* — "Half and half, please."  
Them: **알겠습니다, 잠시만요.** — *algesseumnida, jamsi manyo.* — "Understood, one moment."  
You: **덜 맵게 해주세요.** — *deol maepge haejuseyo.* — "Please make it less spicy."

## What if the spicy sauce is still too hot?

양념 heat level varies between shops — one place's mild is another shop's regular. If the sauce turns out too strong, extra **무** helps balance it out. If you want to skip the heat entirely next time, say **간장 치킨으로 주세요** (*ganjang chikinuro juseyo*) — "Soy-garlic chicken, please." The soy-garlic glaze is savory without any burn and works well with beer or cola.
