Korean phrases at a **헬스장** (*helseujang*, gym) start with Pattern 12 from the SULSUL workbook — verb plus **ㄹ/을 수 있어요?** (*l/eul su isseoyo?*), "Can I…?" Ask **헬스장 이용할 수 있어요?** (*helseujang iyonghal su isseoyo?*), "Can I use the gym?" at the front desk. Pattern 10 — verb plus **고 있어요** (*go isseoyo*), "I am doing" — covers what you are on: **런닝머신 사용하고 있어요** (*reonningmeosin sayonghago isseoyo*), "I am using the treadmill."

> - Ask to use the gym: **헬스장 이용할 수 있어요?** (*helseujang iyonghal su isseoyo?*), "Can I use the gym?"
> - Say what you are on: **런닝머신 사용하고 있어요** (*reonningmeosin sayonghago isseoyo*), "I am using the treadmill."
> - Need a day pass: **일일권 있어요?** (*ilirgwon isseoyo?*), "Do you have a day pass?"
> - Ask where to change: **탈의실 어디예요?** (*taruisil eodiyeyo?*), "Where is the locker room?"
> - Towel request: **수건 하나 주세요** (*sugeon hana juseyo*), "One towel, please."

## How do you check in at a Korean gym?

Hotel gyms and standalone **헬스장** (*helseujang*) often require a membership card or a one-day pass. Front-desk staff answer faster when you lead with Pattern 12 — can I — instead of a long English explanation.

You: **안녕하세요, 헬스장 이용할 수 있어요?** — *annyeonghaseyo, helseujang iyonghal su isseoyo?* — "Hello, can I use the gym?"  
Them: **호텔 투숙객이세요?** — *hotel tusukgaeg-iseyo?* — "Are you a hotel guest?"  
You: **네, 305호예요.** — *ne, sambaek oho-yeyo.* — "Yes, room 305."

|---|---|

| Day pass available? | 일일권 있어요? (*ilirgwon isseoyo?*) |
| Where is the locker room? | 탈의실 어디예요? (*taruisil eodiyeyo?*) |
| One towel, please | 수건 하나 주세요 (*sugeon hana juseyo*) |
| Using the treadmill | 런닝머신 사용하고 있어요 (*reonningmeosin sayonghago isseoyo*) |

## What do you say on the floor?

Pattern 10 tells staff what machine you are on without stopping your set. If someone asks whether a bench is free, answer with **네, 사용하고 있어요** (*ne, sayonghago isseoyo*), "Yes, I am using it," or **아니요, 비었어요** (*aniyo, bieosseoyo*), "No, it is free."

You: **이 기구 사용해도 돼요?** — *i gigu sayonghaedo dwaeyo?* — "Can I use this machine?"  
Them: **네, 괜찮아요.** — *ne, gwaenchanayo.* — "Yes, that is fine."  
You: **감사합니다.** — *gamsahamnida.* — "Thank you."

Pattern 44 from the workbook — **해도 돼요?** (*haedo dwaeyo?*), "May I?" — fits equipment questions the same way it works for parking in [Korean phrases for parking in Korea](/korean-phrases-parking-korea).

## What usually goes wrong at a Korean gym?

Travelers walk straight to the weights and get stopped at the turnstile — ask **일일권 있어요?** at the desk first. A second mistake is wearing outdoor shoes on the mat floor; look for **실내화** (*silnaehwa*, indoor shoes) shelves near **탈의실** (*taruisil*, locker room) and swap before you stretch.

If your shoulder or knee hurts mid-session, **아파요** (*apayo*), "It hurts," from the SULSUL body-and-health unit is enough to pause — then **쉬어야 해요** (*swieoya haeyo*), "I have to rest," uses Pattern 41 (**아/어야 해요**, *a/eoya haeyo*, "have to").

You: **무릎이 아파요.** — *mureup-i apayo.* — "My knee hurts."  
Them: **잠깐 쉬세요.** — *jamkkan swiseyo.* — "Rest for a moment."  
You: **네, 쉬어야 해요.** — *ne, swieoya haeyo.* — "Yes, I need to rest."
