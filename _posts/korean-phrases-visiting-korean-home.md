Visiting a Korean friend's home for the first time feels warm and slightly formal at once — shoes off at the door, dishes appearing before you can offer to help, a farewell that keeps going. Pattern 41 from the SULSUL workbook — **아/어야 해요** (*a/eoya haeyo*), "have to / should" — covers the shoe question before you even step inside. Pattern 03 — **있어요?** (*isseoyo?*) — handles anything you need or want to offer.

> - Shoes off?: **신발 벗어야 해요?** (*sinbal beoseoya haeyo?*), "Should I take off my shoes?"
> - Before the meal: **잘 먹겠습니다** (*jal meokgesseumnida*), "I will eat well."
> - After the meal: **잘 먹었습니다** (*jal meogeosseumnida*), "I ate well, thank you."
> - Offer help: **뭔가 도와드릴까요?** (*mwonga dowadeurilkkayo?*), "Can I help with anything?"
> - Safe farewell: **조심해서 가세요** (*josimhaeseo gaseyo*), "Go safely."

## What do you say when you arrive?

Knock, hear **어서 오세요** (*eoseo oseyo*), "Please come in," and pause at the entrance — called the **현관** (*hyeongwan*). Take off your shoes without asking, but if you are unsure, Pattern 41 settles it politely.

You: **왔어요! 이거 드려도 돼요?** — *wasseoyo! igeo deuryeodo dwaeyo?* — "I'm here! May I give you this?"  
Them: **어머, 감사해요. 어서 들어오세요.** — *eomeo, gamsahaeyo. eoseo deureo oseyo.* — "Oh, thank you. Please come in."  
You: **실례합니다.** — *sillyehamnida.* — "Excuse me for intruding."

| Moment | Say this |
|---|---|
| Coming in | 실례합니다 (*sillyehamnida*) |
| Should I remove shoes? | 신발 벗어야 해요? (*sinbal beoseoya haeyo?*) |
| Before eating | 잘 먹겠습니다 (*jal meokgesseumnida*) |
| Offering help | 뭔가 도와드릴까요? (*mwonga dowadeurilkkayo?*) |

## What do you say at the dinner table?

Korean hosts serve food without waiting to be asked and often say **많이 드세요** (*mani deuseyo*), "Eat a lot." Your job is to eat, express appreciation, and not let your bowl stay empty too long.

Start with **잘 먹겠습니다** (*jal meokgesseumnida*) before the first bite — it signals respect. Mid-meal, say **맛있어요!** (*masisseoyo!*), "It is delicious!" When you genuinely mean it, add **진짜 맛있어요** (*jinjja masisseoyo*), "It is really delicious." Finish with **잘 먹었습니다** (*jal meogeosseumnida*).

You: **이 국 정말 맛있어요.** — *i guk jeongmal masisseoyo.* — "This soup is really delicious."  
Them: **더 드세요!** — *deo deuseyo!* — "Have some more!"  
You: **감사합니다, 조금 더 먹을게요.** — *gamsahamnida, jogeumm deo meogeulgeyo.* — "Thank you, I will have a little more."

## How do you say goodbye at a Korean home?

Leaving takes two rounds. First you say you should go — **이제 가야 해요** (*ije gaya haeyo*), "I have to go now," with Pattern 41. Then your host walks you to the door and says **조심해서 가세요** (*josimhaeseo gaseyo*), "Go safely." Reply with **네, 다음에 또 초대해 주세요** (*ne, daeume tto chodaehaejuseyo*), "Yes, please invite me again next time."

You: **이제 가야 해요. 정말 맛있었어요.** — *ije gaya haeyo. jeongmal masisseotseoyo.* — "I have to go. It was truly delicious."  
Them: **조심해서 가세요!** — *josimhaeseo gaseyo!* — "Go safely!"  
You: **다음에 또 초대해 주세요.** — *daeume tto chodaehaejuseyo.* — "Please invite me again next time."
