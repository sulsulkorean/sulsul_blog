HYBE launched their newest girl group **TUIDE** on August 24, 2026, with the debut single "Sun Kiss" from the EP *TUNE & PLAY* — and within hours Korean fan communities had one phrase running on repeat: **나왔다!** (*nawatda!*), "They're out!" Every K-pop debut creates the same surge of fan-to-fan conversation, and these are the Korean phrases that carry it.

> - First reaction: **나왔다!** (*nawatda!*), "They're out / It dropped!"
> - Ask a friend: **봤어요?** (*bwasseoyo?*), "Have you seen it?"
> - Rate the debut: **데뷔 대박이에요** (*debyu daebagieyo*), "The debut is amazing."
> - Talk about the song: **타이틀곡 너무 좋아요** (*taiteurgo neomu joayo*), "I love the title track so much."
> - Show excitement: **앞으로 기대돼요** (*apeuro gidaedwaeyo*), "I'm looking forward to their future."

## How do you react when a new K-pop group drops?

The moment a debut goes live, Korean fan chats fire off short bursts. Pattern 08 from the SULSUL workbook — **너무** (*neomu*) plus adjective, "too / so [adjective]" — covers almost every reaction: **너무 좋아요** (*neomu joayo*), "So good," **너무 신선해요** (*neomu sinseonhaeyo*), "So fresh."

You: **TUIDE 나왔다! 봤어요?** — *TUIDE nawatda! bwasseoyo?* — "TUIDE dropped! Have you seen it?"  
Them: **아직이요. 지금 볼게요!** — *ajigieyo. jigeum bolgeyo!* — "Not yet. I'll watch it now!"  
You: **진짜 기대돼요.** — *jinjja gidaedwaeyo.* — "I'm really looking forward to it."

## How do you talk about a rookie group with Korean fans?

Talking about a new group is different from talking about an established one. Korean fans often ask **신인이에요?** (*sininieyeo?*), "Are they rookies?" and compare to established groups. Use Pattern 03 — **이에요/예요** — to name and place the group.

You: **TUIDE가 HYBE 소속이에요.** — *TUIDE-ga HYBE sosog-ieyo.* — "TUIDE is under HYBE."  
Them: **어떤 스타일이에요?** — *eotteon seutailieyo?* — "What style are they?"  
You: **팝 스타일이고 완전 신선해요.** — *pap seutailigo wanjeon sinseonhaeyo.* — "They have a pop style and are totally fresh."

Once you have seen the EP, pivot to favorites: **제일 좋아하는 곡이 뭐예요?** (*jeil joahanieun gogieun mwoyeyo?*), "What is your favorite track?" — Pattern 17 from the workbook (comparative favorites).

## How do you cheer on a rookie group in Korean?

Korean fan culture leans heavily on **응원** (*eungwon*), cheering/support. These phrases work for TUIDE's debut and for any group debut you talk about in the future.

You: **앞으로 많이 응원할게요.** — *apeuro mani eungwonhalgeyo.* — "I will cheer for them a lot going forward."  
Them: **저도요! 빨리 팬 됐어요.** — *jeodoyo! ppalli paen dwaesseoyo.* — "Me too! I became a fan quickly."  
You: **완전 기대돼요.** — *wanjeon gidaedwaeyo.* — "I'm so excited for them."

**소름 돋았어요** (*soreum dodasseoyo*), "I got goosebumps," is the highest real-time compliment in Korean fan vocabulary — save it for the moment a performance actually stops you.
