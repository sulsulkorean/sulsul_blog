To ask where the restroom is in Korean, say **화장실 어디 있어요?** (*hwajangsil eodi isseoyo?*), "Where is the restroom?" It works in a cafe, a subway station, or a convenience store. Open with **실례합니다** (*sillyehamnida*), "Excuse me," so you do not startle the person you are asking.

> - **화장실 어디 있어요?** (*hwajangsil eodi isseoyo?*) — "Where is the restroom?"
> - **화장실 있어요?** (*hwajangsil isseoyo?*) — "Is there a restroom?"
> - **남자 화장실** (*namja hwajangsil*) / **여자 화장실** (*yeoja hwajangsil*) — men's / women's restroom
> - **출구** (*chulgu*) — exit (restrooms are often near one)

## How do you ask in a cafe or restaurant?

Staff hear this question all day. One short sentence beats a long English explanation.

You: **실례합니다. 화장실 어디 있어요?** — *sillyehamnida. hwajangsil eodi isseoyo?* — "Excuse me. Where is the restroom?"  
Them: **저쪽이에요.** — *jeojjogiyeyo.* — "It is over there."  
You: **감사합니다.** — *gamsahamnida.* — "Thank you."

If they point, follow the direction and look for **화장실** on a door. Men's rooms are marked **남** (*nam*) and women's **여** (*yeo*).

## What if you only need to know whether one exists?

At a small shop, ask whether a restroom is available before you walk to the back.

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Where is it? | 화장실 어디 있어요? (*hwajangsil eodi isseoyo?*) |
| Is there one? | 화장실 있어요? (*hwajangsil isseoyo?*) |
| Men's room | 남자 화장실 (*namja hwajangsil*) |
| Women's room | 여자 화장실 (*yeoja hwajangsil*) |
| Near the exit | 출구 쪽 (*chulgu jjok*) |

**화장실 있어요?** (*hwajangsil isseoyo?*) is Pattern 03 in the SULSUL workbook — attach **있어요?** (*isseoyo?*) to the thing you are looking for. The full location question uses Pattern 04: place noun plus **어디 있어요?** (*eodi isseoyo?*)

## What usually goes wrong?

Learners often skip **실례합니다** and blurt **bathroom** in English. Koreans understand, but the interaction feels abrupt. Another mistake is mixing up **어디예요?** (*eodiyeyo?*) and **어디 있어요?** (*eodi isseoyo?*) — both work for restrooms, yet **어디 있어요?** sounds more natural when you expect the room to be inside the building.

In subway stations, restrooms sit near **출구** (*chulgu*), the exit. If someone says **출구 쪽** (*chulgu jjok*), walk toward the exit signs, not the platform.

## Steps when you need a restroom fast

1. Catch someone's eye and say **실례합니다**.
2. Ask the location question from the opening line in one breath.
3. Repeat **화장실** (*hwajangsil*) if they look confused — the word itself is clear.
4. Follow their point or gesture.
5. Check the door label for **남** or **여** before you walk in.
