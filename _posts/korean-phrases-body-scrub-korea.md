The **때밀이** (*ttaemiri*) is a Korean body scrub service at most 찜질방 (*jjimjilbang*). An attendant uses a damp **이태리 타올** (*itaeri taol*) — a coarse exfoliating mitt — to scrub dead skin off your back, arms, and legs. The interaction is almost wordless once it begins, but three phrases cover booking, adjusting pressure, and closing politely.

> - Book the service: **때밀이 할 수 있어요?** (*ttaemiri hal su isseoyo?*), "Can I get a body scrub?"
> - Gentler pressure: **살살 해 주세요** (*salsal hae juseyo*), "Please be gentler."
> - Harder pressure: **세게 해 주세요** (*sege hae juseyo*), "Please scrub harder."
> - It hurts: **좀 아파요** (*jom apayo*), "It hurts a bit."
> - After the service: **수고하셨어요** (*sugohasyeosseoyo*), "Thank you for your work."

## How do you book a body scrub at a Korean jjimjilbang?

After [checking into the jjimjilbang](/korean-phrases-jimjilbang-korea) and collecting your locker key, look for a board listing added services — body scrub, massage, hair wash. Walk to the desk and ask:

**때밀이 할 수 있어요?** — *ttaemiri hal su isseoyo?* — "Can I get a body scrub?"

Pay at the desk first. The attendant works in the wet room and will call your locker number when it is your turn.

You: **때밀이 할 수 있어요?** — *ttaemiri hal su isseoyo?* — "Can I get a body scrub?"  
Them: **지금 바로 가능해요. 락커 번호 몇 번이에요?** — *jigeum baro ganeunghaeyo. rakeo beonho myeot beonieyo?* — "Available right now. What is your locker number?"  
You: **이십사번이에요.** — *isip sa beonieyo.* — "It is number 24."

## What do you say during the body scrub session?

The attendant will gesture for you to lie on the low plastic table. Once the scrub starts, give simple feedback — no explanation needed:

**살살 해 주세요.** — *salsal hae juseyo.* — "Please be gentler."

For more pressure: **세게 해 주세요** (*sege hae juseyo*). If a spot is sore: **좀 아파요** (*jom apayo*). Attendants adjust immediately. If they skip an area, say **등도 해 주세요** (*deungdo hae juseyo*), "Please do the back too."

## How do you end the session and thank the attendant?

The standard phrase to close any Korean service interaction is **수고하셨어요** (*sugohasyeosseoyo*), "You worked hard, thank you." Say it as you step off the table — the same line you say to a hairdresser, a taxi driver, or a cashier.

To add that it felt great, say **시원했어요** (*siwonhaesseoyo*), "That was refreshing." Korean uses **시원하다** (*siwonhada*) — literally "cool and refreshing" — for satisfied relief after a good scrub, a cold drink, or a long exhale.
