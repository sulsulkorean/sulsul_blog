Here are nine of the most quoted K-drama lines in English, with the Korean beside them. The best known is from *Goblin*: **날이 좋아서, 날이 좋지 않아서, 날이 적당해서, 모든 날이 좋았다**, "Because the weather was good, because it wasn't, because it was just right, every day was good." The translations below are mine and stay close to the Korean, so they may not match your subtitles word for word. That gap is the interesting part: several of these lines work because of a pun, a name, or a speech level that English has no way to carry.

> - **모든 날이 좋았다** (*Goblin*) — "Every day was good."
> - **편안함에 이르렀나?** (*My Mister*) — "Have you reached peace?"
> - **사과할까요, 고백할까요?** (*Descendants of the Sun*) — "Should I apologize, or confess?"
> - **이 안에 너 있다** (*Lovers in Paris*) — "You're in here."
> - **이게 최선입니까?** (*Secret Garden*) — "Is this your best?"
> - **저를 전적으로 믿으셔야 합니다** (*SKY Castle*) — "You must trust me completely."
> - **멋지다 연진아** (*The Glory*) — "Bravo, Yeon-jin."
> - **우린 깐부잖아** (*Squid Game*) — "We're gganbu, aren't we?"
> - **기러기, 토마토, 스위스** (*Extraordinary Attorney Woo*) — "Goose, tomato, Switzerland."

## The quotes people save: lines about love and peace

**날이 좋아서, 날이 좋지 않아서, 날이 적당해서, 모든 날이 좋았다**
(*na-ri jo-a-seo, na-ri jo-chi a-na-seo, na-ri jeok-dang-hae-seo, mo-deun na-ri jo-a-tta*)

This comes from the goblin's farewell in *Goblin*, and it opens with **너와 함께한 시간 모두 눈부셨다** (*neo-wa ham-kke-han si-gan mo-du nun-bu-syeot-da*), "All the time I spent with you was dazzling." In Korean, the line runs on one word, **날**, which can mean both "day" and "weather" when paired with **좋다**. **날이 좋아서** is "because the day was nice." That is why the ending feels earned: the same word returns, and now it means every day, good weather or not.

**편안함에 이르렀나?** (*pyeon-an-ha-me i-reu-reon-na?*)

This is the question Dong-hoon asks Ji-an near the end of *My Mister*. English gets the words, "Have you reached peace?", but loses the point: her name, 지안, was explained earlier in the show as "to reach peace." So the line is also her name, asked back to her as a question. The old-fashioned **-나** ending adds weight too. Nobody says this at a bus stop.

**사과할까요, 고백할까요?** (*sa-gwa-hal-kka-yo, go-baek-hal-kka-yo?*)

The captain in *Descendants of the Sun* offers this after a kiss neither of them has explained. **-할까요?** means "shall I?", and it makes the line a polite, almost cheeky choice: **사과** (apology) or **고백** (confession of love). Fans still enjoy that **사과** also means "apple."

## The quotes people repeat as jokes

Some lines left the drama and became things Koreans say to make each other laugh.

**이 안에 너 있다** (*i a-ne neo it-da*)

From *Lovers in Paris* (2004), said with a hand at the chest: "Inside here, there's you." The grammar is plain enough to learn from: **이 안에** means "inside this," **너** means "you," and **있다** means "exist." Twenty years on, it sounds gloriously over the top. Say it now and people will laugh, which is what you want.

**이게 최선입니까? 확실해요?** (*i-ge choe-seo-nim-ni-kka? hwak-sil-hae-yo?*)

In *Secret Garden*, a department store CEO says this while judging an employee's work: "Is this your best? Are you sure?" The ending **-입니까** is the stiff, formal question form, so the coldness is built into the grammar. Today, friends use it on a bad haircut or a burnt pancake.

**어머니, 저를 전적으로 믿으셔야 합니다** (*eo-meo-ni, jeo-reul jeon-jeo-geu-ro mi-deu-syeo-ya ham-ni-da*)

The admissions coordinator in *SKY Castle* tells a mother: "You must trust me completely." The word **어머니** ("mother") makes it sound respectful, while the delivery says the opposite. That tension is why it became a meme.

## The quotes that don't survive translation

**멋지다 연진아** (*meot-ji-da yeon-ji-na*)

In *The Glory*, Dong-eun says this to the woman who tormented her at school. "Bravo, Yeon-jin" is accurate and still misses most of it. **멋지다** is plain praise, and **연진아** is the name plus **-아**, the ending you use with close friends. It sounds like an old friend's warm compliment, and that is what makes it chilling. Her other line works the same way: **연진아, 나 지금 되게 신나** (*yeon-ji-na, na ji-geum doe-ge sin-na*), "Yeon-jin, I'm so excited right now."

To use the pattern with a name of your own, add **-아** after a consonant and **-야** after a vowel:

- **민준아** (*min-ju-na*) — calling Min-jun
- **지수야** (*ji-su-ya*) — calling Ji-su

**우린 깐부잖아** (*u-rin kkan-bu-ja-na*)

**깐부** is old playground slang for your partner in a marble game, the friend who shares everything with you. *Squid Game* needed no better word, so the English subtitles simply kept it as "gganbu." The ending **-잖아** means "you know," or "aren't we?" It reminds someone of what you both already know, which is what makes the marble scene hurt.

**기러기, 토마토, 스위스, 인도인, 별똥별, 우영우**
(*gi-reo-gi, to-ma-to, seu-wi-seu, in-do-in, byeol-ttong-byeol, u-yeong-u*)

Attorney Woo introduces herself with this list: goose, tomato, Switzerland, Indian person, shooting star. In English it sounds random. In Korean, every word is a palindrome, read the same forward and backward, just like her name. No translation can do that, but it makes a great first reading exercise in Hangul.

## How a real conversation uses these

Friends quote these lines the way English speakers quote movies. Here is a short exchange after someone cooks dinner:

**A:** 라면 끓였어. 먹어 봐. (*ra-myeon kkeu-ryeo-sseo. meo-geo bwa.*) — "I made ramyeon. Try it."

**B:** 이게 최선입니까? (*i-ge choe-seo-nim-ni-kka?*) — "Is this your best?"

**A:** 야! 저를 전적으로 믿으셔야 합니다. (*ya! jeo-reul jeon-jeo-geu-ro mi-deu-syeo-ya ham-ni-da.*) — "Hey! You must trust me completely."

**B:** 음… 멋지다, 민준아. (*eum… meot-ji-da, min-ju-na.*) — "Hmm… bravo, Min-jun."

The joke is the jump in register. Friends normally speak casually, so a sudden **-입니까** tells everyone a quote is coming.

## Where fans slip when quoting

**Saying 멋지다 연진아 as a compliment to a new friend.** Koreans who have seen *The Glory* will hear the threat. Use **진짜 멋있어요** (*jin-jja meo-si-sseo-yo*), "You're really cool," if you mean it.

**Using -아 and -야 with people you just met.** These endings are for friends and people younger than you. With anyone else, use the name plus **씨**, as in **민준 씨**, or a title.

**Asking 편안함에 이르렀나? as "are you okay?"** It sounds like a line from an old book. The everyday version is **괜찮아요?** (*gwaen-cha-na-yo?*).

**Pronouncing 좋았다 letter by letter.** The ㅎ goes quiet and the double ㅆ tightens the next sound, so it comes out close to *jo-a-tta*.
