Korean Baseball Organization (KBO) games are one of the loudest, most welcoming crowd experiences in Seoul. Home fans sing synchronized chants for every batter, the stands sell fried chicken and beer, and the energy builds from the first inning whether you understand Korean or not. A handful of phrases lets you join in instead of just watching.

> - Ask your neighbor **어느 팀 응원해요?** (*eoneu tim eungwonhaeyo?*) — "Which team are you cheering for?"
> - Cheer a base hit with **안타!** (*anta!*) — the crowd shouts it every time.
> - Shout **홈런!** (*homreon!*) when the ball clears the fence.

## What do you say when your team scores?

KBO crowds respond to every play in unison. For a base hit the whole stand yells **안타!** (*anta!*). A home run gets **홈런!** (*homreon!*), and when you want to cheer your team generally, stretch out a loud **파이팅!** (*paiting!*), Korea's all-purpose rally cry.

| Moment | Shout this |
|---|---|
| Base hit | 안타! (*anta!*) |
| Home run | 홈런! (*homreon!*) |
| General rally | 파이팅! (*paiting!*) |
| Bad call or error | 아 진짜! (*a jinjja!*) |
| Team wins | 이겼다! (*igyeotta!*) |

**아 진짜!** (*a jinjja!*) is the universal "are you serious?!" — swap it in whenever the crowd groans at a bad call.

## How do you talk with fans sitting nearby?

Half the fun of a KBO game is chatting with the people around you. Start with the team question and it usually leads somewhere:

You: **어느 팀 응원해요?** — *eoneu tim eungwonhaeyo?* — "Which team are you cheering for?"  
Them: **저는 두산 팬이에요. 어떠세요?** — *jeoneun Dusan paen-ieyo. eotteuseyo?* — "I'm a Doosan fan. What about you?"  
You: **저도 두산이에요!** — *jeodo Dusan-ieyo!* — "Me too, Doosan!"

**저도** (*jeodo*) means "me too" — one of the fastest ways to build instant stadium solidarity. If you support the visiting team, say your team name plus **팬이에요** (*paen-ieyo*) and prepare for friendly teasing.

## What do you say at the concession stand?

KBO concession stands are a highlight of the experience. Lines move fast, so have your order ready.

Say **맥주 하나 주세요** (*maekju hana juseyo*) for a beer or **치킨 하나 주세요** (*chikin hana juseyo*) for fried chicken — the KBO staple. Vendors usually repeat your order back before you pay, so just listen for **네** (*ne*, yes) and nod along.

One practical tip: check whether your seat is in the home or visitor section before you start cheering loudly for the wrong team. Ask someone **저 여기 앉아도 돼요?** (*jeo yeogi anjado dwaeyo?*), "Is it okay if I sit here?" before you claim an empty spot in the packed home stand.
