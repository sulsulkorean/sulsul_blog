Some K-drama scenes end with the whole fandom hungry — a bowl of noodles eaten on a rooftop, a stew shared at a kitchen table — and the group chat turns into a recipe thread. **레시피 알려주세요** (*resipi allyeojuseyo*), "Please share the recipe," is the line that starts it, and **재료 어디서 사요?** (*jaeryo eodiseo sayo?*), "Where do you buy the ingredients?" is the one that decides whether you actually cook it this week.

> - Name the dish: **이거 드라마에 나온 거예요** (*igeo deuramae naon geoyeyo*), "This is the one from the drama."
> - Ask for the recipe: **레시피 알려주세요** (*resipi allyeojuseyo*), "Please share the recipe."
> - Find the ingredients: **재료 어디서 사요?** (*jaeryo eodiseo sayo?*), "Where do you buy the ingredients?"
> - Check the seasoning: **간 좀 봐주세요** (*gan jom bwajuseyo*), "Taste it and tell me if the seasoning is right."
> - Report the result: **맛있게 됐어요** (*masitge dwaesseoyo*), "It turned out well."

## How do you get from a drama scene to a real dish?

Fans who cook the scene almost always ask a Korean friend first, because the recipe blogs assume you already know the ingredient names.

1. Send a screenshot with **이거 드라마에 나온 거예요** so everyone knows which scene you mean.
2. Ask **레시피 알려주세요** — home versions come back shorter and easier than anything you would find searching.
3. At the store, **재료 어디서 사요?** saves you from wandering the aisles guessing at labels.
4. While it simmers, **간 좀 봐주세요** invites the person next to you to fix it before it is too late.
5. Send a photo of the finished bowl with **맛있게 됐어요** — that is the reply the thread is waiting for.

## What do Korean speakers say back?

Recipe talk is warm and casual — people love being asked about food they grew up with.

You: **이거 드라마에 나온 거예요.** — *igeo deuramae naon geoyeyo.* — "This is the one from the drama."  
Them: **아, 그거 저도 봤어요. 집에서도 쉽게 만들어요** — *a, geugeo jeodo bwasseoyo. jibeseodo swipge mandeureoyo* — "Oh, I watched that too. It's easy to make at home."  
You: **재료 어디서 사요?** — *jaeryo eodiseo sayo?*

If the shop clerk points at a shelf instead of answering, repeating the ingredient name on its own is usually enough. For the aisles themselves, see [Korean phrases at a Korean supermarket](/korean-phrases-supermarket-korea).

## What usually goes wrong in the kitchen?

Learners look up the dish name in English, ask for it by that name, and get a blank look — the drama title travels, the food name often does not. Showing the scene with **이거 드라마에 나온 거예요** clears it up in one message.

The other slip is cooking the whole thing alone and only asking after it is done. **간 좀 봐주세요** halfway through is the sentence that saves the pot. When someone else cooks for you, see [how to compliment a Korean friend's cooking](/compliment-korean-friends-cooking).
