The best Korean bio lines are short, and most of them **aren't full sentences**. Korean profile text usually ends in a noun or an adverb, like **천천히, 꾸준히** (*cheon-cheon-hi, kku-jun-hi*), "slowly, steadily," or **오늘도 행복하게** (*o-neul-do haeng-bo-ka-ge*), "happily, today too." Leave out the subject and the verb ending, and the line reads like a quiet motto rather than a translated sentence. Koreans call this text **프로필 문구** (*peu-ro-pil mun-gu*, "profile line") or **소개글** (*so-gae-geul*, "intro text"), and on KakaoTalk it's the **상태 메시지** (*sang-tae me-si-ji*, "status message").

> - **천천히, 꾸준히** (*cheon-cheon-hi, kku-jun-hi*) — "Slowly, steadily"
> - **오늘도 행복하게** (*o-neul-do haeng-bo-ka-ge*) — "Happily, today too"
> - **나답게** (*na-dap-kke*) — "Like myself"
> - **한국어 공부 중** (*han-gu-geo gong-bu jung*) — "Studying Korean"
> - **꽃길만 걷자** (*kkot-kkil-man geot-jja*) — "Let's walk only on flower paths"

## Why Korean bios drop the sentence ending

In conversation you'd say **저는 한국어를 공부하고 있어요** (*jeo-neun han-gu-geo-reul gong-bu-ha-go i-sseo-yo*), "I am studying Korean." That's Pattern 10 from the SULSUL workbook, **고 있어요** for something in progress, and it's exactly right when you talk to someone.

A bio isn't talking to anyone in particular, so Koreans strip it down. Three endings do most of the work:

| Ending | What it does | Bio example |
|---|---|---|
| noun + **중** | "in the middle of" | 한국어 공부 중 — "studying Korean" |
| adverb **~게 / ~히** | "in this way" | 오늘도 행복하게 — "happily, today too" |
| bare noun list | a snapshot of you | 커피, 여행, 한국어 — "coffee, travel, Korean" |

Dropping **저는** ("I") is normal everywhere in Korean, not just in bios. The subject is obvious: it's your profile.

## Bio lines about how you live

These are the kind of lines you'll see on Korean friends' profiles. They are calm, a little poetic, and never loud.

- **천천히, 꾸준히** (*cheon-cheon-hi, kku-jun-hi*) — "Slowly, steadily." A favorite for anyone working on something long-term.
- **나답게** (*na-dap-kke*) — "Like myself." The **ㅂ** in 답 makes the **ㄱ** tense, so it's *kke*, not *ge*.
- **지금, 여기** (*ji-geum, yeo-gi*) — "Now, here."
- **소소한 행복** (*so-so-han haeng-bok*) — "Small happiness." Close to the popular word **소확행**, short for "small but certain happiness."
- **오늘 하루도 수고했어** (*o-neul ha-ru-do su-go-hae-sseo*) — "You worked hard today too." A line to yourself, built on **수고하다** from Pattern 91, the comfort-and-encouragement set.
- **행복은 멀리 있지 않아** (*haeng-bo-geun meol-li it-jji a-na*) — "Happiness isn't far away." This uses the long negative **지 않아**, the written-sounding cousin of the **안** in Pattern 07.

If you want one line with a story behind it, **하다 보면 돼** (*ha-da bo-myeon dwae*), "Keep doing it and it works out," comes from Pattern 73, **다 보면**: "if you keep at it, eventually…" It's a perfect fit for a language learner. ## Bio lines for Korean learners

Your bio is also a quiet signal to Korean speakers that you'd welcome a message in Korean. Pick one:

- **한국어 공부 중** (*han-gu-geo gong-bu jung*) — "Studying Korean." The simplest and most common.
- **한국어 조금 할 줄 알아요** (*han-gu-geo jo-geum hal jjul a-ra-yo*) — "I can speak a little Korean." Pattern 13, **ㄹ/을 줄 알아요**, for skills you have.
- **한국어로 말 걸어 주세요** (*han-gu-geo-ro mal geo-reo ju-se-yo*) — "Talk to me in Korean." Pattern 23, **아/어 주세요**, makes it a friendly request.
- **일단 해 보자** (*il-dan hae bo-ja*) — "Let's just try." Pattern 14, **아/어 보다**, is "try doing."

Mix languages if you like. **Toronto ✈ 서울** or **coffee · books · 한국어** looks natural to Korean readers, who mix English into their own bios all the time.

## Bio lines for K-pop fans and travelers

Fan bios lean warmer and more playful:

- **꽃길만 걷자** (*kkot-kkil-man geot-jja*) — "Let's walk only on flower paths," a wish that someone's road is easy and bright. Fans say it to idols constantly, and it works for yourself too.
- **언젠가 서울에서** (*eon-jen-ga seo-u-re-seo*) — "Someday, in Seoul." Pattern 26, **에서**, marks where something happens, and leaving the verb off makes it a dream.
- **서울 여행 중** (*seo-ul yeo-haeng jung*) — "Traveling in Seoul." Swap it in while you're there.
- **서울 사는 캐나다 사람** (*seo-ul sa-neun kae-na-da sa-ram*) — "A Canadian who lives in Seoul." Pattern 33, the noun modifier **는**, turns "lives in Seoul" into a description. Swap in your own country.

## Checking your bio with a Korean friend

Before you post, it's worth asking someone. Pattern 44, **아/어도 돼요?**, asks "Is it okay if…?"

> **You:** 이거 프로필에 써도 돼요? 자연스러워요?
> *i-geo peu-ro-pi-re sseo-do dwae-yo? ja-yeon-seu-reo-wo-yo?*
> "Can I put this in my profile? Does it sound natural?"
>
> **Friend:** "천천히, 꾸준히"요? 네, 완전 자연스러워요.
> *cheon-cheon-hi, kku-jun-hi-yo? ne, wan-jeon ja-yeon-seu-reo-wo-yo.*
> "'Slowly, steadily'? Yes, totally natural."
>
> **You:** 그럼 "나는 나를 사랑한다"는요?
> *geu-reom na-neun na-reul sa-rang-han-da-neun-yo?*
> "Then what about 'I love myself'?"
>
> **Friend:** 음… 그건 좀 어색해요. "나를 사랑하기"가 더 좋아요.
> *eum… geu-geon jom eo-sae-kae-yo. na-reul sa-rang-ha-gi-ga deo jo-a-yo.*
> "Hmm… that's a bit awkward. 'Loving myself' is better."

Notice the fix. **사랑하기** (*sa-rang-ha-gi*) turns the verb into a noun with **기**, which is exactly how bios like to end. **어색해요** (*eo-sae-kae-yo*), "it's awkward," is the word you want to hear before posting, not after. The **는요?** in the third line is Pattern 16, the ping-pong "what about…?"

## Mistakes that make a bio look machine-translated

**Writing a full textbook sentence.** **나는 매일 행복하게 살기를 원한다** is grammatical, but it reads like a translation exercise. **매일 행복하게** says the same thing in half the space.

**Using 요 endings.** **한국어 공부해요** isn't wrong, but polite endings sound like you're speaking to one person. Bios usually skip them.

**Romanization only.** *Cheoncheonhi kkujunhi* is hard for Koreans to read. Write it in Hangul, and keep the romanization for your own practice.

**Big English quotes, word for word.** Famous English sayings often come out stiff when translated literally. A short Korean line that means something to you beats a long borrowed one.

**Slang you can't explain.** If a Korean friend asks about your bio and you don't know what it means, the conversation stops there. Pick a line you can actually talk about. For slang that does work, see [Korean slang words and what they mean](/korean-slang-words-what-they-mean).

## Say your bio out loud

A bio is the first Korean sentence many people will ever ask you about, so be able to say it. Read your line aloud five times, then try explaining it: **제 프로필 문구는 "천천히, 꾸준히"예요** (*je peu-ro-pil mun-gu-neun cheon-cheon-hi, kku-jun-hi-ye-yo*), "My bio line is 'slowly, steadily.'" That's Pattern 05, **이에요/예요**, the pattern for saying what something is. If you can say your bio and explain why you chose it, you've already got your first small-talk topic in Korean.
