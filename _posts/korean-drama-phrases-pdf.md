If you want a Korean drama phrases PDF, the sheet is right below: about forty lines that come up in almost every K-drama, sorted by the kind of scene you hear them in — the meet-cute, the fight, the confession, the hospital bed. To keep it as a PDF, open your browser's Print option and choose **Save as PDF** (on iPhone: Share → Print → spread two fingers on the preview → Save to Files). Then pause your next episode on any of these lines and you will recognize it instantly.

> - **괜찮아?** — *gwaen-cha-na?* — are you okay?
> - **가지 마** — *ga-ji ma* — don't go
> - **미안해** — *mi-an-hae* — I'm sorry
> - **보고 싶어** — *bo-go si-peo* — I miss you
> - **말도 안 돼** — *mal-do an dwae* — no way / that makes no sense

Most lists of drama phrases are sorted A to Z, which is useless while you are watching. A drama does not go alphabetically; it goes from scene to scene. So this sheet follows the story, and every line is in the plain form characters actually use with each other. At the end you will see how to make each one polite enough for real life.

## How to keep this sheet as a PDF

**iPhone:** tap Share → Print, then spread two fingers on the page preview. It opens as a PDF. Tap Share again → Save to Files or Books.

**Android:** three dots → Share → Print → choose **Save as PDF** as the printer → tap the download icon.

**Laptop:** Ctrl+P (Cmd+P on Mac) → **Save as PDF**.

If you watch on a TV, print one copy and keep it next to the remote. Ticking off a line each time you hear it is a surprisingly fun way to train your ear.

## Why the same lines show up in every drama

Look across the tables and you will notice a few endings doing most of the work:

- **-어/아** at the end of a plain statement or question: 괜찮아, 보고 싶어, 사랑해, 미안해.
- **-지 마** for "don't": 가지 마, 걱정하지 마, 울지 마 (don't cry).
- **-자** for "let's": 가자, 헤어지자, 그만하자, 먹자 (let's eat).

Once you know these three, new lines stop being new. When a character says **울지 마**, you hear 지 마 and know it is "don't ___" even before you look up 울다 (to cry). When someone says **먹자**, the 자 tells you it is "let's ___."

## One scene, read with the sheet

Here is the kind of scene almost every romance drama has, built only from lines on the sheet:

> **Ji-woo:** *(running in)* 괜찮아? 다친 데 없어?
> **Min-jun:** 괜찮아. 걱정하지 마.
> **Ji-woo:** 말도 안 돼… 진짜 괜찮아?
> **Min-jun:** *(smiles)* 내가 있잖아. 가자.

Ji-woo asks "Are you okay? Are you hurt anywhere?" Min-jun answers "I'm fine. Don't worry." She cannot believe it — "No way… are you really okay?" — and he closes the scene with "I'm here. Let's go." Four lines, and you followed every word without subtitles.

## Which lines you can say in real life (and how to soften them)

Every line on this sheet is in **반말**, the casual speech characters use with close friends, partners, and family. That is why it sounds natural on screen. With a stranger, a shop clerk, or anyone older, it can sound rude. Most lines become polite with one small change — add **요**:

| Drama line | Polite version | When |
|---|---|---|
| 괜찮아? | 괜찮아요? | asking anyone if they're okay |
| 미안해 | 미안해요 / 죄송합니다 | apologizing to someone you don't know well |
| 잠깐만 | 잠깐만요 | asking someone to wait |
| 진짜? | 진짜요? | reacting in a conversation |
| 알았어 | 알겠어요 | "okay, got it" to staff or a teacher |

A few lines are best left on the screen: **비켜**, **너 뭐야?**, and **미쳤어?** sound aggressive outside a drama. And **헤어지자** is, of course, only for one very specific conversation.

## Three ways fans misuse drama phrases

**Using 반말 with a stranger.** Saying **어디야?** to a shop worker sounds like you are scolding them. Add 요 and you are fine.

**Saying 사랑해 too early.** In dramas it lands in episode 12 for a reason. Between friends, **좋아해** or a cheerful **최고야!** (you're the best) fits much better.

**Reading the sheet without saying it.** You can recognize all forty lines and still mumble them when it counts. The words stick when your mouth has made them a few times.
