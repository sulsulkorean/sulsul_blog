Korean phrases at a naengmyeon restaurant in Korea lean on Pattern 23 from the SULSUL workbook — **주세요** (*juseyo*), "Please give me" — and Pattern 12 — **(V)ㄹ/을 수 있어요?** (*eul su isseoyo?*), "Can you…?" The staff's first question is always which style you want: **물냉면** (*mulnaengmyeon*, broth-based cold noodles) or **비빔냉면** (*bibimnaengmyeon*, spicy mixed cold noodles). One extra word — **맵기 조절 돼요?** (*maepgi jojeol dwaeyo?*) — asks if the kitchen can dial back the heat.

> - Order the bowl: **냉면 주세요** (*naengmyeon juseyo*) — "Naengmyeon, please."
> - Choose broth style: **물냉면으로 주세요** (*mulnaengmyeon-euro juseyo*) — "Mul-naengmyeon, please."
> - Adjust spice: **맵기 조절 돼요?** (*maepgi jojeol dwaeyo?*) — "Can you adjust the spice level?"
> - Ask for scissors: **가위 주세요** (*gawi juseyo*) — "Scissors, please."
> - More broth: **육수 더 주세요** (*yuksu deo juseyo*) — "More broth, please."

## How do you order naengmyeon in Korean?

**냉면집** (*naengmyeonjip*, naengmyeon restaurant) menus are short: pick broth or spicy, then add toppings like **편육** (*pyeonyuk*, sliced boiled meat) or **삶은 달걀** (*salmeun dalgyal*, boiled egg). The staff usually ask the style question first — answer with **물냉면이요** (*mulnaengmyeon-iyo*) or **비빔냉면이요** (*bibimnaengmyeon-iyo*) and you are done.

You: **안녕하세요. 냉면 이인분 주세요.** — *annyeonghaseyo. naengmyeon i-inbun juseyo.* — "Hello. Two portions of naengmyeon, please."  
Them: **물냉면이에요, 비빔냉면이에요?** — *mulnaengmyeon-ieyo, bibimnaengmyeon-ieyo?* — "Mul-naengmyeon or bibim-naengmyeon?"  
You: **물냉면으로 주세요. 맵기 조절 돼요?** — *mulnaengmyeon-euro juseyo. maepgi jojeol dwaeyo?* — "Mul-naengmyeon, please. Can you adjust the spice level?"

## What do you say when the bowl arrives?

Naengmyeon noodles are long — most restaurants set scissors on the table so you can cut them before eating. If no scissors appear, ask for **가위** (*gawi*, scissors): the staff will bring a pair without any fuss.

You: **가위 주세요.** — *gawi juseyo.* — "Scissors, please."  
Them: **네, 여기 있어요.** — *ne, yeogi isseoyo.* — "Yes, here you go."  
You: **감사해요. 육수 더 주세요.** — *gamsahaeyo. yuksu deo juseyo.* — "Thank you. More broth, please."

The **더 주세요** (*deo juseyo*, more please) pattern from [ordering at a Korean restaurant](/korean-phrases-ordering-restaurant-korea) works the same way whenever you want a refill.

## What usually goes wrong at a Korean naengmyeon restaurant?

Travelers often skip **맵기 조절 돼요?** and then struggle with a bowl of bibim-naengmyeon they cannot finish. The sauce in the spicy version is much hotter than it looks — ask about spice before you commit to the style.

A second mistake is waiting for a fork. Naengmyeon is eaten with metal chopsticks and a spoon — there are no forks on the table. If chopsticks are new to you, try cutting the noodles short with scissors first, then scoop with the spoon.
