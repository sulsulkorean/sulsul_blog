A fandom keeps two dates in its head that never show up on a comeback schedule: **입대** (*ipdae*), enlistment, and **전역** (*jeonyeok*), discharge. When a notice goes up, international fans usually see the English summary first and the Korean thread second — and the Korean thread is where the short lines are. **입대 언제예요?** (*ipdae eonjeyeyo?*), "When is the enlistment?" is the first question typed, and **건강하게 다녀오세요** (*geonganghage danyeooseyo*), "Stay healthy and come back," is what the fan letters say.

> - Ask the date: **입대 언제예요?** (*ipdae eonjeyeyo?*), "When is the enlistment?"
> - Send him off: **건강하게 다녀오세요** (*geonganghage danyeooseyo*), "Stay healthy and come back."
> - Promise to stay: **기다릴게요** (*gidarilgeyo*), "I'll wait."
> - Check the return: **전역 날짜 나왔어요?** (*jeonyeok naljja nawasseoyo?*), "Has the discharge date come out?"
> - Celebrate the return: **전역 축하해요** (*jeonyeok chukhahaeyo*), "Congratulations on finishing service."

## How do you join an enlistment thread without saying the wrong thing?

The thread moves in one direction — question, goodbye, waiting, return — and each stage has its own line.

1. **입대 언제예요?** when the notice is up but the timeline is still a screenshot nobody has translated.
2. **건강하게 다녀오세요** on the day itself, which is the sentence Korean fans write on banners and in letters.
3. **기다릴게요** in the quiet week after, when the group chat goes still and someone needs to say something.
4. **전역 날짜 나왔어요?** months later, because the return date circulates in fan threads long before any official post.
5. **전역 축하해요** on the morning he is out, and not one day earlier.

| Moment | Say this |
|---|---|
| Ask the date | 입대 언제예요? (*ipdae eonjeyeyo?*) |
| Day of departure | 건강하게 다녀오세요 (*geonganghage danyeooseyo*) |
| The long wait | 기다릴게요 (*gidarilgeyo*) |
| Check the return | 전역 날짜 나왔어요? (*jeonyeok naljja nawasseoyo?*) |
| Discharge day | 전역 축하해요 (*jeonyeok chukhahaeyo*) |

## What do Korean speakers say back?

Replies here are shorter than in comeback threads, and softer.

You: **입대 언제예요?** — *ipdae eonjeyeyo?* — "When is the enlistment?"  
Them: **아직 안 나왔어요** — *ajik an nawasseoyo* — "It hasn't come out yet."  
You: **기다릴게요** — *gidarilgeyo* — "I'll wait."  
Them: **저도 기다리고 있어요** — *jeodo gidarigo isseoyo* — "I'm waiting too."  
You: **전역 날짜 나왔어요?** — *jeonyeok naljja nawasseoyo?* — "Has the discharge date come out?"  
Them: **네, 곧 나와요** — *ne, got nawayo* — "Yes, he's out soon."

**고생 많았어요** (*gosaeng manasseoyo*), "You worked hard," is the other thing fans write on discharge day, and it carries more warmth than a plain congratulations. For the vocabulary fans use the moment he is back on a stage, see [Korean phrases for a K-pop concert](/korean-phrases-kpop-concert).

## What usually goes wrong in enlistment week?

The common slip is putting **축하해요** (*chukhahaeyo*), "congratulations," on the enlistment post. In Korean it reads as celebrating the departure, which is not what anyone means. Save the congratulations for **전역**, and use **건강하게 다녀오세요** for the going-away side.

The other one is treating a fan thread like a news desk. Dates move, and repeating an unconfirmed one in Korean sounds more certain than you intended — **전역 날짜 나왔어요?** asks, which is the safer shape.
