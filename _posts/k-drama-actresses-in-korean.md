The Korean word for an actress is **배우** (*baeu*) — and it is the exact same word Koreans use for an actor. There is a separate word, **여배우** (*yeobaeu*, "female actor"), but in everyday talk, interviews, and fan comments, a K-drama actress is almost always just 배우. So if you want to say "She is my favorite K-drama actress," the natural Korean is **제일 좋아하는 배우예요** (*jeil joahaneun baeu-yeyo*). From there, Korean fans have their own vocabulary for talking about acting: who plays the lead, who steals the scene, which role became the "role of a lifetime," and whether two leads have chemistry. Below is each of those words with a sentence you can actually say.

> - **배우** (*baeu*) — actor or actress. The default word.
> - **여배우** (*yeobaeu*) — actress, when gender matters to the sentence.
> - **주연** / **주인공** (*juyeon* / *juingong*) — lead role / main character.
> - **조연** (*joyeon*) — supporting role.
> - **연기** (*yeongi*) — acting. 연기 잘해요 = "She acts well."
> - **인생캐** (*insaengkae*) — a "life character," the role an actor will be remembered for.
> - **케미** (*kemi*) — chemistry between two actors.
> - **발연기** (*baryeongi*) — "foot acting," i.e. bad acting. Handle with care.

## 배우 vs 여배우: which word to use

Korean nouns do not change for gender, and 배우 follows that rule. A news article listing a drama's cast writes **배우 ○○○**, a talk show host introduces a guest as **배우 ○○○ 씨**, and fans write **이 배우 연기 미쳤다** — "this actor's acting is insane" — about women and men alike.

**여배우** exists and is not rude, but it shows up when the sentence is specifically about gender: an awards category, a headline about "actresses in their 30s," or a conversation comparing female leads. If you use 여배우 every time you mention a woman, it sounds a little like saying "female doctor" in English when gender has nothing to do with the point.

A safe habit: say **배우** by default, and add the person's name. Korean speakers almost never say "the actress" on its own — they say **그 배우** (*geu baeu*, "that actor") or the name plus **배우님** (*baeunim*) when they want to sound respectful, the way fans do in comments.

## Talking about roles: 주연, 조연, and 주인공

When fans list who is in a drama, three words come up again and again.

**주연** (*juyeon*) is the lead role. **여주** (*yeoju*) and **남주** (*namju*) are the shortened forms of 여자 주인공 and 남자 주인공 — "female lead" and "male lead" — and they are everywhere in fan talk. **여주 진짜 예쁘다** means "The female lead is really pretty," and **여주 연기 좋다** means "The female lead's acting is good."

**조연** (*joyeon*) is a supporting role. Korean dramas are famous for supporting actors who carry whole subplots, so you will often see **조연들이 다 살렸어** — "the supporting cast saved the whole thing."

**주인공** (*juingong*) is the main character inside the story, not the casting term. You use it when talking about the plot: **주인공이 너무 답답해** — "the main character is so frustrating."

Two more words help when a show is announced: **캐스팅** (*kaeseuting*, casting) and **출연** (*churyeon*, appearing in). **이 배우가 출연해요?** asks "Is this actor in it?"

## How Korean fans praise acting

The basic compliment is **연기 잘해요** (*yeongi jalhaeyo*), "She acts well." With friends, drop to **연기 잘한다** (*yeongi jalhanda*). Everything else is a stronger version of this.

**연기 미쳤다** (*yeongi michyeotda*) — "the acting is insane." In fan Korean, 미쳤다 is high praise, not an insult.

**눈빛 연기** (*nunbit yeongi*) — "acting with the eyes." Korean viewers single this out more than English-speaking fans do, especially in quiet scenes with no dialogue. **눈빛 연기가 대박이에요** means "Her eye acting is amazing."

**인생캐** (*insaengkae*) — short for 인생 캐릭터, "life character." It is the role that fits an actor so perfectly it defines their career. **이게 그 배우 인생캐야** means "This is that actor's role of a lifetime."

**인생작** (*insaengjak*) — "life work," a drama that you personally consider the best you have ever seen. **제 인생작이에요** means "It's the drama of my life."

**케미** (*kemi*) — chemistry, from the English word. **두 배우 케미 좋다** is "The two actors have great chemistry." Fans also say **케미 장인** (*kemi jangin*, "chemistry master") for an actor who seems to have chemistry with every co-star.

**연기 천재** (*yeongi cheonjae*) — "acting genius," often used for child actors and young leads.

## The words to handle carefully

**발연기** (*baryeongi*) literally means "acting with your feet" — bad acting. It is a real, common word, and you will read it in comment sections. Saying it to a Korean friend about an actor they love can sting, and writing it on an actor's own post is simply rude. If you did not enjoy a performance, **저랑은 좀 안 맞았어요** (*jeorang-eun jom an majasseoyo*, "It didn't really suit me") is the soft, normal way to say so.

**연기력 논란** (*yeongiryeok nollan*) — "controversy over acting skill." This is headline language. It is useful to recognize, but not something you say casually.

**얼굴 천재** (*eolgul cheonjae*, "face genius") is a compliment about looks, not acting. Using it when someone asks about a performance can sound like you are dodging the question.

## A conversation about a K-drama actress

> **A:** 요즘 뭐 봐요? (*yojeum mwo bwayo?*) — What are you watching these days?
> **B:** 새 드라마 정주행 중이에요. 여주가 진짜 연기 잘해요. — I'm binge-watching a new drama. The female lead acts really well.
> **A:** 아, 그 배우요? 이전 작품도 좋았어요. — Oh, that actor? Her last show was good too.
> **B:** 맞아요. 근데 이번이 인생캐 같아요. 눈빛 연기가 대박이에요. — Right. But I think this one's her role of a lifetime. Her eye acting is amazing.
> **A:** 남주랑 케미는 어때요? — How's her chemistry with the male lead?
> **B:** 케미 미쳤어요. 꼭 보세요! — The chemistry is insane. You have to watch it!

Notice that B never says 여배우. "That actor" is **그 배우**, and the role is **여주**. That is how the conversation sounds in real life.

## Four mistakes learners make

**Saying 여배우 every time.** It is not wrong, but it marks you as translating from English. Default to 배우.

**Pronouncing 배우 like "bay-woo."** It is two short, even beats: *bae-u*, with the 배 closer to the "e" in "bed." The ㅂ is soft, almost between "b" and "p."

**Mixing up 연기 and 연극.** 연기 is acting; **연극** (*yeongeuk*) is a stage play. **연극 배우** is a theater actor.

**Using 인생캐 for any role you liked.** It means the defining role of a career. For a performance you simply enjoyed, **이번 역할 잘 어울려요** — "this role suits her" — fits better.
