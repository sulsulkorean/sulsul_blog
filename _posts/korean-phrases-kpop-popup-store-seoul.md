**K-pop pop-up stores** in Seoul's shopping districts ran back-to-back through August 2026 as groups tied limited merch drops to summer comebacks — wristband checks, sold-out lightstick pouches, and a queue that wraps the block before doors open. **줄 서야 해요?** (*jul seoya haeyo?*), "Do I need to line up?" is what you ask the staff member at the rope, and **이거 있어요?** (*igeo isseoyo?*), "Do you have this?" works when you point at a display card instead of guessing sizes in English.

> - Ask about the queue: **줄 서야 해요?** (*jul seoya haeyo?*), "Do I need to line up?"
> - Check stock: **이거 있어요?** (*igeo isseoyo?*), "Do you have this?"
> - Ask the price location: **가격 어디에 있어요?** (*gagyeok eodie isseoyo?*), "Where is the price?"
> - Request a bag: **봉투 주세요** (*bongtu juseyo*), "A bag, please."
> - Thank staff: **감사합니다** (*gamsahamnida*), "Thank you."

## How do you talk your way through a pop-up line in Myeongdong?

Pop-up crowds mix English artist names with Korean at the rope — a few short lines signal you are following staff directions, not just copying the person in front of you.

1. At the entrance rope, ask **줄 서야 해요?** so you know whether to join the back or wait for a number ticket.
2. Inside, point at the item and say **이거 있어요?** instead of reading every size label.
3. If tags are hidden, ask **가격 어디에 있어요?** — staff point faster than you can scroll a translation app.
4. At checkout, add **봉투 주세요** when you need a bag for posters.
5. Step aside with **감사합니다** before the next fan reaches the counter.

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Entrance queue | 줄 서야 해요? (*jul seoya haeyo?*) |
| Check an item | 이거 있어요? (*igeo isseoyo?*) |
| Find the price | 가격 어디에 있어요? (*gagyeok eodie isseoyo?*) |
| At checkout | 봉투 주세요 (*bongtu juseyo*) |
| After paying | 감사합니다 (*gamsahamnida*) |

## What does pop-up staff say when you reach the front?

Replies stay practical because the line behind you is already shuffling forward.

You: **줄 서야 해요?** — *jul seoya haeyo?* — "Do I need to line up?"  
Them: **네, 저쪽 끝에서 기다리세요.** — *ne, jeojjok kkeuteseo gidariseyo.* — "Yes, please wait at the far end."  
You: **이거 있어요?** — *igeo isseoyo?* — "Do you have this?"

If an item is sold out, **다음에 또 열어요?** (*daeume tto yeoreoyo?*), "Will you open again later?" is a safe follow-up — staff often know the restock window.

## What usually goes wrong at a K-pop pop-up store?

Learners memorize fan-chant English from concert clips but never practice the short stock question staff answer fifty times an hour.

A second mistake is assuming every clerk speaks English — one short stock question and **봉투 주세요** (*bongtu juseyo*) are enough to finish a purchase without switching languages mid-counter.
