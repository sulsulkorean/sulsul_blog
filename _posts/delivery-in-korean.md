"Delivery" in Korean is three different words, and Koreans pick one without thinking. **배달** (*baedal*) is food or drinks brought to you by a rider — fried chicken, tteokbokki, iced coffee. **택배** (*taekbae*) is a package carried by a courier company. **배송** (*baesong*) is the shipping of something you bought, the word you see on a shopping app's tracking screen. If you only learn one, learn 배달: it is the one you will say out loud most, as in **치킨 배달시킬까요?** — "Shall we order chicken for delivery?"

> - **배달** (*baedal*) — delivery of food, by a rider
> - **택배** (*taekbae*) — courier parcel, the package itself
> - **배송** (*baesong*) — shipping of an order (online shopping)
> - **배달시키다** (*baedalsikida*) — to order something for delivery
> - **배달비** (*baedalbi*) — delivery fee
> - **배송 조회** (*baesong johoe*) — order tracking
> - **문 앞에 두고 벨 눌러 주세요** — Please leave it at the door and ring the bell

The good news: in real life, the situation usually picks the word for you. Food is 배달. A box with a courier logo is 택배. A status line on an app is 배송. The rest of this post shows where each one lives and what to say around it.

## 배달 vs 택배 vs 배송 at a glance

| Word | Pronunciation | What arrives | Who brings it | Where you see or hear it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 배달 | *baedal* | food, drinks, groceries from a nearby shop | a motorbike rider | ordering apps, phone calls from the rider |
| 택배 | *taekbae* | a box or envelope | a courier company driver | your door, apartment lobby, courier text messages |
| 배송 | *baesong* | anything you bought online | whoever ships it | shopping apps, "배송 중", "배송 완료" |

A simple way to remember it: **배달** is about *distance* — it comes from down the street, today, while it is still hot. **택배** is about the *box* — it went through a courier warehouse. **배송** is about the *process* — the word a company uses to describe moving your order.

That is why one online order can use two of them. The app says **배송 중** (*baesong jung*, "in transit"), and the text from the driver says **택배 문 앞에 두었습니다** — "I left the parcel at your door."

## 배달: food at your door

Korea runs on 배달. Riders bring chicken to riverside parks, coffee to offices, and late-night snacks to apartments long after most kitchens elsewhere have closed. The verb you need is **배달시키다**, "to have something delivered," or the shorter everyday form **시켜 먹다**, "to order in and eat."

**치킨 배달시켜 먹을까요?** (*chikin baedalsikyeo meogeulkkayo?*) — "Shall we order chicken?"
**오늘은 그냥 시켜 먹어요.** — "Let's just order in today."
**여기 배달 돼요?** (*yeogi baedal dwaeyo?*) — "Do you deliver here?" (useful when you call a shop)

**(V)을까요?** is the textbook "Shall we…?" pattern, and it is how Koreans actually suggest ordering in. Nobody says "Let us make a delivery order." They say 시켜 먹을까요?

On the app, three words matter before you pay:

- **배달비** (*baedalbi*) — the delivery fee. Koreans compare this before anything else.
- **최소 주문 금액** (*choeso jumun geumaek*) — the minimum order amount.
- **요청 사항** (*yocheong sahang*) — the note to the shop or rider.

The most common note, word for word: **문 앞에 두고 벨 눌러 주세요** — "Leave it at the door and ring the bell." If you would rather get a text, write **문 앞에 두고 문자 주세요**. ## 택배: the package and the driver

택배 is both the service and the package. You say **택배 왔어요** (*taekbae wasseoyo*) — "A package came" — the same way English speakers say "the mail came." The person is the **택배 기사님** (*taekbae gisanim*), the courier driver; the 님 makes it polite, and that is exactly how Koreans refer to them.

Lines you will use:

**택배 보내고 싶어요.** — "I want to send a package." (at a post office or convenience store)
**택배 아직 안 왔어요.** — "My package hasn't come yet."
**경비실에 맡겨 주세요.** (*gyeongbisire matgyeo juseyo*) — "Please leave it with the security office."

If a driver calls you, the conversation is short. They usually say **택배입니다, 집에 계세요?** — "Courier here, are you home?" You answer **아니요, 문 앞에 두고 가 주세요** — "No, please leave it at the door." The detailed version of that phone call, with the questions drivers ask, is in [parcel delivery phrases](/korean-phrases-parcel-delivery-korea).

## 배송: the word on the tracking screen

You will read 배송 far more than you say it. Shopping apps show your order moving through these steps:

| Status | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 결제 완료 | *gyeolje wallyo* | payment complete |
| 배송 준비 중 | *baesong junbi jung* | preparing for shipping |
| 배송 중 | *baesong jung* | in transit |
| 배송 완료 | *baesong wallyo* | delivered |
| 무료 배송 | *muryo baesong* | free shipping |
| 새벽 배송 | *saebyeok baesong* | early-morning delivery (before you wake up) |

The button you want is **배송 조회** — "track my order." When you talk about it, though, Koreans slide back to everyday words: **언제 와요?** — "When is it coming?" — or **벌써 도착했어요!** — "It already arrived!" That second one uses the textbook pattern **벌써 (V)았어요**, "already did," and it is the happiest sentence in Korean online shopping.

## A real exchange: ordering in with a Korean friend

You are at a friend's place on a rainy evening.

**Friend:** 비 와서 나가기 싫다. 시켜 먹을까?
*It's raining, I don't want to go out. Shall we order in?*

**You:** 좋아요! 뭐 시켜요? 치킨?
*Sure! What should we get? Chicken?*

**Friend:** 응. 근데 배달비가 좀 비싸네.
*Yeah. But the delivery fee is kind of high.*

**You:** 그럼 떡볶이도 같이 시켜요. 최소 주문 금액 넘게요.
*Then let's add tteokbokki too. So we're over the minimum order.*

**Friend:** 오, 좋은 생각. 요청 사항에 "문 앞에 두고 벨 눌러 주세요" 쓸게.
*Oh, good idea. I'll write "leave it at the door and ring the bell" in the note.*

**You:** 아, 그리고 오늘 제 택배도 온대요. 배송 완료 뜨면 알려 주세요.
*Oh, and my package is coming today too. Tell me when it says "delivered."*

All three words in one evening, each doing its own job.

## Mix-ups that confuse people

**Calling food 택배.** If you say **치킨 택배 시켰어요**, Koreans will understand, but it sounds like your chicken was shipped in a cardboard box from another city. Food is always 배달.

**Using 배달 for online shopping.** **옷 배달 왔어요** is not wrong, but most people say **옷 택배 왔어요**. Clothes, books, and cosmetics travel as 택배.

**Saying 배송 in conversation.** "제 배송이 왔어요" sounds like reading a tracking screen aloud. In speech, the thing that arrives is the 택배; 배송 is the process.

**Forgetting 시키다.** **배달해요** means *you* are the one delivering. If you want to order delivery, say **배달시켜요** or **시켜 먹어요**.

**Mishearing 택배.** It sounds like *taek-bae*, not *tack-bay*. The first syllable is short and clipped. Say it fast, as one word.

## Practice the lines out loud

1. **치킨 배달시켜 먹을까요?** — suggesting delivery.
2. **문 앞에 두고 벨 눌러 주세요.** — the delivery note.
3. **택배 아직 안 왔어요.** — when something is late.
4. **벌써 도착했어요!** — when it comes early.
