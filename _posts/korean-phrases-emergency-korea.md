In a real emergency in Korea, you do not have time to look up phrases. **도와주세요!** (*dowajuseyo!*), "Please help me!", is the one shout that stops people on any Korean street. Once someone stops, **119 불러 주세요** (*119 bulleo juseyo*), "Please call 119," gets the fire and ambulance service on the line in seconds. These two phrases alone cover the most critical moments before help arrives.

> - Flag for help: **도와주세요!** (*dowajuseyo!*), "Please help me!"
> - Call ambulance / fire: **119 불러 주세요** (*119 bulleo juseyo*), "Please call 119"
> - Call police: **112 불러 주세요** (*112 bulleo juseyo*), "Please call 112"
> - Express urgency: **빨리요!** (*ppalliyo!*), "Hurry!"
> - Report an incident: **사고났어요** (*sagonnasseoyo*), "There's been an accident"

## What are the emergency numbers in Korea?

Korea uses two main numbers rather than one. Knowing both takes ten seconds and removes the risk of dialing the wrong service when your hands are shaking.

| Service | What to say |
|---|---|
| Ambulance / Fire — 119 | 119 불러 주세요 (*119 bulleo juseyo*) |
| Police — 112 | 112 불러 주세요 (*112 bulleo juseyo*) |
| Tourist helpline — 1330 | English operators available around the clock |
| Emergency room | 응급실 어디예요? (*eunggeupsil eodiyeyo?*) |

If you call **1330**, the operator speaks English and can transfer your call or tell a nearby Korean speaker exactly what you need.

## How do you get help from bystanders?

One shout cuts through street noise. **도와주세요!** stops every adult within earshot and draws people to you immediately. Then name the situation in two or three words:

**사고났어요.** — *sagonnasseoyo.* — "There's been an accident."

**아파요.** — *apayo.* — "I'm in pain."

**불이 났어요.** — *buri nasseoyo.* — "There's a fire."

Point clearly. Koreans generally respond fast to visible distress — you do not need to explain everything.

You: **도와주세요! 사고났어요.** — *dowajuseyo! sagonnasseoyo.* — "Please help! There's been an accident."  
Them: **어디서요?** — *eodiseoyo?* — "Where?"  
You: **저기요. 119로 전화해 주세요!** — *jeogiyo. 119ro jeonhwahae juseyo!* — "Over there. Please call 119!"

## What do you say when talking to a dispatcher or medical staff?

The dispatcher will ask questions quickly. Your location matters most. Say it first:

**저 지금 [장소]에 있어요.** — *jeo jigeum [place name]e isseoyo.* — "I am at [place name] right now."

Swap in your hotel name, the nearest landmark, or the street name. If you cannot say it, show your phone screen — Google Maps in Korean is readable by any dispatcher.

If someone else is injured, add one clear line:

**사람이 쓰러졌어요.** — *sarami sseurojyeosseoyo.* — "Someone has collapsed."

These phrases give emergency services exactly what they need before you say anything else.
