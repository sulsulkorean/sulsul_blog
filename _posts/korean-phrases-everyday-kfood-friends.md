The Korea Times reported in August 2026 that international visitors are moving beyond Korean barbecue and tteokbokki — they now want banana milk coffee mixes, Buldak noodle hacks, and the nostalgic snacks Koreans grew up with. The shift matters for language learners because the conversation around everyday food moves fast, in Korean, and the phrases are completely different from the ones you use to order at a restaurant. Here is what you actually need to join that chat.

> - **이거 한번 먹어봐요** (*igeo hanbeon meogeo bwayo*) — "Try this once."
> - **어떤 맛이에요?** (*eotteon masieyo?*) — "What does it taste like?"
> - **인기 많아요** (*ingi manayo*) — "It is very popular."
> - **진짜 맛있어요** (*jinjja massisseoyo*) — "It is really delicious."

## How do you recommend a Korean food to a friend?

The simplest opener is a direct push: **이거 한번 먹어봐요** (*igeo hanbeon meogeo bwayo*), "Try this once," while pointing at the snack or bowl. If they look hesitant, add **맵지 않아요** (*maepji anayo*), "It is not spicy," or **달아요** (*darayo*), "It is sweet," so they know what to expect.

You: **이거 한번 먹어봐요.** — *igeo hanbeon meogeo bwayo.* — "Try this once."  
Them: **어떤 맛이에요?** — *eotteon masieyo?* — "What does it taste like?"  
You: **고소하고 달아요.** — *gosohago darayo.* — "It is nutty and sweet."

| Flavor description | Say this |
|---|---|
| Spicy | 매워요 (*maeweoyo*) |
| Sweet | 달아요 (*darayo*) |
| Savory / nutty | 고소해요 (*gosohaeyo*) |
| Not spicy | 맵지 않아요 (*maepji anayo*) |

## How do you ask where someone ate or found something?

When a Korean friend mentions a dish you have never tried, the natural follow-up is location. **어디서 먹었어요?** (*eodiseo meogeosseoyo?*), "Where did you eat this?", works for restaurants, convenience stores, and market stalls equally. If they describe a convenience store hack — like the banana milk and hazelnut coffee mix — say **어떻게 만들어요?** (*eotteoke mandeureoyo?*), "How do you make it?"

You: **이게 뭐예요?** — *ige mwoyeyo?* — "What is this?"  
Them: **바나나 우유에 헤이즐넛 커피 섞은 거예요.** — *banana uyue heijeulleot keopi seokkeun geoyeyo.* — "It is banana milk mixed with hazelnut coffee."  
You: **진짜요? 저도 해볼게요.** — *jinjjayo? jeodo haebolgeyeo.* — "Really? I will try it too."

## How do you say a food is worth trying?

**인기 많아요** (*ingi manayo*), "It is very popular," is the social proof phrase Koreans use most. For a personal stamp: **강추예요** (*ganchu yeyeyo*), "I strongly recommend it," — short for 강력 추천 (*gangnyeok chucheon*). If something is already a known hit, **유명해요** (*yumyeonghaeyo*), "It is famous," signals that the food has cultural weight, not just personal taste.

One phrase to avoid mixing up: **별로예요** (*byeollo yeyeyo*) means "it is not great" — a polite way to say you were not impressed. Use it carefully; Koreans read it as a real opinion, not a soft pass.
