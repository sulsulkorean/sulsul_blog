August fan-sign season means **K-pop fan meeting lottery results** land in group chats while the official email is still loading — and the Korean fans type still works even if you read the notice in English. **당첨됐어요?** (*dangcheomdwaesseoyo?*), "Did you win?" opens the thread when someone posts a screenshot, and **아쉽지만 다음에요** (*aswipjiman daeumeyo*), "Too bad, but next time," is what you send when the list shows a friend's name missing.

> - Ask about the result: **당첨됐어요?** (*dangcheomdwaesseoyo?*), "Did you win?"
> - Celebrate a win: **축하해요** (*chukahaeyo*), "Congratulations."
> - Console a miss: **아쉽지만 다음에요** (*aswipejiman daeumeyo*), "Too bad, but next time."
> - Share the hype: **꼭 가고 싶어요** (*kkok gago sipeoyo*), "I really want to go."
> - Plan together: **같이 가요** (*gati gayo*), "Let us go together."

## How do you react when fan meeting lottery results start scrolling?

Fan communities mix English artist names with Korean reactions — a few Korean lines signal you are in the room, not just refreshing your inbox alone.

1. Drop the screenshot and ask **당첨됐어요?** before anyone spoils the full list.
2. When a friend wins, send **축하해요** — short praise beats a long English essay.
3. If your name is missing, type **아쉽지만 다음에요** so the chat stays supportive.
4. Add **꼭 가고 싶어요** when you are already planning the next application round.
5. Close with **같이 가요** when you and a friend both made the list.

|---|---|
| Open the chat | 당첨됐어요? (*dangcheomdwaesseoyo?*) |

## What do Korean speakers say back?

Replies in lottery-result threads stay loud and short.

You: **당첨됐어요?** — *dangcheomdwaesseoyo?* — "Did you win?"  
Them: **응, 됐어!** — *eung, dwaesseo!* — "Yeah, I got in!"  
You: **축하해요!** — *chukahaeyo!* — "Congratulations!"
