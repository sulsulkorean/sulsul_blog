"Exchange" in Korean depends on what you are exchanging. **교환** (*gyohwan*) is swapping a product for another one — a shirt in the wrong size, a cracked phone case. **환전** (*hwanjeon*) is changing money from one currency to another. And in everyday speech, most Koreans skip both nouns and use the verb **바꾸다** (*bakkuda*, to change), as in **바꿔 주세요** — "Please switch it for me." If you only remember one line, make it that one: it works at a clothing shop, a cafe counter, and a money exchange booth.

> - **교환** (*gyohwan*) — exchange of a product (store, online order)
> - **환전** (*hwanjeon*) — currency exchange
> - **환전소** (*hwanjeonso*) — money exchange booth
> - **바꾸다** (*bakkuda*) — to change, to switch
> - **바꿔 주세요** (*bakkwo juseyo*) — Please change it (for me)
> - **바꿔도 돼요?** (*bakkwodo dwaeyo?*) — Can I change it?
> - **환불** (*hwanbul*) — refund

So you have two nouns for two very different places, and one verb that quietly covers both. The sections below take them one at a time.

## Which word for which exchange

| You want to... | Noun | What you actually say |
|---|---|---|
| Swap a product for a different size or color | 교환 | 다른 사이즈로 바꿔 주세요 |
| Change dollars into won | 환전 | 달러를 원으로 바꿔 주세요 |
| Return it and get a refund | 환불 | 환불돼요? |
| Swap one seat, cup, or plate for another | (no noun) | 이거 바꿔 주세요 |
| Practice Korean with a partner | 언어 교환 | 언어 교환 하실래요? |

Notice the last column. Even when the "official" word is 교환 or 환전, the sentence people say out loud usually leans on 바꿔.

## 교환: swapping something you bought

교환 is the word printed on receipts, store signs, and online shop policies: **교환 및 환불** ("exchanges and refunds"), **교환 가능** ("exchange possible"), **교환 불가** ("no exchanges"). You will read it more often than you hear it.

At the counter, the clearest request is **교환하고 싶어요** — "I'd like to exchange this." Hand over the item and the receipt (**영수증**, *yeongsujeung*) together. Then say what you want instead:

- **사이즈가 안 맞아요.** — The size doesn't fit.
- **한 사이즈 큰 걸로 바꿔 주세요.** — Please change it to one size bigger.
- **다른 색으로 바꿔도 돼요?** — Can I change it to a different color?

That last line uses the SULSUL textbook's permission pattern **(V)아/어도 돼요?** — "Is it okay if I...?" It is softer than a direct request, which helps when the item is already opened.

Staff will often ask **영수증 있으세요?** ("Do you have the receipt?") and **언제 구매하셨어요?** ("When did you buy it?"). A short answer is enough: **네, 여기요** or **어제요** ("Yesterday").

## 환전: changing money

환전 is only about currency. You will see **환전** on bank windows, airport counters, and booths around Myeongdong, often next to **환율** (*hwanyul*, exchange rate). The booth itself is a **환전소**.

The polite, natural request is **환전하고 싶어요** ("I'd like to exchange money") followed by what you have and what you want:

- **달러를 원으로 바꿔 주세요.** — Please change dollars into won.
- **엔화 환전돼요?** — Can you exchange yen?
- **오늘 환율이 얼마예요?** — What's today's rate?
- **작은 돈도 섞어 주세요.** — Please include some small bills.

The particle **(으)로** does the heavy lifting here. It marks what something turns *into*: 원으로 ("into won"), 큰 걸로 ("into a bigger one"). Get that one particle right and your sentence is clear even if the rest is rough.

## 바꿔 주세요: the everyday verb

Outside shops and banks, nobody says 교환 for small swaps. If your coffee comes hot instead of iced, you don't announce an "exchange." You say **아이스로 바꿔 주세요** — "Please change it to iced." This is Pattern 23 from the SULSUL textbook, **(V)아/어 주세요**: take a verb, add 아/어, then 주세요, and you have a polite request.

A few you can use right away:

- **자리 바꿔 주실 수 있어요?** — Could you switch seats with me?
- **이 접시 바꿔 주세요.** — Please change this plate (it's dirty or chipped).
- **현금으로 바꿔 주세요.** — Please change it to cash (payment).
- **잔돈으로 바꿔 주세요.** — Please break this into small change.

## A real exchange at a clothing shop

> **You:** 저기요, 이거 교환하고 싶어요.
> *Jeogiyo, igeo gyohwanhago sipeoyo.* — Excuse me, I'd like to exchange this.
>
> **Staff:** 네, 영수증 있으세요?
> *Ne, yeongsujeung isseuseyo?* — Sure, do you have the receipt?
>
> **You:** 네, 여기요. 사이즈가 좀 작아요.
> *Ne, yeogiyo. Saijeuga jom jagayo.* — Yes, here. The size is a bit small.
>
> **Staff:** 어떤 사이즈로 드릴까요?
> *Eotteon saijeuro deurilkkayo?* — Which size would you like?
>
> **You:** 한 사이즈 큰 걸로 바꿔 주세요.
> *Han saijeu keun geollo bakkwo juseyo.* — One size up, please.
>
> **Staff:** 잠시만요. 여기 있습니다.
> *Jamsimanyo. Yeogi itseumnida.* — Just a moment. Here you go.

Two lines did all the work: 교환하고 싶어요 to open, 바꿔 주세요 to close.

## Mix-ups that confuse people

**Using 교환 for money.** "돈 교환해 주세요" sounds like you want to trade cash with someone. For currency, the word is 환전.

**Asking for 교환 when you want a refund.** 교환 means you leave with a different item. If you want to return it for a refund, the word is **환불** — **환불돼요?** ("Can I get a refund?").

**Dropping (으)로.** "아이스 바꿔 주세요" is understandable, but "아이스로 바꿔 주세요" is what a Korean would say. The 로 tells the listener what you want it changed *into*.

**Mispronouncing 바꿔.** The ㄲ is a tense sound, not a soft *g*. Say *ba-kkwo* with a little catch before the second syllable, as if you were about to say "kw" quickly.

## Practice the lines out loud

1. **이거 교환하고 싶어요.** — opening an exchange at a shop.
2. **다른 색으로 바꿔도 돼요?** — asking politely.
3. **달러를 원으로 바꿔 주세요.** — at the exchange booth.
4. **아이스로 바꿔 주세요.** — fixing a cafe order.
