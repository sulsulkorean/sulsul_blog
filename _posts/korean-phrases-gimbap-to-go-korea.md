Gimbap travels better than almost anything else in Korea, which is why it ends up in hiking bags, on trains, and in a foil wrapper eaten standing up. It is also counted in a unit most learners have never used: **줄** (*jul*), a roll. **김밥 한 줄 주세요** (*gimbap han jul juseyo*), "One roll of gimbap, please," is the whole order at a small shop, and **포장이요** (*pojangiyo*), "To go," is the word that decides whether it comes on a plate or in a bag.

> - Order one: **김밥 한 줄 주세요** (*gimbap han jul juseyo*), "One roll of gimbap, please."
> - Take it away: **포장이요** (*pojangiyo*), "To go."
> - Stay and eat: **여기서 먹을게요** (*yeogiseo meogeulgeyo*), "I'll eat here."
> - Leave something out: **오이 빼 주세요** (*oi ppae juseyo*), "Please leave out the cucumber."
> - Check the wait: **얼마나 걸려요?** (*eolmana geollyeoyo?*), "How long will it take?"

## How do you order gimbap in ten seconds?

Gimbap shops are built for speed — the rolls are cut while you stand there, and the questions come at you in short bursts.

1. **김밥 한 줄 주세요** to open, since the counter is where you say both the item and the number.
2. **포장이요** or **여기서 먹을게요** immediately after, because it changes what the staff reach for.
3. **오이 빼 주세요** if something inside is a problem for you — cucumber, carrot, and pickled radish are the usual swaps.
4. **얼마나 걸려요?** when there is a queue and you are deciding whether to wait or walk.
5. Say **감사합니다** (*gamsahamnida*) at the bag, which is all the closing anyone expects.

| Moment | Say this |
|---|---|
| Order one roll | 김밥 한 줄 주세요 (*gimbap han jul juseyo*) |
| Takeout | 포장이요 (*pojangiyo*) |
| Eat in | 여기서 먹을게요 (*yeogiseo meogeulgeyo*) |
| Leave an ingredient out | 오이 빼 주세요 (*oi ppae juseyo*) |
| Check the wait | 얼마나 걸려요? (*eolmana geollyeoyo?*) |

## What do Korean speakers say back?

Staff answer in two or three words and hand you a question back, usually about which kind.

You: **김밥 한 줄 주세요** — *gimbap han jul juseyo* — "One roll of gimbap, please."  
Them: **어떤 걸로 드릴까요? 참치김밥도 있어요** — *eotteon geollo deurilkkayo? chamchigimbapdo isseoyo* — "Which one? We have tuna gimbap too."  
You: **포장이요** — *pojangiyo* — "To go."  
Them: **금방 돼요** — *geumbang dwaeyo* — "It'll be ready in a moment."  
You: **얼마나 걸려요?** — *eolmana geollyeoyo?* — "How long will it take?"  
Them: **따로 담아 드릴까요?** — *ttaro dama deurilkkayo?* — "Shall I pack them separately?"

If you want more than one, the counter changes: **두 줄** (*du jul*), two rolls, **세 줄** (*se jul*), three. For the rest of the takeout vocabulary, see [Korean phrases for ordering takeout food in Korea](/korean-phrases-takeout-food-korea).

## What usually goes wrong at a gimbap counter?

The first one is the counter word. Learners ask for **김밥 하나** and it is usually understood, but **한 줄** is what the shop says back, so the number in the reply lands as noise. Hearing **몇 줄 드릴까요?** (*myeot jul deurilkkayo?*), "How many rolls?" and answering with **한 줄** keeps the exchange in one language.

The second is treating **오이 빼 주세요** as an allergy sentence. It removes one ingredient from one roll; it does not cover sesame oil, egg, or fish cake already mixed into the filling. If something is a real medical problem rather than a preference, use the wording in [Korean phrases for food allergies and diet restrictions](/korean-phrases-food-allergies-korea).
