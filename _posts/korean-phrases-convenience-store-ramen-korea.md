Korean phrases for eating cup ramen at a Korean convenience store lean on Pattern 03 from the SULSUL workbook — **있어요?** (*isseoyo?*), "Is there…?" Late-night **편의점** (*pyeonuijeom*, convenience store) ramen is a national ritual. Pay at the counter, fill the cup at the **온수기** (*onsuggi*, hot water dispenser) near the seating area, then eat at the plastic counter by the window. No Korean is strictly required — but knowing a few phrases makes the experience feel real.

> - Order ramen: **컵라면 주세요.** (*keomnaemyeon juseyo.*), "Cup ramen, please."
> - Hot water machine: **온수기 어디 있어요?** (*onsuggi eodi isseoyo?*), "Where is the hot water machine?"
> - May I eat here: **여기서 먹어도 돼요?** (*yeogiseo meogeodo dwaeyo?*), "May I eat here?"
> - Chopsticks: **젓가락 있어요?** (*jeotgarak isseoyo?*), "Do you have chopsticks?"

## How do you buy and prepare cup ramen at a Korean convenience store?

Pick up the cup from the ramen shelf — **신라면** (*Sillamen*, Shin Ramen) and **불닭볶음면** (*buldak bogeummyeon*, Fire Chicken Noodle) are the most common choices. Bring it to the register along with anything else you want. After paying, walk to the **온수기** at the back, peel the lid halfway, pour the hot water to the marked line inside the cup, and fold the lid closed. Wait three minutes.

You: **신라면 하나랑 삶은 계란 주세요.** — *Sillamen hana-rang salmeun gyeran juseyo.* — "One Shin Ramen and a boiled egg, please."  
Them: **온수기는 저기 있어요.** — *onsugineun jeogi isseoyo.* — "The hot water machine is over there."  
You: **감사해요. 여기서 먹어도 돼요?** — *gamsahaeyo. yeogiseo meogeodo dwaeyo?* — "Thank you. May I eat here?"

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Order the ramen | 컵라면 주세요. (*keomnaemyeon juseyo.*) |
| Find the hot water | 온수기 어디 있어요? (*onsuggi eodi isseoyo?*) |
| Ask to eat in-store | 여기서 먹어도 돼요? (*yeogiseo meogeodo dwaeyo?*) |
| Ask for chopsticks | 젓가락 있어요? (*jeotgarak isseoyo?*) |

## What do you say while you're waiting for the noodles to cook?

Most 편의점 seating areas have a condiment counter with **고추장** (*gochujang*, red chili paste) and kimchi packets — help yourself, they are always free.

You: **몇 분 기다려야 해요?** — *myeot bun gidaryeoya haeyo?* — "How many minutes do I need to wait?"  
Them: **3분이요. 뚜껑 꼭 눌러요.** — *sambun-iyo. ttukkkeong kkok nulleoyo.* — "Three minutes. Press the lid down tight."  
You: **아, 알겠어요. 고마워요!** — *a, algeseoyo. gomaweoyo!* — "Oh, I see. Thank you!"

The **맛있어요** (*massisseoyo*, "it's delicious") from the [Korean food phrases guide](/compliment-korean-friends-cooking) also works when a Korean stranger at the next seat comments on what you ordered.

## What usually goes wrong for travelers?

The most common mistake is using the wrong **온수기** temperature setting. Some machines have two taps — one for warm water and one for boiling. Always use the tap labeled **뜨거운 물** (*ddeugeoun mul*, hot water) or the one with a flame icon. The warm-water tap won't cook the noodles through.

A second mistake is arriving when the 편의점 is out of your chosen cup ramen. Have a backup in mind — if **신라면** is sold out, try **육개장 사발면** (*yukgaejang sabalmen*), a smoky beef version that's just as popular.
