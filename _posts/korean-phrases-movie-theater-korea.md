Korean phrases at a **영화관** (*yeonghwagwan*, movie theater) start with Pattern 03 from the SULSUL workbook — noun plus **있어요?** (*isseoyo?*), "do you have?" Ask **표 있어요?** (*pyo isseoyo?*), "Do you have tickets?" at the box office, then name the film with Pattern 02 — verb plus **고 싶어요** (*go sipeoyo*), "I want to" — **이 영화 보고 싶어요** (*i yeonghwa bogo sipeoyo*), "I want to watch this movie."

> - Ask about tickets: **표 있어요?** (*pyo isseoyo?*), "Do you have tickets?"
> - Name the film: **이 영화 보고 싶어요** (*i yeonghwa bogo sipeoyo*), "I want to watch this movie."
> - Snack counter: **팝콘 주세요** (*papkon juseyo*), "Popcorn, please."
> - Pick a showtime: **몇 시 상영이에요?** (*myeot si sangyeong-ieyo?*), "What time is the screening?"
> - Theater rules: **극장에서 전화하면 안 돼요** (*geukjang-eseo jeonhwahamyeon an dwaeyo*), "You must not use your phone in the theater."

## How do you buy tickets at a Korean cinema?

Major chains — **CGV**, **롯데시네마** (*Lotte Cinema*), **메가박스** (*Megabox*) — sell tickets at kiosks and counters. English menus appear on many screens, but staff still answer faster when you lead with Pattern 03 instead of a long English question.

You: **안녕하세요, 표 있어요?** — *annyeonghaseyo, pyo isseoyo?* — "Hello, do you have tickets?"  
Them: **몇 시요?** — *myeot siyo?* — "For what time?"  
You: **7시 상영 보고 싶어요.** — *ilgop si sangyeong bogo sipeoyo.* — "I want the 7 o'clock screening."

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Tickets available? | 표 있어요? (*pyo isseoyo?*) |
| Want this movie | 이 영화 보고 싶어요 (*i yeonghwa bogo sipeoyo*) |
| What time? | 몇 시 상영이에요? (*myeot si sangyeong-ieyo?*) |
| Popcorn, please | 팝콘 주세요 (*papkon juseyo*) |
| No phone calls | 극장에서 전화하면 안 돼요 (*geukjang-eseo jeonhwahamyeon an dwaeyo*) |

## What do you say at the snack counter?

Pattern 01 — noun plus **주세요** (*juseyo*), "please give me" — is the fastest line at **매점** (*maejeom*, concession stand). Point at the combo on the menu board and add **콜라도 주세요** (*kollado juseyo*), "A cola too, please," when you want a drink with your **팝콘** (*papkon*, popcorn).

You: **팝콘 주세요.** — *papkon juseyo.* — "Popcorn, please."  
Them: **어떤 거요?** — *eotteon geoyo?* — "Which kind?"  
You: **버터 맛이요.** — *beoteo mas-iyo.* — "Butter flavor."

For inviting a friend before you reach the lobby, Pattern 25 from the workbook — **같이 영화 볼래요?** (*gachi yeonghwa bollaeyo?*), "Shall we watch a movie together?" — is covered in [how to ask a Korean friend to hang out](/ask-korean-friend-to-hang-out). At the counter, stick to Pattern 02 and Pattern 03.

## What usually goes wrong at a Korean movie theater?

Travelers buy the wrong ticket type — **2D** versus **4DX** or **IMAX** — because the kiosk screen switched to Korean mid-flow. Say **영어 메뉴 있어요?** (*yeongeo menu isseoyo?*), "Is there an English menu?" or tap the globe icon before you pay.

A second mistake is leaving the phone on silent. Pattern 44 in the workbook covers theater etiquette: **극장에서 전화하면 안 돼요** (*geukjang-eseo jeonhwahamyeon an dwaeyo*), "You must not make phone calls in the theater." Vibrate is fine; a bright screen during a quiet scene is not.

You: **자막 영화예요?** — *jamak yeonghwayeyo?* — "Is this a subtitled screening?"  
Them: **네, 한국어 자막이에요.** — *ne, hangugeo jamak-ieyo.* — "Yes, Korean subtitles."  
You: **알겠어요, 감사합니다.** — *algesseoyo, gamsahamnida.* — "Got it, thank you."
