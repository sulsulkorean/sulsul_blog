Korean phrases for a **찜질방** (*jjimjilbang*, Korean spa) start at the front desk. Say **어디서 받아요?** (*eodiseo badayo?*), "Where do I pay?" when you walk in, then **옷 대여해요?** (*ot daeryeohaeyo?*), "Do you rent clothes?" if you need a **이벤옷** (*baenot*, spa uniform). Pattern 04 in the SULSUL workbook — place plus **어디예요?** (*eodiyeyo?*) — covers finding the locker room and sauna levels.

> - Where to pay: **어디서 받아요?** (*eodiseo badayo?*), "Where do I pay?"
> - Rent a uniform: **옷 대여해요?** (*ot daeryeohaeyo?*), "Do you rent clothes?"
> - Find lockers: **사물함 어디예요?** (*samullham eodiyeyo?*), "Where are the lockers?"
> - Ask about towels: **수건 있어요?** (*sugeon isseoyo?*), "Are there towels?"
> - Check sauna temperature: **너무 뜨거워요** (*neomu tteugeowoyo*), "It's too hot."

## How do you check in at the jimjilbang desk?

Most **찜질방** charge by time block — day pass versus overnight. Ask before you tap your card.

You: **안녕하세요. 어디서 받아요?** — *annyeonghaseyo. eodiseo badayo?* — "Hello. Where do I pay?"  
Them: **여기서 받아요.** — *yeogiseo badayo.* — "You pay here."  
You: **옷 대여해요?** — *ot daeryeohaeyo?* — "Do you rent clothes?"

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Where to pay | 어디서 받아요? (*eodiseo badayo?*) |
| Locker location | 사물함 어디예요? (*samullham eodiyeyo?*) |
| Towels available? | 수건 있어요? (*sugeon isseoyo?*) |
| Too hot | 너무 뜨거워요 (*neomu tteugeowoyo*) |
| Overnight stay | 여기서 자도 돼요? (*yeogiseo jado dwaeyo?*) |

## What do you say on the sauna floor?

Gender-separated floors use different locker colors — staff point with **남자** (*namja*, men) or **여자** (*yeoja*, women).

You: **사물함 어디예요?** — *samullham eodiyeyo?* — "Where are the lockers?"  
Them: **2층이에요.** — *icheung-ieyo.* — "It's on the second floor."  
You: **감사합니다.** — *gamsahamnida.* — "Thank you."

For asking where the restroom is once you are inside, **화장실 어디 있어요?** (*hwajangsil eodi isseoyo?*) from [asking where the restroom is](/ask-restroom-korean) works the same way on every floor.

## What usually goes wrong at a jimjilbang?

Travelers walk past the desk straight to the lockers and get sent back — **어디서 받아요?** takes five seconds and saves the awkward U-turn.

A second mistake is wearing shoes past the shoe locker line. Watch where locals swap to **슬리퍼** (*seullipeo*, slippers) and follow. For hotel check-in phrases before a spa night near your guesthouse, see [how to check into a hotel in Korean](/check-into-hotel-korean).
