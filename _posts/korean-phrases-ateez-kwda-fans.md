When KStarTrend covered the 10th K-World Dream Awards on August 28, 2026, ATEEZ had just walked offstage at KINTEX in Goyang with four trophies — including the coveted **대상** (*daesang*, Best Artist grand prize). International fans watching the live stream screamed in English. Korean fans replied with three short words: **대박! 당연하죠!** (*daebak! dangnyeonhajyo!*) — "No way! Of course they deserved it!" Knowing those words in the right moment is the difference between watching a fan community happen and actually being part of it.

> - Huge reaction: **대박!** (*daebak!*), "No way! / Amazing!"
> - They won the grand prize: **대상 받았어요!** (*daesang badasseoyo!*), "They won the daesang!"
> - Of course they deserved it: **당연하죠!** (*dangnyeonhajyo!*), "Of course!"
> - The speech was moving: **수상 소감이 감동이에요.** (*susang sogami gamdong-ieyo.*), "The acceptance speech is so moving."

## What do you say when the winner is announced?

Award show moments move fast. Korean fans have a short burst vocabulary built for those seconds: a reaction word, a verdict, then a response to whoever is next to you. Here is the loop in real time.

You: **대박! 에이티즈 대상 받았어요! 봤어요?** — *daebak! Eititeu daesang badasseoyo! bwasseoyo?* — "No way! ATEEZ won the daesang! Did you see it?"  
Them: **네! 수상 소감이 진짜 감동이었어요.** — *ne! susang sogami jinjja gamdong-ieosseoyo.* — "Yes! The acceptance speech was genuinely moving."  
You: **맞아요. 다음에 또 받을 거예요!** — *majayo. daeum-e tto badeul geoyeyo!* — "Exactly. They'll win again next time!"

| They deserved it | 당연하죠! (*dangnyeonhajyo!*) |

## How do you talk about an award show with Korean fans?

After the ceremony ends, Korean fans move to fan cafes, KakaoTalk group chats, and X (Twitter) to break it all down. The key skill is past-tense reaction: **받았어요** (*badasseoyo*, won/received), **울었어요** (*ureosseoyo*, cried), **봤어요** (*bwasseoyo*, watched). These are Pattern 09 past-tense forms you can attach to anything that just happened.

You: **무대 봤어요? 너무 멋있었어요!** — *mudae bwasseoyo? neomu meosisseosseoyo!* — "Did you see the stage? It was incredible!"  
Them: **네, 퍼포먼스 진짜 최고였어요.** — *ne, peopoemeonseu jinjja choegoyeosseoyo.* — "Yes, the performance was truly the best."  
You: **수상 소감도 너무 감동이었어요. 울었어요.** — *susang sogamdo neomu gamdong-ieosseoyo. ureosseoyo.* — "The acceptance speech was so moving too. I cried."

The **최고였어요** (*choegoyeosseoyo*, "it was the best") from the [K-pop concert phrase guide](/korean-phrases-kpop-concert) carries the same weight here — save it for moments that genuinely earned it.

## What usually trips up international fans?

The most common slip is confusing **대상** (*daesang*, grand prize) with **상** (*sang*, award). Not every trophy is a 대상. When you say **대상 받았어요** (*daesang badasseoyo*) for a popularity vote award, Korean fans will gently correct you — which is a great conversation starter.

A second mistake is staying in English when the trophy drops. Korean fans notice when an international ATINY switches languages for the big moment. One phrase — **대박!** (*daebak!*) — is all you need. It translates to "jackpot" literally but functions as pure disbelief and joy.
