**Daebak** (대박, *dae-bak*) means "awesome," "jackpot," or "no way!" in Korean. Literally it is a "huge hit": 대 means big, and 대박 was first used for a business or a bet that paid off far beyond expectations. Today it is the reaction word Koreans reach for whenever something is surprising. Good news? **대박!** A friend got concert tickets? **대박!** Your phone just died at 1%? Also **대박**, said in a very different voice. The word itself only says "this is big." Your tone tells people whether it's big-good or big-bad.

> - **대박** (*dae-bak*) — awesome / no way / wow.
> - **대박이다** (*dae-bak-i-da*) — "That's amazing." 대박이에요 is the softer polite form.
> - **헐 대박** (*heol dae-bak*) — "Whoa, no way." Double shock.
> - **대박 나다** (*dae-bak na-da*) — to become a huge hit.
> - **대박 나세요** (*dae-bak na-se-yo*) — "I hope you make it big!" A wish for success.
> - **쪽박** (*jjok-bak*) — the opposite: a total flop.

## Where 대박 comes from

The 박 part is most often traced to a gourd. In the old folk tale of Heungbu, a poor but kind brother plants a seed, and the giant gourds he grows split open full of treasure. A huge gourd, 큰 박, became shorthand for sudden fortune. Another common story links 대박 to gambling, where it meant hitting the jackpot. Either way, the core idea is the same: a win so big it surprises you.

That's why the word still lives in business talk. A film with ten million viewers **대박 났어요** (became a smash hit). A restaurant with a line around the block is a **대박집** (jackpot restaurant). And when a friend opens a shop, Koreans say **대박 나세요!** It is a kind and very common wish, like "I hope this takes off!"

The flop version is **쪽박**. A 쪽박 is a small, broken gourd bowl, the kind a beggar might hold. So 대박 or 쪽박 means "a huge win or total ruin." You'll hear it when characters talk about risky investments.

## How 대박 sounds in real conversation

Most of the time, 대박 stands alone as a one-word reaction. The meaning comes from what just happened and how you say it.

**Pure excitement:**

- **대박! 진짜 붙었어?** — "No way! You really passed?"
- **이 노래 대박이다.** — "This song is amazing."
- **오늘 날씨 대박.** — "The weather today is incredible."

**Pure shock:**

- **헐, 대박. 둘이 헤어졌대.** — "Whoa, no way. They broke up."
- **대박… 그게 말이 돼?** — "Wow… does that even make sense?"

**Bad news, said with a groan:**

- **대박, 지갑 놓고 왔어.** — "Oh no, I left my wallet."
- **비 오는데 우산 없어. 대박.** — "It's raining and I have no umbrella. Great."

In the last two, 대박 works like an English "great" said sarcastically. Your voice drops, the word drags a little, and everyone understands.

You can also use 대박 as an adjective for something you love. **대박 맛있어** means "insanely delicious," and **대박 귀여워** means "ridiculously cute." This is very casual. It's how friends talk, not how a food critic writes.

## 대박 in K-dramas and K-pop

Once you know the word, you'll catch it in almost every episode. A high school student sees her crush walk into class: **대박.** A team finds the evidence they needed: **대박, 이거야!** Variety shows put **대박** in giant captions on the screen whenever a guest pulls off something surprising.

K-pop fans use it nonstop too. When a group drops a new music video, the comments fill with **대박**, **미쳤다** (literally "it's crazy," meaning amazing), and **레전드** (legend). If you want to sound like a fan, these three cover most reactions:

- **무대 대박이었어.** — "The stage was amazing."
- **이번 앨범 대박 날 것 같아.** — "I think this album is going to be a huge hit."
- **직캠 봤어? 대박.** — "Did you see the fancam? Unreal."

Notice the second line uses 대박 나다, the "become a hit" meaning. Fans use it to predict that a song or show will blow up.

## Is 대박 polite?

대박 is slang. It's warm and friendly, but it belongs with friends, siblings, classmates, and people your age. If you say a bare **대박!** to your boss or to an older stranger, it sounds a bit too loose, the way "dude, sick!" would sound in a job interview.

Here is how to scale it:

| Who you're talking to | What to say |
|---|---|
| Close friend | **대박!** / **헐 대박** |
| Friendly coworker, same age | **대박이에요!** |
| Boss, teacher, older person | **와, 정말 대단하세요.** ("Wow, that's really impressive.") |
| Someone opening a business | **대박 나세요!** (polite and very welcome) |

The phrase **대박 나세요** is the exception. Because it's a wish, not a reaction, it's fine to say to almost anyone. You'll see it on cards and messages for new shops, new jobs, and new channels.

## Common mistakes with 대박

- **Saying "dae-back" with a big K at the end.** The final ㄱ is unreleased. Close your throat on it without letting the K pop out: *dae-bak*, almost like *dae-ba(k)*.
- **Using it at a sad moment.** If someone tells you their pet died, 대박 is wrong even in a low voice. It sounds like gossip. Say **어떡해…** ("Oh no, what do I do…") or **많이 힘들겠다** ("That must be so hard") instead.
- **Overusing it with older people.** A single 대박이에요 is fine. Five of them in one conversation will make you sound like a teenager.
- **Thinking it only means "good."** Many learners are confused when a Korean friend says 대박 at bad news. Listen to the tone, not the word.
- **Mixing up 대박 and 대단해.** 대단해 means "impressive" and praises a person or effort. 대박 reacts to a situation. **너 대단하다** ("You're amazing") is a compliment; **너 대박이다** usually means "You're unbelievable," which can be praise or teasing.

## A quick 대박 conversation

> **A:** 나 BTS 콘서트 표 구했어!
> *(I got BTS concert tickets!)*
>
> **B:** 헐, 대박! 어떻게?
> *(Whoa, no way! How?)*
>
> **A:** 취소표 떴어. 완전 운 좋았어.
> *(A canceled ticket popped up. I was so lucky.)*
>
> **B:** 대박이다. 진짜 부럽다.
> *(That's amazing. I'm so jealous.)*

Short, loud, and natural. That's exactly how 대박 lives in Korean.

## Say it out loud

Reaction words only become yours when they come out of your mouth without thinking. Try three versions of 대박 right now: one excited (high and quick), one shocked (slow, eyes wide), and one groaning (low and long). Then add a full line: **헐 대박, 진짜?**, **대박 맛있어**, **대박 나세요!** Say each one three times until it feels natural.
