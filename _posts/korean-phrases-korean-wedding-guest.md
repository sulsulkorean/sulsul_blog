Being invited to a Korean wedding is a real honor — and Korean weddings move fast, often wrapping up in under an hour at a dedicated wedding hall called a **예식장** (*yesigjang*). Two lines cover most of what a guest needs to say: **축하해요** (*chukhahaeyo*), "Congratulations," when you reach the couple, and **행복하세요** (*haengbokhaseyo*), "Be happy," as a short blessing right after.

> - Greet the couple: **축하해요** (*chukhahaeyo*), "Congratulations."
> - Add a blessing: **행복하세요** (*haengbokhaseyo*), "Be happy."
> - Compliment them: **정말 잘 어울려요** (*jeongmal jal eoullyeoyo*), "You two look great together."
> - Find the gift table: **봉투 어디에 내요?** (*bongtu eodie naeyo?*), "Where do I hand the envelope?"
> - Ask about the meal: **식사는 어디서 해요?** (*siksaneun eodiseo haeyo?*), "Where do we eat?"

## How do you greet the couple and their family?

After the ceremony, guests form a brief line near the aisle to say a few words directly to the bride and groom — and often their parents who stand nearby. Keep it to one warm sentence and a bow.

You: **축하해요. 정말 잘 어울려요.** — *chukhahaeyo. jeongmal jal eoullyeoyo.* — "Congratulations. You two look great together."  
Them: **감사해요. 잘 오셨어요.** — *gamsahaeyo. jal osyeosseoyo.* — "Thank you. We are so glad you came."  
You: **행복하세요.** — *haengbokhaseyo.* — "Be happy."

If you are greeting parents instead of the couple, 축하해요 still works perfectly. They will bow back and reply 감사합니다 (*gamsahamnida*).

| Moment | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Meet the couple | 축하해요 (*chukhahaeyo*) |
| Wish them happiness | 행복하세요 (*haengbokhaseyo*) |
| Compliment their look | 정말 잘 어울려요 (*jeongmal jal eoullyeoyo*) |
| Hand in the cash envelope | 봉투 어디에 내요? (*bongtu eodie naeyo?*) |
| Find the banquet floor | 식사는 어디서 해요? (*siksaneun eodiseo haeyo?*) |

## What happens at the banquet after the ceremony?

Most Korean wedding halls have a buffet on the same floor or one floor below. Guests head there right after the ceremony, eat quickly, and leave — the whole event often takes 90 minutes from arrival to departure. One phrase makes you fit in immediately: **음식이 너무 맛있어요** (*eumsigi neomu massisseoyo*), "The food is so delicious," said to any family member you meet at your table.

You: **음식이 너무 맛있어요.** — *eumsigi neomu massisseoyo.* — "The food is so delicious."  
Them: **많이 드세요.** — *mani deuseyo.* — "Please eat plenty."  
You: **감사합니다.** — *gamsahamnida.* — "Thank you."

Korean banquet food is served buffet-style and typically includes soup, rice, grilled meat, and several side dishes. No special phrase needed to fill your tray — just grab one at the start and follow the flow.

## What usually goes wrong for first-time guests?

The biggest freeze point is the **축의금** (*chugeuigeum*) table just inside the entrance. You put cash in a plain white envelope, write your name on the outside, hand it to a staff member, and sign the guest book beside it. If you are not sure where to go, ask **봉투 어디에 내요?** and any staff member will point you right.

A second mistake is stressing about when the ceremony starts. Arrive five to ten minutes early — ceremonies begin on time — but if you are late, you can walk straight to the banquet floor and join the meal without awkwardness.

One more thing: dress code is smart casual to semi-formal. Avoid all-white outfits, which are associated with funerals in Korean tradition.
