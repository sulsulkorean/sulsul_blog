Korean phrases at a tailor in Korea lean on Pattern 23 from the SULSUL workbook — verb stem plus **아/어 주세요** (*a/eo juseyo*), "please do this for me." Hand over pants that drag on the floor and say **기장 줄여 주세요** (*gijang jullyeo juseyo*), "Please shorten the length." Pattern 30 — **더** (*deo*, more) plus an adjective — fine-tunes the fit: **조금 더 짧게 해 주세요** (*jogeum deo jjapge hae juseyo*), "Please make it a bit shorter."

> - Shorten the hem: **기장 줄여 주세요** (*gijang jullyeo juseyo*), "Please shorten the length."
> - Take in the waist: **허리 좁혀 주세요** (*heori jobhyeo juseyo*), "Please take in the waist."
> - When ready: **언제 되나요?** (*eonje doenayo?*), "When will it be ready?"
> - Try it on: **입어 봐도 돼요?** (*ibeo bwaodo dwaeyo?*), "May I try it on?"
> - Price check: **얼마예요?** (*eolmayeyo?*), "How much is it?"

## How do you drop off clothes at a Korean tailor?

**수선집** (*suseonjip*, repair and alteration shop) counters sit near markets, subway exits, and shopping streets. Many handle **바지** (*baji*, pants), **치마** (*chima*, skirt), and **코트** (*koteu*, coat) in one small storefront. Start with Pattern 23 and point to the part that needs work.

You: **안녕하세요. 기장 줄여 주세요.** — *annyeonghaseyo. gijang jullyeo juseyo.* — "Hello. Please shorten the length."  
Them: **언제 필요하세요?** — *eonje piryohaseyo?* — "When do you need it?"  
You: **내일까지 가능해요?** — *naejikkaji ganeunghaeyo?* — "Is it possible by tomorrow?"

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Shorten length | 기장 줄여 주세요 (*gijang jullyeo juseyo*) |
| Take in waist | 허리 좁혀 주세요 (*heori jobhyeo juseyo*) |
| When ready? | 언제 되나요? (*eonje doenayo?*) |
| Try it on | 입어 봐도 돼요? (*ibeo bwaodo dwaeyo?*) |
| How much? | 얼마예요? (*eolmayeyo?*) |

## What do you say at the fitting?

Pattern 30 helps when the first pin is close but not quite right — **조금 더 짧게** (*jogeum deo jjapge*), "a bit shorter," or **조금 더 길게** (*jogeum deo gilge*), "a bit longer." Pattern 44 — **(V)아/어도 돼요?** (*a/eodo dwaeyo?*), "May I?" — covers trying the garment on.

You: **입어 봐도 돼요?** — *ibeo bwaodo dwaeyo?* — "May I try it on?"  
Them: **네, 탈의실 있어요.** — *ne, taruisil isseoyo.* — "Yes, there is a fitting room."  
You: **조금 더 짧게 해 주세요.** — *jogeum deo jjapge hae juseyo.* — "Please make it a bit shorter."

## What usually goes wrong at a Korean tailor?

Travelers often mime "short" with their hands but skip the pickup date — **언제 되나요?** at drop-off prevents showing up the night before a flight when the shop needs two days. A second mistake is leaving without a receipt slip; ask **영수증 주세요** (*yeongsujeung juseyo*), "Receipt, please," so staff can find your tagged **바지** (*baji*, pants) on a crowded rack.

You: **얼마예요?** — *eolmayeyo?* — "How much is it?"  
Them: **8,000원이에요.** — *palcheon won-ieyo.* — "It is 8,000 won."  
You: **네, 내일 오후에 올게요.** — *ne, naeil ohu-e olgeyo.* — "Yes, I will come tomorrow afternoon."
