Korean phrases for a laundromat matter the moment your travel bag runs out of clean socks. Ask the hotel desk **빨래방 어디 있어요?** (*ppallaebang eodi isseoyo?*), "Where is the laundromat?" and you get a pin on your map instead of wandering side streets. Inside the shop, **세탁기 어떻게 써요?** (*setakgi eotteoke sseoyo?*), "How do I use the washer?" opens every coin-laundry machine in Korea.

> - Find a shop nearby: **빨래방 어디 있어요?** (*ppallaebang eodi isseoyo?*), "Where is the laundromat?"
> - Ask how the machine works: **세탁기 어떻게 써요?** (*setakgi eotteoke sseoyo?*), "How do I use the washer?"
> - Buy detergent: **세제 있어요?** (*seje isseoyo?*), "Do you have detergent?"
> - Check the dryer: **건조기도 있어요?** (*geonjogido isseoyo?*), "Is there a dryer too?"

## How do you find a laundromat near your hotel?

Many Seoul neighborhoods have a **빨래방** (*ppallaebang*) — a self-service coin laundry — within a five-minute walk. Hotel staff know the closest one. At the front desk, ask:

**근처에 빨래방 있어요?** — *geuncheoe ppallaebang isseoyo?* — "Is there a laundromat nearby?"

If they point you down the street, confirm with **걸어서 얼마나 걸려요?** (*georeoseo eolmana geollyeoyo?*), "How long does it take on foot?" Most laundromats are open late and run on coins or a prepaid card at the entrance.

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Find a shop | 빨래방 어디 있어요? (*ppallaebang eodi isseoyo?*) |
| Need detergent | 세제 있어요? (*seje isseoyo?*) |
| Use the washer | 세탁기 어떻게 써요? (*setakgi eotteoke sseoyo?*) |
| Check the dryer | 건조기도 있어요? (*geonjogido isseoyo?*) |

## What do you say once you are inside?

Coin laundries in Korea look similar: a row of washers, a change machine, sometimes a vending slot for detergent. If the buttons are all in Korean and you are not sure which cycle to pick, ask another customer or the shop owner if someone is there.

You: **세탁기 어떻게 써요?** — *setakgi eotteoke sseoyo?* — "How do I use the washer?"  
Them: **동전 넣고 이 버튼 누르세요.** — *dongjeon neogo i beoteun nureuseyo.* — "Put in coins and press this button."  
You: **몇 분 돌아요?** — *myeot bun dorayo?* — "How many minutes does it run?"  
Them: **삼십 분이에요.** — *samsip bunieyo.* — "Thirty minutes."

**몇 분 돌아요?** (*myeot bun dorayo?*) is worth memorizing — the answer tells you whether you have time to grab coffee or need to stay by the machine. ## What if you left something in the machine?

It happens. Come back and ask **제 옷 여기 있어요?** (*je ot yeogi isseoyo?*), "Are my clothes here?" Staff or other customers often move finished laundry to a folding table. If a sock is missing, **하나 없어졌어요** (*hana eopseojyeosseoyo*), "One is missing," is clearer than a long story.

Self-service laundry is cheaper than hotel dry cleaning and faster than waiting three days. The phrases are short because the situation is simple — find the shop, run the machine, collect your clothes.
