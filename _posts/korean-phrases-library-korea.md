Korean phrases at a library start with Pattern 04 from the SULSUL workbook — **어디 있어요?** (*eodi isseoyo?*), "Where is…?" Ask **도서관 어디 있어요?** (*doseogwan eodi isseoyo?*), "Where is the library?" at a subway information desk, then **책 빌릴 수 있어요?** (*chaek billil su isseoyo?*), "Can I borrow books?" at the counter. Pattern 25 opens a study session with a Korean friend — **같이 공부할래요?** (*gachi gongbuhallaeyo?*), "Shall we study together?"

> - Find the building: **도서관 어디 있어요?** (*doseogwan eodi isseoyo?*), "Where is the library?"
> - Ask to borrow: **책 빌릴 수 있어요?** (*chaek billil su isseoyo?*), "Can I borrow books?"
> - Study together: **같이 공부할래요?** (*gachi gongbuhallaeyo?*), "Shall we study together?"
> - Ask where to sit: **앉을 자리 있어요?** (*anjeul jari isseoyo?*), "Is there a seat?"
> - Return a book: **반납하고 싶어요** (*bannaphago sipeoyo*), "I'd like to return (this)."

## How do you find the library desk?

Public libraries in Seoul — **도서관** (*doseogwan*) — often share a building with a community center. Signs mix Korean and English, but the service desk is where you need Pattern 04 and Pattern 26 (**에서**, *-eseo*, "at/in").

You: **도서관 어디 있어요?** — *doseogwan eodi isseoyo?* — "Where is the library?"  
Them: **3층이에요.** — *samcheung-ieyo.* — "It is on the third floor."  
You: **책 빌릴 수 있어요?** — *chaek billil su isseoyo?* — "Can I borrow books?"

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Where is the library? | 도서관 어디 있어요? (*doseogwan eodi isseoyo?*) |
| Can I borrow books? | 책 빌릴 수 있어요? (*chaek billil su isseoyo?*) |
| Is there a seat? | 앉을 자리 있어요? (*anjeul jari isseoyo?*) |
| Study together? | 같이 공부할래요? (*gachi gongbuhallaeyo?*) |
| Return this book | 반납하고 싶어요 (*bannaphago sipeoyo*) |

## What do you say when you need a library card?

Many branches issue a free membership card with a passport or ARC. Ask **회원증 만들 수 있어요?** (*hoewonjeung mandeul su isseoyo?*), "Can I get a membership card?" Staff may ask how long you stay — answer with **일주일 있어요** (*iljuil isseoyo*), "I am here for a week," or your actual dates.

You: **회원증 만들 수 있어요?** — *hoewonjeung mandeul su isseoyo?* — "Can I get a membership card?"  
Them: **여권 보여 주세요.** — *yeogwon boyeo juseyo.* — "Please show your passport."  
You: **네, 여기 있어요.** — *ne, yeogi isseoyo.* — "Yes, here it is."

Pattern 25 from the workbook fits once you have a seat — text a study partner **내일 같이 도서관에서 공부할래요?** (*naeil gachi doseogwan-eseo gongbuhallaeyo?*), "Shall we study at the library together tomorrow?" ## What usually goes wrong at a Korean library?

Travelers often walk past the **반납** (*bannap*, return) slot and queue at the wrong desk — look for **대출** (*daechul*, checkout) at the main counter. A second mistake is whispering in English when staff ask **앉을 자리 있어요?** — a short **네, 있어요** (*ne, isseoyo*), "Yes, there is," or **없어요** (*eopseoyo*), "There is not," keeps the line moving.

You: **반납하고 싶어요.** — *bannaphago sipeoyo.* — "I'd like to return this."  
Them: **여기 넣으시면 돼요.** — *yeogi neoeusimyeon dwaeyo.* — "You can put it in here."  
You: **감사합니다.** — *gamsahamnida.* — "Thank you."
