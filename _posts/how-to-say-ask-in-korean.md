"Ask" in Korean is **물어보다** (*mul-eo-bo-da*) when you mean asking a question. When you are asking someone older, a teacher, or a stranger you want to be extra polite to, it becomes **여쭤보다** (*yeo-jjwo-bo-da*). But when "ask" means asking someone *to do something* for you — a favor, a photo, a little help — Korean uses a different word entirely: **부탁하다** (*bu-tak-ha-da*). English packs all of this into one verb. Korean splits it in two, and picking the right one is most of the battle.

> - **물어보다** — to ask (a question) — everyday word
> - **여쭤보다** — to ask (a question), humbly — to elders, teachers, staff
> - **부탁하다** — to ask (a favor), to request
> - **질문** — a question (the noun)
> - **뭐 하나 물어봐도 돼요?** — Can I ask you something?
> - **부탁이 있어요** — I have a favor to ask

The sentence you will actually say most is the last-but-one: **뭐 하나 물어봐도 돼요?** It is the polite door-opener before any question to a stranger. Below: how each verb changes when you speak, the door-opener line, a full street dialogue, and the small mistakes that make a question sound off.

## Asking a question vs. asking a favor

Think of it this way. If the answer is *information*, use 물어보다. If the answer is *an action*, use 부탁하다.

| You want… | English | Korean verb |
|---|---|---|
| directions, a price, a name | "Can I ask where…?" | **물어보다** |
| someone to take your photo | "Can I ask you to…?" | **부탁하다** |
| the teacher's opinion | "I'd like to ask you…" | **여쭤보다** |
| a stranger to watch your bag | "Could I ask a favor?" | **부탁하다** |

A lot of learners say 물어봐도 돼요? and then ask for a photo. Koreans will understand, but it sounds like "Can I ask a question?" followed by a request. The natural opener for a request is **부탁 하나 해도 돼요?** (*bu-tak ha-na hae-do dwae-yo?*), "Can I ask you a favor?"

## The verb forms you will actually say

Dictionary forms like 물어보다 almost never come out of a mouth in conversation. These do:

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **물어볼게요** | *mul-eo-bol-ge-yo* | I'll ask (someone) |
| **물어봤어요** | *mul-eo-bwa-sseo-yo* | I asked |
| **물어보세요** | *mul-eo-bo-se-yo* | Please ask / Go ahead and ask |
| **물어봐** | *mul-eo-bwa* | Ask (casual, to a friend) |
| **여쭤볼게요** | *yeo-jjwo-bol-ge-yo* | I'll ask (my boss, the teacher) |
| **부탁해요** | *bu-tak-hae-yo* | Please (I'm counting on you) |
| **부탁드려요** | *bu-tak-deu-ryeo-yo* | I kindly ask you (extra polite) |

You will hear **물어보세요** a lot from shop staff and guides. It means "go ahead, ask me." And **부탁해요** is one of the most useful words in Korea: hand a taxi driver an address and say **여기로 부탁해요**, "here, please."

## "Can I ask you something?" — the one line to memorize

In Korea you rarely jump straight into a question with a stranger. You open with a short permission line first. It comes from a textbook pattern: **(V)아/어도 돼요?**, "Is it okay if I…?"

- **물어보다** → **물어봐도 돼요?** — Is it okay if I ask?
- **뭐 하나 물어봐도 돼요?** — Can I ask you one thing?
- **잠깐 물어봐도 될까요?** — Could I ask you something for a second? (softer)
- **하나 여쭤봐도 될까요?** — May I ask you one thing? (to an older person)

Put **저기요** (*jeo-gi-yo*, "excuse me") in front, and you have the full approach:

**저기요, 뭐 하나 물어봐도 돼요?**
*jeo-gi-yo, mwo ha-na mul-eo-bwa-do dwae-yo?*
"Excuse me, can I ask you something?"

Most people will stop, nod, and say **네, 물어보세요** or just **네?** Then ask your real question.

## A real street conversation

You are near a subway exit with your map app spinning in circles.

**You:** 저기요, 뭐 하나 물어봐도 돼요?
*Excuse me, can I ask you something?*

**Local:** 네, 말씀하세요.
*Sure, go ahead.*

**You:** 경복궁 어떻게 가요?
*How do I get to Gyeongbokgung?*

**Local:** 3번 출구로 나가서 쭉 가세요.
*Go out exit 3 and walk straight.*

**You:** 감사합니다! 그리고… 부탁 하나 해도 돼요? 사진 좀 찍어 주세요.
*Thank you! And… can I ask a favor? Please take a photo for me.*

**Local:** 네, 그럼요.
*Sure, of course.*

Notice the switch in the middle: 물어봐도 돼요 for the directions, 부탁 for the photo. That is the whole difference, said out loud.

## When to use 여쭤보다

여쭤보다 is the humble version of 물어보다. You lower yourself a little to show respect. Use it when the person you are asking is older, senior, or in a role you respect:

- To a teacher: **선생님, 하나 여쭤봐도 될까요?**
- To a hotel concierge: **여쭤볼 게 있는데요…** ("I have something to ask…")
- About your boss: **팀장님께 여쭤볼게요.** ("I'll ask the team leader.")

You never use 여쭤보다 about yourself being asked. If someone asks *you*, it is still 물어보다: **친구가 물어봤어요**, "My friend asked me."

## Mistakes that make a question sound off

**Using 질문하다 in casual speech.** 질문하다 means "to ask a question," but it sounds formal, like a classroom or a press conference. In daily life, 물어보다 is what people say. Use **질문** as a noun instead: **질문 있어요!** ("I have a question!") works well in a class or tour.

**Asking for a favor with 물어보다.** As above — a request is 부탁. 사진 좀 물어봐도 돼요 makes no sense in Korean.

**Forgetting 좀.** **좀** (*jom*) softens any request. **사진 좀 찍어 주세요** is friendly; without 좀 it sounds blunter than you mean.

**Skipping the opener.** Firing a question at a stranger without 저기요 or 물어봐도 돼요 is not rude, but it can startle people. One short line first gets you a warmer answer.

## Say it out loud

Practice these three until they come out in one breath:

1. **저기요, 뭐 하나 물어봐도 돼요?** — to a stranger on the street.
2. **부탁 하나 해도 돼요?** — before asking for a photo.
3. **하나 여쭤봐도 될까요?** — to an older shop owner or a guide.
