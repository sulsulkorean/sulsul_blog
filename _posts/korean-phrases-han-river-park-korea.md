Korean phrases for Han River Park pay off the second you step off the subway at Yeouinaru or Ttukseom. The riverfront is how Seoulites recharge on a weekend — cycling, eating fried chicken on a blanket, watching the bridge lights come on — and a few simple phrases open every part of it. Start with **자전거 빌릴 수 있어요?** (*jajeongeo billil su isseoyo?*), "Can I rent a bicycle?" at any of the riverside rental kiosks, and the attendant will hand you a helmet and a route map.

> - Rent a bike: **자전거 빌릴 수 있어요?** (*jajeongeo billil su isseoyo?*), "Can I rent a bicycle?"
> - Find a good picnic spot: **좋은 자리 어디예요?** (*joeun jari eodiyeyo?*), "Where is a good spot?"
> - Ask for a blanket mat: **돗자리 빌릴 수 있어요?** (*dotjari billil su isseoyo?*), "Can I borrow a picnic mat?"
> - Order at a riverside stall: **여기 뭐가 맛있어요?** (*yeogi mwoga massisseoyo?*), "What is good here?"
> - Ask about the fireworks schedule: **불꽃놀이 언제예요?** (*bulkkonnori eonjeyeyo?*), "When are the fireworks?"

## How do you rent a bicycle at the Han River?

Bike rental huts (*대여소*, *daeyeoso*) line both banks and open early. You pay per hour with a card or T-money, return the bike at any hut on the same bank, and the staff will give you a short safety run-down in Korean. If you miss a word, confirm with:

**다시 한번 말해 주세요** (*dasi hanbeon malhae juseyo*), "Could you say that again, please?" — Korea's most useful four seconds of talking.

After you pay, ask **어디로 가면 좋아요?** (*eodiro gamyeon joayo?*), "Which direction do you recommend?" Most staff will gesture toward the Cherry Blossom Road or the path toward Yeouido Park, depending on the season.

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Rent a bike | 자전거 빌릴 수 있어요? (*jajeongeo billil su isseoyo?*) |
| Return the bike | 여기서 반납해요? (*yeogiseo bannapaeyo?*) |
| Borrow a picnic mat | 돗자리 빌릴 수 있어요? (*dotjari billil su isseoyo?*) |
| Ask for a route tip | 어디로 가면 좋아요? (*eodiro gamyeon joayo?*) |

## What do you say at the riverside food stalls?

Hangang convenience stores (CU, GS25) have dedicated picnic sections selling chicken, beer, cup noodles, and ramyeon. Most Koreans grab a bag, spread a mat, and eat facing the water. At the stall or counter:

You: **여기 뭐가 맛있어요?** — *yeogi mwoga massisseoyo?* — "What is good here?"  
Them: **치킨이랑 맥주요.** — *chikinrang maekjuyo.* — "Chicken and beer."  
You: **치킨 하나 주세요.** — *chikin hana juseyo.* — "One chicken, please."

**치킨 하나 주세요** (*chikin hana juseyo*) is the four-word sentence that defines a Korean riverside evening. For counting and ordering phrases more broadly, the post on [Korean numbers for paying and counting in Korea](/korean-numbers-paying-counting-korea) covers the same pattern in full.

## How do you find your friends in a crowd?

Hangang parks are big. If your group splits at the convenience store, ask a passerby:

**한강 다리 보이는 쪽이 어디예요?** (*hangang dari boinеun jjogi eodiyeyo?*), "Which way faces the bridge?" — the bridge view is the landmark everyone can describe.

If you want to grab a specific spot before sunset, say **저기 자리 있어요?** (*jeogi jari isseoyo?*), "Is that spot taken?" Korean park-goers are friendly about sharing the waterfront and will shake their head or wave you over without fuss.

One phrase that helps at any crowded Korean outdoor spot: **실례합니다** (*sillyehamnida*), "Excuse me" — it signals you are polite before you even say the actual question.
