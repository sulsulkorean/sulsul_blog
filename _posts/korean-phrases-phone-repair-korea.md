Korean phrases at a phone repair shop in Korea lean on Pattern 08 from the SULSUL workbook — past tense **았/었어요** (*ass/eosseoyo*), "something happened." Walk in with a cracked screen and say **화면이 깨졌어요** (*hwamyeoni kkaejyeosseoyo*), "The screen cracked," or **충전이 안 돼요** (*chungjeoni an dwaeyo*), "It won't charge." Pattern 12 — **(V)ㄹ/을 수 있어요?** (*eul su isseoyo?*), "Can you…?" — covers the repair itself: **수리할 수 있어요?** (*surihal su isseoyo?*), "Can you repair it?"

> - Screen cracked: **화면이 깨졌어요** (*hwamyeoni kkaejyeosseoyo*), "The screen cracked."
> - Won't charge: **충전이 안 돼요** (*chungjeoni an dwaeyo*), "It won't charge."
> - Can you fix it: **수리할 수 있어요?** (*surihal su isseoyo?*), "Can you repair it?"
> - How long: **얼마나 걸려요?** (*eolmana geollyeoyo?*), "How long will it take?"
> - Ask the price: **얼마예요?** (*eolmayeyo?*), "How much is it?"

## How do you explain what broke at a Korean phone shop?

**핸드폰 수리점** (*haendeupon surijeom*, phone repair shop) counters cluster near electronics districts like Yongsan and inside major shopping malls. Staff see cracked screens every day — lead with Pattern 08, name the part, then ask Pattern 12.

You: **안녕하세요. 화면이 깨졌어요. 수리할 수 있어요?** — *annyeonghaseyo. hwamyeoni kkaejyeosseoyo. surihal su isseoyo?* — "Hello. The screen cracked. Can you repair it?"  
Them: **기종이 뭐예요?** — *gijong-i mwoyeyo?* — "What model is it?"  
You: **아이폰 15예요. 얼마나 걸려요?** — *aipon sib-o-yeyo. eolmana geollyeoyo?* — "It is an iPhone 15. How long will it take?"

## What do you say about data and pickup time?

Repair shops often ask whether you backed up your photos. If you need the phone the same day, say so upfront — some jobs take an hour, others need overnight.

You: **오늘 안에 되어요?** — *oneul an-e doeeoyo?* — "Can it be done today?"  
Them: **한 시간 정도 걸려요.** — *han sigan jeongdo geollyeoyo.* — "It takes about an hour."  
You: **데이터는 그대로 있어요?** — *deiteoneun geudaero isseoyo?* — "Will my data stay intact?"

The **도와주세요** (*dowajuseyo*, please help me) line from [getting a SIM card in Korea](/korean-phrases-sim-card-korea) works when the clerk switches to technical Korean and you need them to slow down.

## What usually goes wrong at a Korean phone repair shop?

Travelers often say **고장 났어요** (*gojang nasseoyo*, it broke) without naming the part — the clerk has to guess whether you mean the screen, battery, or charging port. Add **화면이 깨졌어요** or **충전이 안 돼요** so the quote is accurate.

A second mistake is assuming original parts at every counter. Some shops offer **호환** (*hogwan*, compatible) screens at a lower price. If that matters to you, ask **정품 부품이에요?** (*jeongpum buumpum-ieyo?*), "Are these original parts?" before you agree to the repair.
