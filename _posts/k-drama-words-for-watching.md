When Koreans talk about watching K-dramas, they use a small set of words you will not find in a drama's script: **정주행** (*jeongjuhaeng*, binge-watching a series from episode one), **몰아보기** (*morabogi*, watching many episodes in one go), **본방사수** (*bonbangsasu*, watching an episode the moment it airs), **스포** (*seupo*, a spoiler), **자막** (*jamak*, subtitles), and **다음 화** (*daeum hwa*, the next episode). Learn these and you can join any Korean conversation about what you are watching — and you will also know what to look for when you switch your subtitles to Korean. Below is each word, a sentence you can actually say, and how to turn your watching time into Korean practice.

> - **드라마** (*deurama*) — a TV series. Koreans rarely say "K-drama"; it is just 드라마.
> - **화** / **회** (*hwa* / *hoe*) — episode. 1화 = episode one.
> - **정주행** (*jeongjuhaeng*) — watching a whole series in order, start to finish.
> - **몰아보기** (*morabogi*) — watching several episodes at once.
> - **본방사수** (*bonbangsasu*) — "defending the live broadcast": watching as soon as it is released.
> - **스포** (*seupo*) — spoiler. 스포하지 마 = "Don't spoil it."
> - **자막** (*jamak*) — subtitles. 한국어 자막 = Korean subtitles.
> - **결말** (*gyeolmal*) — the ending.

## The first word: it's just 드라마

Fans abroad say "K-drama." In Korea, a Korean show is simply a **드라마**, and a show you stream is still a 드라마 — the platform rarely gets mentioned. The most common opening question is:

- **요즘 무슨 드라마 봐요?** (*yojeum museun deurama bwayo?*) — "What drama are you watching these days?"
- **요즘 뭐 봐?** (*yojeum mwo bwa?*) — "What are you watching these days?" (casual)

If you answer with a title, you will usually get **그거 재밌어요?** (*geugeo jaemisseoyo?*, "Is it good?") back. **재밌어요** (*jaemisseoyo*, "It's fun / good") and **완전 꿀잼이에요** (*wanjeon kkuljaemieyo*, "It's so good") are the two answers you need.

## Counting episodes: 화, 회, and 다음 화

Streaming apps in Korea label episodes with **화**: 1화, 2화, 3화. Broadcast TV and fans often use **회** for the same thing. You do not need to choose — both are understood.

- **지금 몇 화까지 봤어요?** (*jigeum myeot hwakkaji bwasseoyo?*) — "Which episode are you up to?"
- **6화까지 봤어요.** (*yuk hwakkaji bwasseoyo*) — "I've watched up to episode 6."
- **다음 화 너무 궁금해요.** (*daeum hwa neomu gunggeumhaeyo*) — "I'm dying to know what happens next episode."

The last episode is the **마지막 화** (*majimak hwa*) or **최종화** (*choejonghwa*), and a second season is **시즌 2** (*sijeun tu*) — Koreans say the English number here.

## Binge-watching: 정주행 vs 몰아보기

These two are the most useful words on this list, and they are not quite the same.

**정주행** comes from racing — 정 (forward) + 주행 (driving). It means going through a whole series in order, from 1화 to the end. It is the word for "I started a new show and watched all of it."

- **주말에 이 드라마 정주행했어요.** (*jumare i deurama jeongjuhaenghaesseoyo*) — "I binged this whole drama over the weekend."
- **처음부터 다시 정주행할 거예요.** (*cheoeumbuteo dasi jeongjuhaenghal geoyeyo*) — "I'm going to rewatch it from the beginning."

A rewatch also has its own word: **재정주행** (*jaejeongjuhaeng*), or more playfully **N차 정주행** (*en-cha jeongjuhaeng*, "my Nth time through").

**몰아보기** is about quantity, not order — "watching a lot at once." 몰아보다 means to gather things up and watch them together.

- **밀린 거 오늘 몰아볼 거예요.** (*millin geo oneul morabol geoyeyo*) — "I'm going to catch up on everything I missed today."

So: you 정주행 a series, and you 몰아보기 the episodes you fell behind on.

## Watching live: 본방사수

When a drama airs weekly, fans compete to watch it the second it drops. **본방사수** combines 본방 (*bonbang*, the original broadcast) and 사수 (*sasu*, defending to the end) — you are "defending" the premiere slot. It is said with a little pride:

- **오늘 본방사수해야 돼요.** (*oneul bonbangsasuhaeya dwaeyo*) — "I have to watch it live tonight."
- **본방사수 실패했어요.** (*bonbangsasu silpaehaesseoyo*) — "I missed the live release."

On a streaming app, this means pressing play the minute a new episode unlocks. The opposite is **다시보기** (*dasibogi*, watching a replay later) — not embarrassing, just less heroic.

## Spoilers and endings: 스포 and 결말

**스포** is the shortened English "spoiler," and it works as a verb with 하다:

- **스포하지 마세요!** (*seupohaji maseyo!*) — "Please don't spoil it!"
- **스포 주의** (*seupo juui*) — "Spoiler warning." You will see this at the top of Korean posts and comments.
- **혹시 스포예요?** (*hoksi seupoyeyo?*) — "Wait, is that a spoiler?"

When the show ends, the talk shifts to the **결말** (*gyeolmal*, ending). **결말 어땠어요?** (*gyeolmal eottaesseoyo?*) asks "How was the ending?", and the two classic reactions are **해피엔딩이에요** (*haepiendingieyo*, "It's a happy ending") and **결말이 좀 아쉬웠어요** (*gyeormari jom aswiwosseoyo*, "The ending was a bit disappointing"). 아쉽다 — "a little short of what I hoped" — is the polite Korean way to complain about a finale.

## Subtitles: 자막 and how to learn from Korean ones

**자막** means subtitles. Korean viewers ask **자막 있어요?** (*jamak isseoyo?*, "Are there subtitles?") about foreign films, and you can ask the same about anything. Two phrases worth knowing:

- **한국어 자막으로 봐요.** (*hangugeo jamageuro bwayo*) — "I watch with Korean subtitles."
- **자막 없이 보고 싶어요.** (*jamak eopsi bogo sipeoyo*) — "I want to watch without subtitles."

That second sentence is the goal of most learners, and most streaming apps make the middle step easy. In the subtitle menu of a Korean show, pick **Korean** instead of English. Now the words you hear and the words you read are the same, so you can catch the exact sentence — **괜찮아?**, **가지 마**, **밥 먹었어?** — instead of a translator's smoothed-over English.

A simple routine that works: watch an episode with English subtitles first, then rewatch one favourite scene with Korean subtitles, pause on a line you like, and say it out loud. One scene a day beats a whole season of passive watching.

## Four mistakes fans make with these words

**Saying "K-drama" in Korean.** 케이드라마 is understood, but it sounds like a foreigner's word. Just say 드라마.

**Using 정주행 for one episode.** 정주행 means a whole series, or at least a long run. For one episode, say **1화 봤어요** — "I watched episode one."

**Mixing up 다음 화 and 다음 시즌.** 다음 화 is the next episode; 다음 시즌 (*daeum sijeun*) is the next season. Asking **다음 화 언제 나와요?** ("When does the next episode come out?") about a finished series will get a confused look.

**Dropping 요 with strangers.** Friends say 스포하지 마! A comment to someone you do not know should be **스포하지 마세요**. The [guide to polite Korean](/how-to-be-polite-in-korean) shows when to switch.

## Put the words into one conversation

Here is a short exchange that uses almost everything above:

> **A:** 요즘 무슨 드라마 봐요?
> **B:** 새 드라마 정주행 중이에요. 지금 8화까지 봤어요.
> **A:** 아, 그거 결말 진짜… 
> **B:** 스포하지 마세요!

*A: "What drama are you watching these days?" B: "I'm binging a new drama. I'm up to episode 8." A: "Oh, the ending of that one is really…" B: "Don't spoil it!"*
