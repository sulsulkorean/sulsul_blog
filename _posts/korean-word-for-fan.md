The Korean word for a fan — a person who loves a singer, an actor, or a team — is **팬** (*paen*). It is borrowed straight from English, so "I'm a fan" is simply **팬이에요** (*paen-ieyo*). But if you mean the machine that blows air, the word is completely different: an electric fan is **선풍기** (*seonpunggi*), and a folding hand fan is **부채** (*buchae*). Korean never mixes these up the way English does, so the first thing to decide is which "fan" you are looking for.

> - **팬** (*paen*) — a fan of someone. "Fan" in the K-pop sense.
> - **선풍기** (*seonpunggi*) — an electric fan. Standing or desk fan.
> - **손풍기** (*sonpunggi*) — a small handheld battery fan. Everywhere in a Korean summer.
> - **부채** (*buchae*) — a paper hand fan you wave yourself.
> - **에어컨** (*eeokeon*) — the air conditioner, which is often what you actually want.

## 팬: saying "I'm a fan" in Korean

Because **팬** is a loanword, it behaves like any other noun. Put a name in front of it and **-이에요** after it:

- **저 아이유 팬이에요.** — *jeo aiyu paen-ieyo.* — "I'm an IU fan."
- **완전 팬이에요!** — *wanjeon paen-ieyo!* — "I'm a huge fan!"
- **오래된 팬이에요.** — *oraedoen paen-ieyo.* — "I've been a fan for a long time."
- **팬이 됐어요.** — *paeni dwaesseoyo.* — "I became a fan."

The last one is worth noticing. In English you "become a fan." In Korean the fan "becomes" — **팬이 되다** — with **이** marking what you turned into. **드라마 보고 팬이 됐어요** (*deurama bogo paeni dwaesseoyo*) is "I became a fan after watching the drama," and it is one of the most natural sentences you can say to a Korean who asks how you got into their country's shows.

Pronunciation matters more than it looks. Koreans do not say "fan" with an English *f* — Korean has no *f* sound, so it becomes a soft *p*: *paen*. If you say "fan" the English way, people will understand you, but **팬** with a light *p* sounds like you have been listening to Korean for a while.

## The words built on 팬

Once you have **팬**, a whole family of fan vocabulary opens up. Most of it is easy to guess:

- **팬클럽** (*paenkeulleop*) — fan club.
- **팬덤** (*paendeom*) — fandom.
- **팬미팅** (*paenmiting*) — fan meeting.
- **팬싸** (*paenssa*) — short for **팬사인회**, a fan sign event.
- **팬심** (*paensim*) — "fan heart," the feeling of being a fan. **팬심으로 샀어요** — "I bought it out of fan love."
- **찐팬** (*jjinpaen*) — a true, real fan. **찐** is slang for "genuine."
- **성덕** (*seongdeok*) — a fan who met, or ended up working with, the person they love.

Korean fans also call themselves by the **fandom name** of the group — ARMY, MY, CARAT — and ask each other **무슨 팬이에요?** (*museun paen-ieyo?*, "Who are you a fan of?"). ## A conversation in the merch line

This is the kind of exchange that actually happens in front of a pop-up store in Seoul:

Them: **혹시 외국에서 오셨어요?** — *hoksi oegugeseo osyeosseoyo?* — "Did you come from abroad?"  
You: **네, 미국에서 왔어요. 저 세븐틴 팬이에요.** — *ne, migugeseo wasseoyo. jeo sebeuntin paen-ieyo.* — "Yes, from the US. I'm a SEVENTEEN fan."  
Them: **와, 저도요! 언제부터 팬이었어요?** — *wa, jeodoyo! eonjebuteo paenieosseoyo?* — "Me too! Since when?"  
You: **3년 됐어요. 콘서트 영상 보고 팬이 됐어요.** — *samnyeon dwaesseoyo. konseoteu yeongsang bogo paeni dwaesseoyo.* — "Three years. I became a fan after a concert video."  

Notice **저도요** (*jeodoyo*, "me too") — the fastest way to turn two strangers into fan friends.

## 선풍기 and 부채: when "fan" means cool air

![A convenience store on a hot Seoul afternoon, the kind of day you want a 선풍기 or a 손풍기](/assets/blog/scenes/convenience-heat.jpg)

If you landed here because your hotel room is hot, this is the section you need. **선풍기** literally means "wind-spinning machine" — 선 (spin) + 풍 (wind) + 기 (machine). The **풍** in it is the same one you see in **손풍기**, the handheld fan: 손 means "hand."

- **선풍기 있어요?** — *seonpunggi isseoyo?* — "Do you have a fan?"
- **선풍기 좀 켜 주세요.** — *seonpunggi jom kyeo juseyo.* — "Please turn on the fan."
- **선풍기 좀 꺼 주세요.** — *seonpunggi jom kkeo juseyo.* — "Please turn off the fan."
- **손풍기 어디서 팔아요?** — *sonpunggi eodiseo parayo?* — "Where do they sell handheld fans?"
- **너무 더워요.** — *neomu deowoyo.* — "It's too hot."

**켜다** (on) and **끄다** (off) work for every machine in Korea: lights, TV, air conditioner. Learn them once with 선풍기 and you can use them everywhere.

**부채** is the traditional hand fan, but you will still see it handed out free at summer festivals and outdoor concerts, often printed with an idol's face. At a fan meeting someone may hand you one and say **부채 하나 가져가세요** — "take a fan." In that one sentence, both kinds of fan are in the same place.

## Mistakes learners make with "fan" in Korean

**Saying 팬 when you mean the machine.** **팬 있어요?** at a hotel front desk will get a confused look. Some appliances have a **팬** inside them, but the thing on the floor is always **선풍기**.

**Forgetting the particle when you "become" a fan.** **팬 됐어요** is understood in casual speech, but **팬이 됐어요** is the clean version, and it is what you want with someone you just met.

**Using 빠 as a general word for fan.** You may see **빠순이** or **-빠** online. It started as a word for an obsessive fan and is still used to put fans down. Stick to **팬** and **찐팬** — they are warm, not mocking.

**Saying 완전 팬이야 to a stranger.** Idols talk to fans in casual Korean, and it is easy to copy. With someone you just met, use **완전 팬이에요** — the **요** keeps it friendly instead of overfamiliar.

## Practice the three sentences that matter

If you only practice three lines from this page, make them these:

1. **저 ○○ 팬이에요.** — "I'm a fan of ○○."
2. **○○ 보고 팬이 됐어요.** — "I became a fan after watching ○○."
3. **선풍기 좀 켜 주세요.** — "Please turn on the fan."

Say each one out loud three times, filling in a real name. The first two get you a conversation at a fan event. The third gets you through a Seoul August.
