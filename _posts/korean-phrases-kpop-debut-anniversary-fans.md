Every K-pop group has a date that turns the timeline into a rewatch party — the **debut anniversary**, when fans post the first stage, count the years, and flood the tag in Korean. **데뷔 축하해요** (*debwi chukahaeyo*), "Congratulations on the anniversary," is the line the whole tag repeats that day, and **첫 무대 다시 봤어요** (*cheot mudae dasi bwasseoyo*), "I rewatched their debut stage," is what you send when the grainy old clip shows up on your feed again.

> - Check the count: **데뷔 몇 주년이에요?** (*debwi myeot junyeonieyo?*), "How many years since debut?"
> - Say congratulations: **데뷔 축하해요** (*debwi chukahaeyo*), "Congratulations on the anniversary."
> - React to the years: **벌써 그렇게 됐어요?** (*beolsseo geureoke dwaesseoyo?*), "Has it been that long already?"
> - Share the rewatch: **첫 무대 다시 봤어요** (*cheot mudae dasi bwasseoyo*), "I rewatched their debut stage."
> - Promise to stay: **앞으로도 응원할게요** (*apeurodo eungwonhalgeyo*), "I'll keep cheering for them."

## How do you join an anniversary tag without just posting a heart?

Anniversary tags fill up fast with old photos and long English essays. A few short Korean lines put you inside the celebration instead of beside it.

1. If you joined the fandom late, ask **데뷔 몇 주년이에요?** and someone will happily tell you the whole story.
2. Post **데뷔 축하해요** early in the day, while the tag is still climbing.
3. When an old trainee photo shows up, reply **벌써 그렇게 됐어요?** — it is the reaction longtime fans expect.
4. Share the first stage link with **첫 무대 다시 봤어요** so the thread turns into a rewatch.
5. Close your post with **앞으로도 응원할게요**, the sentence fans read as a promise.

| Moment | Say this |
|---|---|
| Ask the year count | 데뷔 몇 주년이에요? (*debwi myeot junyeonieyo?*) |
| Congratulate | 데뷔 축하해요 (*debwi chukahaeyo*) |
| React to the years | 벌써 그렇게 됐어요? (*beolsseo geureoke dwaesseoyo?*) |
| Share the rewatch | 첫 무대 다시 봤어요 (*cheot mudae dasi bwasseoyo*) |
| Promise to stay | 앞으로도 응원할게요 (*apeurodo eungwonhalgeyo*) |

## What do Korean speakers say back?

Anniversary replies are warm and short — the tag moves quickly and nobody writes paragraphs.

You: **데뷔 축하해요!** — *debwi chukahaeyo!* — "Congratulations on the anniversary!"  
Them: **벌써 그렇게 됐어요, 시간 진짜 빠르다** — *beolsseo geureoke dwaesseoyo, sigan jinjja ppareuda* — "Has it been that long already, time really flies."  
You: **앞으로도 응원할게요.** — *apeurodo eungwonhalgeyo.*

If someone posts the debut stage without a caption, **첫 무대 다시 봤어요** is enough to start a whole conversation about how much the group changed. For the chant that carries the same energy at a live show, see [Korean phrases for K-pop fanchant practice](/korean-phrases-kpop-fanchant-practice-fans).

## What usually goes wrong on anniversary day?

Learners write a long English message, translate it, and post it hours after the tag has cooled off. The short Korean line sent on time reads warmer than the perfect paragraph sent late.

The other slip is saving **축하해요** for birthdays only — fans use it for debut dates, wins, and comebacks too, so it is worth having ready. For the day a group takes the trophy, see [Korean phrases when your K-pop group wins on a music show](/korean-phrases-kpop-music-show-win-fans).
