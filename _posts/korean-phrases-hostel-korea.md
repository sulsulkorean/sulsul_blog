Korean phrases at a **호스텔** (*hoseutel*, hostel) or **게스트하우스** (*geseuteu hauseu*, guesthouse) start with Pattern 05 from the SULSUL workbook — noun plus **이에요 / 예요** (*ieyo / yeyo*), "it is." Walk up to the desk and say **예약자예요** (*yeoyakjayeoyo*), "I have a reservation," or **체크인하려고 왔어요** (*chekeu-in haryeogo wasseoyo*), "I came to check in." Pattern 03 — **있어요?** (*isseoyo?*) — covers basics the moment you drop your bag: **와이파이 있어요?** (*waipai isseoyo?*), "Is there Wi-Fi?"

> - I have a booking: **예약자예요** (*yeoyakjayeoyo*), "I have a reservation."
> - Check in: **체크인하려고 왔어요** (*chekeu-in haryeogo wasseoyo*), "I came to check in."
> - Wi-Fi available: **와이파이 있어요?** (*waipai isseoyo?*), "Is there Wi-Fi?"
> - Locker for bags: **짐 보관함 있어요?** (*jim bogwanham isseoyo?*), "Is there a luggage locker?"
> - Quiet hours: **몇 시까지 조용해야 해요?** (*myeot sikkaji joyonghaeya haeyo?*), "Until what time should it be quiet?"

## How do you check in at a Korean hostel?

Budget **호스텔** (*hoseutel*) desks are often staffed by one person who also answers KakaoTalk messages. Lead with Pattern 05 — who you are on the booking — not a long travel story.

You: **안녕하세요. 예약자예요.** — *annyeonghaseyo. yeoyakjayeoyo.* — "Hello. I have a reservation."  
Them: **성함이 어떻게 되세요?** — *seonghami eotteoke doeseyo?* — "What is your name?"  
You: **매튜예요. Booking.com으로 했어요.** — *maetyuyeoyo. Booking.com-euro haesseoyo.* — "It is Matthew. I booked on Booking.com."

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Have a reservation | 예약자예요 (*yeoyakjayeoyo*) |
| Came to check in | 체크인하려고 왔어요 (*chekeu-in haryeogo wasseoyo*) |
| Wi-Fi? | 와이파이 있어요? (*waipai isseoyo?*) |
| Luggage locker | 짐 보관함 있어요? (*jim bogwanham isseoyo?*) |
| Quiet hours | 몇 시까지 조용해야 해요? (*myeot sikkaji joyonghaeya haeyo?*) |

## What do you ask after you get your room key?

Hostels often use a code lock or a card instead of a metal key. Pattern 41 — **아/어야 해요** (*a/eoya haeyo*), "have to" — covers house rules: **신발 벗어야 해요?** (*sinbal beoseoya haeyo?*), "Do I have to take off my shoes?" Pattern 23 — **알려 주세요** (*allyeo juseyo*) — gets the Wi-Fi password fast.

You: **와이파이 있어요?** — *waipai isseoyo?* — "Is there Wi-Fi?"  
Them: **네, 비밀번호는 reception123이에요.** — *ne, bimilbeonhoneun reception123-ieyo.* — "Yes, the password is reception123."  
You: **짐 보관함 있어요?** — *jim bogwanham isseoyo?* — "Is there a luggage locker?"

## What usually goes wrong at a Korean hostel?

Travelers walk into the dorm before staff assign a bed and get redirected — **체크인하려고 왔어요** at the desk first. A second mistake is showering during **조용 시간** (*joyong sigan*, quiet hours) without asking; **몇 시까지 조용해야 해요?** at check-in saves an awkward note on the whiteboard later.

You: **몇 시까지 조용해야 해요?** — *myeot sikkaji joyonghaeya haeyo?* — "Until what time should it be quiet?"  
Them: **11시부터예요.** — *yeol han sibuteo-yeyo.* — "From 11 p.m."  
You: **네, 알겠어요. 감사합니다.** — *ne, algesseoyo. gamsahamnida.* — "Okay, got it. Thank you."
