Korean phrases at a **강아지 카페** (*gangaji kape*, dog cafe) revolve around Pattern 44 from the SULSUL workbook — **해도 돼요?** (*haedo dwaeyo?*), "May I?" Every cafe posts its own house rules, so asking before you reach for a dog keeps everyone comfortable. Staff answer **네, 돼요** (*ne, dwaeyo*), "Yes, you may," or wave you toward the friendlier residents first.

> - Entry fee: **입장료 얼마예요?** (*ipjangnyo eolmayeyo?*), "How much is the entry fee?"
> - Touch a dog: **만져도 돼요?** (*manjyeodo dwaeyo?*), "May I touch it?"
> - Dog's name: **이름이 뭐예요?** (*ireumi mwoyeyo?*), "What is its name?"
> - Take a photo: **사진 찍어도 돼요?** (*sajin jjigeodo dwaeyo?*), "May I take a photo?"
> - Cute reaction: **너무 귀여워요!** (*neomu gwiyeowoyo!*), "So cute!"

## How do you enter a dog cafe in Korea?

Most **강아지 카페** include one drink in the entry fee. Staff will ask how many people are in your group, take payment, then point you to the lockers — shoes and bags usually stay outside the dog zone. Pattern 5 from the workbook, noun plus **있어요?** (*isseoyo?*), "Do you have?", handles any follow-up question.

You: **안녕하세요, 두 명이에요.** — *annyeonghaseyo, du myeong-ieyo.* — "Hello, two of us."  
Them: **입장료는 만 원이에요.** — *ipjangnyo-neun man wonieyo.* — "The entry fee is 10,000 won."  
You: **음료 포함이에요?** — *eumryo poham-ieyo?* — "Is a drink included?"

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 만져도 돼요? (*manjyeodo dwaeyo?*) | May I touch it? |
| 사진 찍어도 돼요? (*sajin jjigeodo dwaeyo?*) | May I take a photo? |
| 강아지 간식 있어요? (*gangaji gansik isseoyo?*) | Do you have dog treats? |
| 이름이 뭐예요? (*ireumi mwoyeyo?*) | What is its name? |
| 너무 귀여워요! (*neomu gwiyeowoyo!*) | So cute! |

## What do you say while you're with the dogs?

Staff usually flag which dogs like attention — **순해요** (*sunhaeyo*), "It's gentle," and which prefer space — **예민해요** (*yeminhaeyo*), "It's sensitive." If a dog startles you, **강아지가 무서워요** (*gangajiga museowayo*), "I'm scared of the dog," tells staff immediately.

You: **이 강아지 이름이 뭐예요?** — *i gangaji ireumi mwoyeyo?* — "What is this dog's name?"  
Them: **코코예요. 순해요.** — *kokoyeyo. sunhaeyo.* — "It's Coco — very gentle."  
You: **너무 귀여워요!** — *neomu gwiyeowoyo!* — "So cute!"

Many cafes sell dog treats at the counter. **강아지 간식 있어요?** (*gangaji gansik isseoyo?*) is Pattern 5 again — same structure, different noun.

## What do you order at a dog cafe?

Drinks work exactly like any Korean cafe. Pattern 1 — noun plus **주세요** (*juseyo*), "Please give me" — closes every order. [Korean numbers for paying in Korea](/korean-numbers-paying-counting-korea) covers the counting needed at the counter.

You: **아이스 아메리카노 주세요.** — *aiseu amerikano juseyo.* — "An iced Americano, please."  
Them: **네, 잠깐만요.** — *ne, jamkkanmanyo.* — "Yes, one moment."  
You: **감사합니다.** — *gamsahamnida.* — "Thank you."

If you feel awkward leaving — **또 올게요** (*tto olgeyo*), "I'll come again" — is the soft exit most regulars use on the way out.
