Korean phrases for a cooking class in Korea hinge on Pattern 15 from the SULSUL workbook — **어떻게 해요?** (*eotteoke haeyo?*), "How do you do this?" Whether you are folding **만두** (*mandu*, dumplings) or stirring **된장찌개** (*doenjang jjigae*, soybean-paste stew), the phrase to have ready next is **다시 보여 주세요** (*dasi boyeo juseyo*), "Please show me again," from Pattern 26.

> - Ask the method: **어떻게 해요?** (*eotteoke haeyo?*), "How do you do this?"
> - Request a replay: **다시 보여 주세요** (*dasi boyeo juseyo*), "Please show me again."
> - Check your work: **이거 맞아요?** (*igeo majayo?*), "Is this right?"
> - Dial down the heat: **맵게 하지 마세요** (*maepge haji maseyo*), "Please do not make it spicy."
> - Wrap it up happy: **맛있어요!** (*masisseoyo!*), "It's delicious!"

## How do you join a tourist cooking class in Korea?

Studios in Hongdae, Insadong, and Bukchon run drop-in or booked sessions. A quick reservation through Naver or Airbnb Experiences is the safest option. At the door, one short question settles availability before you hand over payment.

You: **오늘 자리 있어요?** — *oneul jari isseoyo?* — "Is there a spot today?"  
Them: **네, 두 명 가능해요.** — *ne, du myeong ganeunghaeyo.* — "Yes, two people is fine."  
You: **얼마예요?** — *eolmayeyo?* — "How much is it?"

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Spot available? | 오늘 자리 있어요? (*oneul jari isseoyo?*) |
| How do I do this? | 어떻게 해요? (*eotteoke haeyo?*) |
| Show me again | 다시 보여 주세요 (*dasi boyeo juseyo*) |
| Is this right? | 이거 맞아요? (*igeo majayo?*) |
| It's delicious! | 맛있어요! (*masisseoyo!*) |

## What do you say when you fall behind during the class?

Instructors move quickly once the oil is hot. Catch their attention between steps with **잠깐만요** (*jamkkanmanyo*), "Just a moment," before asking your follow-up question.

You: **잠깐만요. 이거 몇 분이에요?** — *jamkkanmanyo. igeo myeot bunieyo?* — "Just a moment. How many minutes is this?"  
Them: **5분이면 돼요.** — *o buniemyeon dwaeyo.* — "Five minutes is fine."  
You: **이거 맞아요?** — *igeo majayo?* — "Is this right?"

For staying polite when you need more time, the **다시 한 번** (*dasi han beon*), "one more time," shortcut from [asking for help politely in Korean](/politely-ask-for-help-in-korean) keeps any instructor patient with you.

## What do you say when you want to take the recipe home?

Many studios in **북촌** (*Bukchon*) and Insadong hand out printed recipe cards after the tasting. If yours does not, ask before the session wraps up.

You: **레시피 줄 수 있어요?** — *resiphi jul su isseoyo?* — "Can you give me the recipe?"  
Them: **네, 이메일로 보내 드릴게요.** — *ne, imeillo bonae deurilgeyo.* — "Yes, I will send it by email."  
You: **감사합니다! 정말 맛있어요.** — *gamsahamnida! jeongmal maisseoyo.* — "Thank you! It's really delicious."

A compliment goes a long way — the same **정말 맛있어요** (*jeongmal maisseoyo*) phrase from [complimenting a Korean friend's cooking](/compliment-korean-friends-cooking) works just as well in a studio kitchen.
