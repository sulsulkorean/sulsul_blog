Korea's **스터디카페** (*seuteodikapae*, "study cafe") charges by the hour and runs on a Korean-only touch screen. Individual cubicles, whisper-quiet rooms, and printer access make these spots a favourite for university students and exam-preppers — and any traveller who needs a focused afternoon off the tourist trail. Three Pattern-03 questions get you set up fast.

> - Check availability: **자리 있어요?** (*jari isseoyo?*), "Is there a seat?"
> - Get the password: **와이파이 비밀번호가 뭐예요?** (*waipai bimilbeonhoga mwoyeyo?*), "What is the Wi-Fi password?"
> - Find a charger: **충전기 있어요?** (*chungjeongi isseoyo?*), "Is there a charger?"
> - Extend time: **시간 더 추가할게요** (*sigan deo chugahalgeyo*), "I will add more time."
> - Ask for a locker: **사물함 있어요?** (*samulham isseoyo?*), "Is there a locker?"

## How do you check in at a 스터디카페?

Walk up to the front desk or the self-check kiosk and say **자리 있어요?** (*jari isseoyo?*). Staff will ask **몇 시간 이용하실 거예요?** (*myeot sigan iyonghashil geoyeyo?*), "How many hours will you use?" — answer with a number plus **시간이요** (*siganiyo*): **두 시간이요** (*du siganiyo*), "Two hours." You will receive a locker key and a cubicle number on a slip.

You: **자리 있어요?** — *jari isseoyo?* — "Is there a seat?"  
Them: **네, 몇 시간 이용하실 거예요?** — *ne, myeot sigan iyonghashil geoyeyo?* — "Yes, how many hours?"  
You: **두 시간이요.** — *du siganiyo.* — "Two hours."

## What are the unwritten rules inside?

Most 스터디카페 rooms follow **독서실** (*dokseosil*, reading-room) rules — no phone calls, no video without headphones, no food beyond sealed snacks. The sign by the door often reads **통화는 밖에서** (*tonghwaneun bakkeseo*), "Phone calls outside." Respect it or staff will appear with a quiet nod.

If you need to print before leaving, ask **프린터 쓸 수 있어요?** (*peurinteo sseul su isseoyo?*), "Can I use the printer?" Most desks run a card reader for per-page printing. Just tap the screen at the printer station and follow the Korean menu — it mirrors the same icons as most office printers.

## What do you do when your time is almost up?

Watch for the countdown on the desk screen. To extend, go back to the kiosk and say **시간 더 추가할게요** (*sigan deo chugahalgeyo*), "I will add more time." If you are done, pack up and leave — no checkout is needed at most cafes, as the system logs your exit automatically.

For late-night sessions, the vending machine near the entrance handles coffee. Point and say **이거 하나요** (*igeo hanayo*), "One of this, please," and you are covered without breaking silence.
