A fan coffee truck parked outside a filming set is one of the warmest things a fandom does, and almost all of it happens in a short message thread. **커피차 보내고 싶어요** (*keopicha bonaego sipeoyo*), "I'd like to send a coffee truck," opens it, and **문구 이렇게 해주세요** (*mungu ireoke haejuseyo*), "Please make the banner wording like this," is the line that keeps your message from being paraphrased into something else.

> - Open the request: **커피차 보내고 싶어요** (*keopicha bonaego sipeoyo*), "I'd like to send a coffee truck."
> - Get the location: **촬영장 주소 알려주세요** (*chwaryeongjang juso allyeojuseyo*), "Please tell me the filming location address."
> - Set the amount: **몇 잔 준비해요?** (*myeot jan junbihaeyo?*), "How many cups should we prepare?"
> - Fix the banner: **문구 이렇게 해주세요** (*mungu ireoke haejuseyo*), "Please make the wording like this."
> - Close it warmly: **잘 부탁드립니다** (*jal butakdeurimnida*), "I'm counting on you — thank you."

## How does a fan coffee truck actually get booked?

The shops that run these trucks answer dozens of fan messages a week, so the requests that get handled first are the ones that arrive complete.

1. Start with **커피차 보내고 싶어요** and the date — that alone tells them everything about what kind of job it is.
2. Ask **촬영장 주소 알려주세요**, because sets move and the address in the fan chat is often last week's.
3. **몇 잔 준비해요?** settles the size of the order before anything gets designed.
4. Send your banner text with **문구 이렇게 해주세요** — spelling of the show and the name matters more than the design.
5. Finish with **잘 부탁드립니다**, the standard close that leaves the thread on good terms.

## What do Korean speakers say back?

Coffee truck shops write like any other small business — short, direct, and happy to guide a first-timer through it.

You: **커피차 보내고 싶어요.** — *keopicha bonaego sipeoyo.* — "I'd like to send a coffee truck."  
Them: **네, 날짜랑 장소 알려주시면 확인해 드릴게요** — *ne, naljjarang jangso allyeojusimyeon hwaginhae deurilgeyo* — "Sure, send me the date and place and I'll check for you."  
You: **문구 이렇게 해주세요.** — *mungu ireoke haejuseyo.*

If they ask for the banner text again, sending it as plain typed Korean rather than an image saves a round of corrections. For visiting the same kind of set as a fan, see [Korean phrases for a K-drama filming location tour in Seoul](/korean-phrases-kdrama-filming-location-tour-seoul).

## What usually goes wrong with a fan support order?

The most common mistake is describing the banner instead of writing it. "Something with the drama name and our fandom" comes back looking nothing like what the group agreed on — **문구 이렇게 해주세요** followed by the exact line fixes that.

The other slip is confirming the address only once, days ahead. Asking **촬영장 주소 알려주세요** again the morning before is normal and nobody finds it annoying. For the other fan project fandoms run all year, see [Korean phrases at an idol birthday cafe in Seoul](/korean-phrases-idol-birthday-cafe-seoul).
