BigHit Music confirmed on Weverse that BTS returns as a complete seven-member group in September 2026 — the first full-group album since 2022. According to Forbes and Korea Unboxed, pre-orders set a Weverse Shop record within 24 hours of the announcement. When Korean ARMY fans saw the post, group chats lit up with two phrases back to back: **컴백이에요!** (*keombaegieyo!*), "It's a comeback!" and **들었어요?** (*deureosseoyo?*), "Have you heard it?" Those two lines drive every album-drop conversation, no matter which group you follow.

> - Announce the comeback: **컴백이에요!** (*keombaegieyo!*), "It's a comeback!"
> - Ask if they heard it: **들었어요?** (*deureosseoyo?*), "Have you heard it?"
> - Suggest listening together: **같이 들어요** (*gachi deureoyo*), "Let's listen together."
> - React to a great moment: **진짜 좋아요** (*jinjja joayo*), "It's really good."
> - Ask about the title track: **타이틀곡 어때요?** (*taiteulgok eottaeyo?*), "How is the title track?"

## What do Korean fans say when a comeback is announced?

The announcement phase is where the vocabulary runs fastest. Korean fans use a short stack of words that non-Korean speakers often only see in subtitles:

**컴백이에요!** is the declaration — any text thread that starts with it means something new is confirmed. Once the album date is set, people start asking:

**예약했어요?** — *yeyakhaesseoyo?* — "Did you pre-order?"

And then the countdown language kicks in:

**기대돼요** — *gidaedwaeyo* — "I am excited / I cannot wait."

| Korean (*romanization*) | When you say it |
|---|---|
| **컴백이에요!** (*keombaegieyo!*) | First text when a release is confirmed |
| **예약했어요?** (*yeyakhaesseoyo?*) | After the pre-order link goes live |
| **기대돼요** (*gidaedwaeyo*) | During the wait before the drop |
| **드디어!** (*deudieo!*) | "Finally!" — the moment it releases |

## How do you react to the music with Korean friends?

On album drop day, the conversation shifts from anticipation to reaction. If you have already listened, open with:

**들었어요?** — *deureosseoyo?* — "Have you heard it?"

Then keep going with short reactions:

**이 파트 진짜 좋아요** — *i pateu jinjja joayo* — "This part is really good."

**타이틀곡 어때요?** opens a longer discussion — Korean fans debate title track choices the same way sports fans debate starting lineups.

## What do you say to set up a listening session?

If you want to listen together — over a call, in person, or synced on a streaming countdown — the phrase is:

**같이 들어요** — *gachi deureoyo* — "Let's listen together."

You can add a time:

**지금 같이 들을까요?** — *jigeum gachi deureulkkayo?* — "Should we listen together right now?"

When a standout moment hits — a high note, a lyrics line, a production detail — say **대박이에요** (*daebagieyo*) and you will get an immediate reaction from any Korean fan in the room.

You: **들었어요? 타이틀곡 어때요?** — *deureosseoyo? taiteulgok eottaeyo?* — "Did you hear it? How is the title track?"  
Them: **진짜 좋아요! 같이 들어요.** — *jinjja joayo! gachi deureoyo.* — "It's really good! Let's listen together."  
You: **드디어!** — *deudieo!* — "Finally!"

## Do these phrases only work for BTS comebacks?

Not at all. **컴백이에요!**, **들었어요?**, **같이 들어요** — these run in the same pattern every time any group releases new music. Whether you follow BLACKPINK, SEVENTEEN, aespa, or a rookie group on debut, the comeback vocabulary is the same.
