Korean phrases for asking about Wi-Fi in Korea come down to two short lines. Say **와이파이 있어요?** (*waipai isseoyo?*), "Is there Wi-Fi?" when you sit down at a cafe or check into a guesthouse, then **비밀번호 알려 주세요** (*bimilbeonho allyeo juseyo*), "Please tell me the password," when they say yes. Pattern 03 in the SULSUL workbook — noun plus **있어요?** (*isseoyo?*) — handles the first question, and Pattern 23 — verb stem plus **주세요** (*juseyo*) — covers the second.

> - **와이파이 있어요?** (*waipai isseoyo?*) — "Is there Wi-Fi?"
> - **비밀번호 알려 주세요** (*bimilbeonho allyeo juseyo*) — "Please tell me the password."
> - **와이파이 비밀번호 뭐예요?** (*waipai bimilbeonho mwoyeyo?*) — "What is the Wi-Fi password?"
> - **여기서 써도 돼요?** (*yeogiseo sseodo dwaeyo?*) — "Can I use it here?"

## How do you ask at a cafe counter?

Most Seoul cafes have guest Wi-Fi, but the password is rarely posted where you can see it from the door. Order first, then ask while you are paying or picking up your drink.

You: **안녕하세요. 와이파이 있어요?** — *annyeonghaseyo. waipai isseoyo?* — "Hello. Is there Wi-Fi?"  
Them: **네, 있어요.** — *ne, isseoyo.* — "Yes, there is."  
You: **비밀번호 알려 주세요.** — *bimilbeonho allyeo juseyo.* — "Please tell me the password."

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Is there Wi-Fi? | 와이파이 있어요? (*waipai isseoyo?*) |
| Tell me the password | 비밀번호 알려 주세요 (*bimilbeonho allyeo juseyo*) |
| What is the password? | 와이파이 비밀번호 뭐예요? (*waipai bimilbeonho mwoyeyo?*) |
| Can I use it here? | 여기서 써도 돼요? (*yeogiseo sseodo dwaeyo?*) |
| It is not connecting | 연결이 안 돼요 (*yeongyeori an dwaeyo*) |

## What does staff say when you ask?

Staff often point at a small card on the counter or write the password on your receipt. Catch **카운터** (*kaunteo*, counter) or **영수증** (*yeongsujeung*, receipt) if they mention where to look.

You: **와이파이 비밀번호 뭐예요?** — *waipai bimilbeonho mwoyeyo?* — "What is the Wi-Fi password?"  
Them: **영수증 뒤에 있어요.** — *yeongsujeung dwie isseoyo.* — "It is on the back of the receipt."  
You: **감사합니다.** — *gamsahamnida.* — "Thank you."

If the network name is unusual, repeat it once: **네트워크 이름이 뭐예요?** (*neteuwokeu ireumi mwoyeyo?*), "What is the network name?" For ordering at the same counter, see [Korean phrases for ordering iced drinks at a Seoul cafe](/korean-phrases-korean-cafe-summer-drinks).

## What usually goes wrong?

Travelers often skip the existence question and jump straight to **password** in English. Staff understand, but you miss a clean two-step pattern. Another mistake is asking before you order — some small shops only share Wi-Fi with paying customers, so ask right after **이거 주세요** (*igeo juseyo*), not from the doorway.

You: **연결이 안 돼요.** — *yeongyeori an dwaeyo.* — "It is not connecting."  
Them: **다시 한번 해보세요.** — *dasi hanbeon haeboseyo.* — "Try once more."  
You: **아, 됐어요. 감사합니다.** — *a, dwaesseoyo. gamsahamnida.* — "Oh, it works. Thank you."

A second slip is typing the password wrong — Korean passwords sometimes mix letters and numbers that look alike. Ask them to **써 주세요** (*sso juseyo*), "please write it," on a napkin if the connection keeps failing.
