Korean phrases for asking about Wi-Fi in Korea start with two questions. Say **와이파이 있어요?** (*waipai isseoyo?*), "Do you have Wi-Fi?" and follow with **와이파이 비밀번호가 뭐예요?** (*waipai bimilbeonhoga mwoyeyo?*), "What is the Wi-Fi password?" Those two lines handle every cafe, guesthouse, and restaurant in Seoul.

> - Ask if there is Wi-Fi: **와이파이 있어요?** (*waipai isseoyo?*), "Do you have Wi-Fi?"
> - Ask for the password: **와이파이 비밀번호가 뭐예요?** (*waipai bimilbeonhoga mwoyeyo?*), "What is the Wi-Fi password?"
> - Say you cannot connect: **연결이 안 돼요** (*yeongyeori an dwaeyo*), "I cannot connect."
> - Ask if it is free: **무료예요?** (*muryoyeyo?*), "Is it free?"

## How do you ask for the Wi-Fi password in Korean?

Korean cafes almost always post the password on the menu board or counter, but smaller spots keep it verbal. Lead with the shorter check — **와이파이 있어요?** — so you know it exists before asking for the code.

You: **저기요, 와이파이 있어요?** — *jeogiyo, waipai isseoyo?* — "Excuse me, do you have Wi-Fi?"  
Them: **네, 있어요. 비밀번호가 seoul1234예요.** — *ne, isseoyo. bimilbeonhoga seoul1234yeyo.* — "Yes. The password is seoul1234."  
You: **감사합니다!** — *gamsahamnida!* — "Thank you!"

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Check if Wi-Fi exists | 와이파이 있어요? (*waipai isseoyo?*) |
| Get the password | 와이파이 비밀번호가 뭐예요? (*waipai bimilbeonhoga mwoyeyo?*) |
| Cannot connect | 연결이 안 돼요 (*yeongyeori an dwaeyo*) |
| Ask if it is free | 무료예요? (*muryoyeyo?*) |
| Ask for help | 도와주세요 (*dowajuseyo*) |

## What do you say when you cannot connect?

Say **연결이 안 돼요** to the staff and they will reset the router or give you a second password for guests. If you need to narrow down the problem, add **다시 한 번 알려주세요** (*dasi han beon allyeojuseyo*), "Please tell me again," and write the password as they speak.

## Where else can you ask for Wi-Fi in Korea?

The same two phrases work at guesthouses, PC bangs, hotel lobbies, and convenience stores. At a hotel front desk, swap the opener: **와이파이 비밀번호 주세요** (*waipai bimilbeonho juseyo*), "Please give me the Wi-Fi password," drops the question form and lands softer at a check-in counter. At a convenience store like GS25, look for a QR code near the register — no phrase needed — but the opener above still works if you cannot find it.

Knowing a few extra words helps when the staff reads the password aloud: **숫자** (*sutja*, number), **영어** (*yeongeeo*, English letter), **대문자** (*daemuna*, uppercase).
