**Achim** (아침) is the Korean word for **morning**, and it is also the everyday word for **breakfast**. Korean doesn't need a separate word because the morning meal is simply "the morning": **아침 먹었어요?** (*a-chim meo-geo-sseo-yo?*) means "Did you eat breakfast?", while **아침에 만나요** (*a-chi-me man-na-yo*) means "Let's meet in the morning." The verb next to it tells you which one is meant. When you travel, you'll meet one more breakfast word, **조식** (*jo-sik*), which is what hotels and guesthouses print on signs and booking pages.

> - **아침** (*a-chim*) — morning / breakfast
> - **아침 먹었어요?** (*a-chim meo-geo-sseo-yo?*) — "Did you have breakfast?"
> - **조식 포함이에요?** (*jo-sik po-ham-i-e-yo?*) — "Is breakfast included?"
> - **조식 몇 시부터예요?** (*jo-sik myeot si-bu-teo-ye-yo?*) — "From what time is breakfast?"
> - **이 근처에 아침 먹을 데 있어요?** (*i geun-cheo-e a-chim meo-geul de i-sseo-yo?*) — "Is there a place for breakfast around here?"

## How one word means both morning and breakfast

Korean meal words follow the clock. **아침** is morning and the morning meal, **점심** (*jeom-sim*) is midday and lunch, and **저녁** (*jeo-nyeok*) is evening and dinner. So the same trick works three times:

| Word | As a time | As a meal |
|---|---|---|
| 아침 | 아침에 일어나요 — "I get up in the morning" | 아침 먹어요 — "I eat breakfast" |
| 점심 | 점심에 만나요 — "Let's meet at lunchtime" | 점심 먹었어요? — "Did you have lunch?" |
| 저녁 | 저녁에 전화할게요 — "I'll call in the evening" | 저녁 같이 먹어요 — "Let's have dinner together" |

The rule of thumb: if **아침** is followed by **에** (*e*, "at/in"), it's the time. If it's followed by a verb about eating, **먹다** or **하다**, it's the meal. You'll also hear **아침밥** (*a-chim-ppap*), literally "morning rice," which leaves no doubt that you mean food. Notice the **ㅂ** in **밥** turns tense here, so it sounds like *ppap*, not *bap*.

A third word, **아침 식사** (*a-chim sik-sa*), is the polite, slightly formal "breakfast meal." Staff use it with guests: **아침 식사하셨어요?** (*a-chim sik-sa-ha-syeo-sseo-yo?*), "Have you had breakfast?"

## Pronouncing achim so it doesn't sound like a sneeze

Romanized as *achim*, it's tempting to say "a-CHEEM" with a hard English *ch* and stress on the end. Korean keeps both syllables flat and even: *a-chim*. The **ㅊ** is a breathy *ch*, and the final **ㅁ** closes your lips.

Things change when a vowel follows. **ㅁ** slides forward into the next syllable:

- **아침에** → *a-chi-me* ("in the morning")
- **아침을** → *a-chi-meul* (breakfast, as an object)
- **아침이에요** → *a-chi-mi-e-yo* ("It's morning")

If you've heard **좋은 아침** in a K-drama and wondered why it sounded like *jo-eun a-chim*, that's the same word. The everyday greeting side is covered in [how to say good morning in Korean](/how-to-say-good-morning-in-korean). This post is about the food.

## Breakfast at your hotel: the word 조식

On a booking site or a lobby sign, breakfast is almost never **아침**. It's **조식**, a Sino-Korean word (**조** = morning, **식** = meal). You'll see it in these combinations:

| On the sign | Say it | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 조식 포함 | *jo-sik po-ham* | Breakfast included |
| 조식 불포함 | *jo-sik bul-po-ham* | Breakfast not included |
| 조식 뷔페 | *jo-sik bwi-pe* | Breakfast buffet |
| 조식 시간 | *jo-sik si-gan* | Breakfast hours |
| 조식권 | *jo-sik-kkwon* | Breakfast voucher |

Asking about it uses Pattern 05 from the SULSUL workbook, noun + **이에요/예요**, turned into a question: **조식 포함이에요?** (*jo-sik po-ham-i-e-yo?*), "Is breakfast included?" For the hours, combine Pattern 15 (question words) with Pattern 27, **부터 / 까지** ("from / until"):

- **조식 몇 시부터예요?** (*jo-sik myeot si-bu-teo-ye-yo?*) — "From what time is breakfast?"
- **조식 몇 시까지예요?** (*jo-sik myeot si-kka-ji-ye-yo?*) — "Until what time is breakfast?"
- **조식 어디서 먹어요?** (*jo-sik eo-di-seo meo-geo-yo?*) — "Where do we eat breakfast?"

The answer will often come back as a range: **일곱 시부터 열 시까지예요** (*il-gop si-bu-teo yeol si-kka-ji-ye-yo*), "From seven to ten." Listen for **부터** and **까지** and you've caught the important part even if the numbers fly by. More front-desk lines are in [how to check into a hotel in Korean](/check-into-hotel-korean).

## Finding breakfast outside the hotel

Here's what surprises many visitors: plenty of Korean restaurants don't open until late morning, and the classic sit-down breakfast café is less common than in the US or Europe. Koreans who eat breakfast out usually go to one of four places:

- **편의점** (*pyeo-nui-jeom*) — the convenience store: triangle **삼각김밥** (*sam-gak-gim-bap*), sandwiches, banana milk.
- **김밥집** (*gim-bap-jjip*) — gimbap shops, many open early for commuters. See [ordering gimbap to go](/korean-phrases-gimbap-to-go-korea).
- **국밥집 / 해장국집** (*guk-bap-jjip / hae-jang-guk-jjip*) — rice-and-soup places, some open all night.
- **빵집** (*ppang-jjip*) — bakeries, which pair well with coffee. More in [Korean at a bakery](/korean-phrases-bakery-korea).

To ask a local where to go, use Pattern 03, noun + **있어요?**, "Is there ___?":

- **이 근처에 아침 먹을 데 있어요?** — "Is there a place to eat breakfast around here?"
- **지금 문 연 식당 있어요?** (*ji-geum mun yeon sik-dang i-sseo-yo?*) — "Is there a restaurant open right now?"

At the door of a restaurant early in the morning, check before you sit down:

- **지금 식사 돼요?** (*ji-geum sik-sa dwae-yo?*) — "Can I eat now?"

**돼요?** is the all-purpose "is it possible?" If they're still prepping, you'll hear **아직 준비 중이에요** (*a-jik jun-bi jung-i-e-yo*), "We're still getting ready."

## A morning exchange at the front desk

> **You:** 안녕하세요. 조식 포함이에요?
> *an-nyeong-ha-se-yo. jo-sik po-ham-i-e-yo?*
> "Hello. Is breakfast included?"
>
> **Staff:** 네, 포함이에요. 이 층 식당에서 드시면 돼요.
> *ne, po-ham-i-e-yo. i cheung sik-dang-e-seo deu-si-myeon dwae-yo.*
> "Yes, it is. You can eat at the restaurant on the second floor."
>
> **You:** 몇 시까지예요?
> *myeot si-kka-ji-ye-yo?*
> "Until what time?"
>
> **Staff:** 열 시까지예요.
> *yeol si-kka-ji-ye-yo.*
> "Until ten."
>
> **You:** 감사합니다!
> *gam-sa-ham-ni-da!*
> "Thank you!"

Notice **이 층** (*i cheung*): here **이** is the number two, not "this." Floors use Sino-Korean numbers, so **일 층** is the first floor and **이 층** the second.

## 아침 먹었어요? is often not a real question

When a Korean coworker, host, or friend asks **아침 먹었어요?** before ten in the morning, it's usually a greeting, the morning version of "How are you?" A short answer is perfect. This is Pattern 08, the past tense **았/었어요**:

- **네, 먹었어요.** (*ne, meo-geo-sseo-yo.*) — "Yes, I ate."
- **아직 안 먹었어요.** (*a-jik an meo-geo-sseo-yo.*) — "Not yet." (Pattern 07 adds **안** for "not.")
- **아침은 잘 안 먹어요.** (*a-chi-meun jal an meo-geo-yo.*) — "I don't really eat breakfast."

Be ready: "not yet" with an older Korean host can lead to food appearing in front of you. Then just say **잘 먹겠습니다** (*jal meok-ge-sseum-ni-da*) before you start.

## Mistakes to avoid

**Saying 아침 when the sign says 조식.** Staff will understand either, but if you're scanning a booking page for "breakfast included," search for **조식**, not **아침**.

**Reading 아침에 as "at breakfast."** **아침에 만나요** is "see you in the morning," not "see you at breakfast." For a meal plan, say **아침 같이 먹어요** (*a-chim ga-chi meo-geo-yo*), "Let's have breakfast together."

**Heading out at 7 a.m. for a sit-down meal.** Many places are still closed. Ask the front desk first, or go straight to a convenience store or gimbap shop.

**Stressing the second syllable.** *a-CHEEM* sounds off. Keep it level: *a-chim*.
