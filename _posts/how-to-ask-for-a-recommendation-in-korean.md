The simplest way to ask for a recommendation in Korean is **추천해 주세요** (*chu-cheon-hae ju-se-yo*), which means "Please recommend something." It works anywhere: a restaurant, a cosmetics shop, a bookstore, a hotel front desk. At a restaurant, though, most Koreans would skip the word "recommend" and just ask **뭐가 맛있어요?** (*mwo-ga ma-si-sseo-yo*), "What's good here?" Both are polite enough for staff and strangers, and both get you a real answer instead of a shrug.

> - **추천해 주세요.** (*chu-cheon-hae ju-se-yo*) — "Please recommend something."
> - **뭐가 맛있어요?** (*mwo-ga ma-si-sseo-yo*) — "What's good here?"
> - **뭐 추천해 주실 수 있어요?** (*mwo chu-cheon-hae ju-sil su i-sseo-yo*) — "Could you recommend something?" Extra polite.
> - **제일 잘 나가는 게 뭐예요?** (*je-il jal na-ga-neun ge mwo-ye-yo*) — "What's your best seller?"
> - **여기 뭐가 유명해요?** (*yeo-gi mwo-ga yu-myeong-hae-yo*) — "What's this place famous for?"
> - **하나만 골라 주세요.** (*ha-na-man gol-la ju-se-yo*) — "Just pick one for me."

## Why 추천해 주세요 works: the "do it for me" pattern

추천해 주세요 is built from one of the most useful patterns in Korean: a verb + **아/어 주세요**, "please do this for me." The verb here is **추천하다** (*chu-cheon-ha-da*, to recommend). Every 하다 verb turns into **해 주세요**, so 추천하다 becomes 추천해 주세요.

Once you see the pattern, you have a whole family of requests:

- **포장해 주세요.** — "Please pack it to go."
- **확인해 주세요.** — "Please check it."
- **써 주세요.** — "Please write it down."
- **추천해 주세요.** — "Please recommend something."

Put **좀** (*jom*) in front to soften it: **좀 추천해 주세요.** Koreans add 좀 to requests almost automatically, the way English speakers add "just" or "maybe." If you want to sound a little more careful, as with a hotel concierge, swap the ending for **주시겠어요?**: **추천해 주시겠어요?** ("Would you recommend something?").

## At a restaurant: "What's good here?"

Most of the time you'll want a recommendation when you're staring at a wall menu with no pictures. Here, **뭐가 맛있어요?** is the line Koreans really use. It sounds friendly, a bit casual, and it invites the server to point at something.

A few more lines that narrow it down:

- **여기 대표 메뉴가 뭐예요?** — "What's your signature dish?" 대표 메뉴 (*dae-pyo me-nyu*) is the house specialty, and many places print it in a bigger font.
- **처음 왔는데, 뭐 먹으면 돼요?** — "It's my first time here. What should I get?" Saying 처음 왔어요 makes staff explain more slowly.
- **안 매운 거 추천해 주세요.** — "Please recommend something that isn't spicy."
- **두 명이서 먹을 만한 거 있어요?** — "Anything good for two people to share?"

If you have a food allergy or don't eat meat, say that first and then ask. For those lines, see [Korean phrases for food allergies and diet restrictions](/korean-phrases-food-allergies-korea). For reading the menu words themselves (찌개, 볶음, 구이), see [how to read a Korean menu](/korean-food-phrases-reading-a-menu).

## In shops and cafes: best sellers and "what's popular"

In a shop, you're usually asking what other people buy. Two words carry this:

- **인기** (*in-gi*) — popularity. **뭐가 제일 인기 많아요?** "What's the most popular?"
- **잘 나가다** (*jal na-ga-da*) — literally "goes out well," meaning "sells well." **요즘 잘 나가는 게 뭐예요?** "What's selling well these days?"

At a K-beauty store, add your need: **건성 피부인데, 뭐 추천해 주세요.** ("I have dry skin. What do you recommend?") At a cafe, try **여기 시그니처 음료가 뭐예요?** ("What's your signature drink?") Many Korean cafes have a 시그니처 (signature) drink and are happy to point you to it. For the rest of the ordering, see [how to order coffee in Korean](/ordering-coffee-in-korean).

## Asking a person, not a business: places to go

You can also ask a local, a guesthouse host, or a taxi driver where to go:

- **이 근처에 맛집 있어요?** — "Is there a good restaurant around here?" 맛집 (*mat-jip*) means "tasty place," and every Korean knows the word.
- **꼭 가 봐야 하는 곳 있어요?** — "Is there a place I really have to see?"
- **현지인들이 가는 데 추천해 주세요.** — "Please recommend a place locals go."

Start with **저기요** or **실례지만** ("excuse me") before you ask a stranger. For more on getting someone's attention politely, see [how to politely ask for help in Korean](/politely-ask-for-help-in-korean).

## Understanding the answer

The tricky part isn't asking. It's catching the reply. Listen for these:

- **이거 많이 드세요.** — "People order this a lot." 많이 드세요 here means "lots of people eat it," not "eat a lot."
- **이게 제일 잘 나가요.** — "This one sells best."
- **이거 괜찮아요.** — "This one's good." 괜찮아요 is a mild yes, often a real recommendation.
- **다 맛있어요.** — "Everything's good." A polite non-answer. Ask again: **그중에 하나만요?** ("Just one of them?")
- **취향에 따라 달라요.** — "Depends on your taste." Follow up with what you like: **달달한 거 좋아해요.** ("I like sweet things.")

If they talk too fast, point and repeat: **이거요?** ("This one?") That single word confirms the dish without a whole sentence.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Saying 추천 alone.** "추천?" with a rising tone sounds like you're repeating a word, not asking. Add 해 주세요 or ask 뭐 추천해요?
- **Using 추천하세요.** That means "Recommend it!" as a command, or "You recommend things." The request ending is 해 주세요.
- **Translating "What do you suggest?" word for word.** 제안하다 is "suggest" in a business sense, like a proposal in a meeting. For food and shopping, stick with 추천.
- **Forgetting to answer.** After a recommendation, say **그걸로 주세요** ("I'll have that, please") or **그거 하나 주세요.** For more on that ending, see [주세요 and when to add 좀](/how-to-say-please-give-me-in-korean).

## A short scene: first time at a 국밥 restaurant

> **You:** 저기요, 처음 왔는데 뭐가 맛있어요?
> *(Excuse me, it's my first time here. What's good?)*
>
> **Server:** 순대국밥 제일 많이 드세요.
> *(Most people get the sundae gukbap.)*
>
> **You:** 많이 매워요?
> *(Is it very spicy?)*
>
> **Server:** 아니요, 안 매워요. 다대기는 따로 드려요.
> *(No, it's not spicy. The chili paste comes on the side.)*
>
> **You:** 좋아요. 그걸로 두 개 주세요.
> *(Great. Two of those, please.)*

Notice that the server never says the word 추천. In Korea, a recommendation often comes as "most people eat this," so listen for **많이 드세요** and **제일 잘 나가요** as your answer.

## Say it before you need it

These lines only help if they come out without thinking when the server is standing over your table. Say **추천해 주세요**, **뭐가 맛있어요?**, and **그걸로 주세요** out loud a few times, as one small conversation, until the rhythm feels natural.
