A **고시원** (*gosiwon*, budget single-person room) is the most common long-stay option in Korean cities for students, solo travelers, and working-holiday visitors. Pattern 03 from the SULSUL workbook — **있어요?** (*isseoyo?*), "Is there / Do you have?" — covers most desk questions. Pattern 12 — verb plus **ㄹ/을 수 있어요?** (*l/eul su isseoyo?*), "Can I?" — handles the check-in itself.

> - Check in: **체크인할 수 있어요?** (*chekeuinhal su isseoyo?*), "Can I check in?"
> - Shared kitchen: **공용 주방 있어요?** (*gongyong jubang isseoyo?*), "Is there a shared kitchen?"
> - Get the key: **열쇠 받을 수 있어요?** (*yeolsoe badeul su isseoyo?*), "Can I get the key?"
> - WiFi password: **와이파이 비밀번호가 뭐예요?** (*waipai bimilbeonhoga mwoyeyo?*), "What is the WiFi password?"
> - Leave luggage: **짐 맡겨도 돼요?** (*jim matgyeodo dwaeyo?*), "May I leave my luggage?"

## How do you check in at a goshiwon?

Most **고시원** (*gosiwon*) offices are unstaffed after 10 PM. Arrive before then or text the owner using **지금 도착했어요** (*jigeum dochakhaesseoyo*), "I have just arrived." At the desk, lead with your name and Pattern 12 for a fast handover.

You: **안녕하세요, 체크인할 수 있어요?** — *annyeonghaseyo, chekeuinhal su isseoyo?* — "Hello, can I check in?"  
Them: **예약하셨어요?** — *yeyakhasheosseoyo?* — "Did you make a reservation?"  
You: **네, 박지수예요.** — *ne, Bak Jisuyeyo.* — "Yes, I am Park Jisu."

| Situation | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Can I check in? | 체크인할 수 있어요? (*chekeuinhal su isseoyo?*) |
| Is there a shared kitchen? | 공용 주방 있어요? (*gongyong jubang isseoyo?*) |
| Can I get the key? | 열쇠 받을 수 있어요? (*yeolsoe badeul su isseoyo?*) |
| What time is checkout? | 체크아웃 몇 시예요? (*chekeuaut myeot siyeyo?*) |

## What do you ask about in your room?

Pattern 44 — **해도 돼요?** (*haedo dwaeyo?*), "May I?" — is the safest way to ask before touching the AC or leaving a window open. Pattern 03 covers anything missing: **수건 있어요?** (*sugeon isseoyo?*), "Is there a towel?"

You: **에어컨 켜도 돼요?** — *eeokon kyeodo dwaeyo?* — "May I turn on the AC?"  
Them: **네, 마음대로 쓰세요.** — *ne, maeumdaero seuseyo.* — "Yes, feel free."  
You: **감사합니다.** — *gamsahamnida.* — "Thank you."

The bathroom in a **고시원** (*gosiwon*) is usually shared. Knock first and ask **사용 중이에요?** (*sayong jungieyo?*), "Is someone using it?" — just three words from Pattern 03.

## What if something stops working?

Say the problem plainly, then use Pattern 45 — **주실 수 있어요?** (*jusil su isseoyo?*), "Could you?" — to make a polite fix request.

You: **뜨거운 물이 안 나와요.** — *tteugeoun muri an nawayo.* — "The hot water is not coming out."  
Them: **바로 확인할게요.** — *baro hwaginhalgeyo.* — "I will check right away."  
You: **감사합니다, 빨리 부탁드려요.** — *gamsahamnida, ppalli butakdeuryeoyo.* — "Thank you, please hurry."

If the issue cannot be fixed fast, ask **다른 방 있어요?** (*dareun bang isseoyo?*), "Is there another room?" — same Pattern 03, two seconds flat.
