Korean phrases for visiting a Korean temple or historic site focus on three short interactions: buying your ticket, asking what you can and cannot do inside, and understanding a guide's directions. At the gate, say **입장료 얼마예요?** (*ipjangnyo eolmayeyo?*), "How much is the entrance fee?", and follow with **여기서 사요?** (*yeogiseo sayo?*), "Do I buy it here?", if the booth is not obvious. Pattern 06 in the SULSUL workbook — **얼마예요?** (*eolmayeyo?*) — works everywhere from palaces to folk villages.

> - **입장료 얼마예요?** (*ipjangnyo eolmayeyo?*) — "How much is the entrance fee?"
> - **사진 찍어도 돼요?** (*sajin jjigeodo dwaeyo?*) — "Can I take a photo?"
> - **이 건물이 뭐예요?** (*i geonmuri mwoyeyo?*) — "What is this building?"
> - **관람 시간이 언제까지예요?** (*gwallan sigani eonjekajiyeyo?*) — "Until what time is it open?"

## How do you buy a ticket at the entrance gate?

Most major sites — Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Bulguksa — sell tickets at a small booth near the main gate. Staff usually speak some English, but knowing the Korean question gets you a faster and friendlier response.

You: **입장료 얼마예요?** — *ipjangnyo eolmayeyo?* — "How much is the entrance fee?"  
Them: **어른은 3,000원이에요.** — *eoreun-eun samcheonwon-ieyo.* — "For adults it is 3,000 won."  
You: **한 장 주세요.** — *han jang juseyo.* — "One ticket, please."

| Question | Say this |
|---|---|
| Entrance fee | 입장료 얼마예요? (*ipjangnyo eolmayeyo?*) |
| Where to buy | 표 어디서 사요? (*pyo eodiseo sayo?*) |
| Can I take a photo | 사진 찍어도 돼요? (*sajin jjigeodo dwaeyo?*) |
| What is this building | 이 건물이 뭐예요? (*i geonmuri mwoyeyo?*) |

## What do you say to a guide or docent?

Many temples and palaces offer free Korean-language guided tours. Even if your Korean is basic, joining is worth it — you can ask one question and understand the answer more easily than you expect.

You: **한국어 안내 있어요?** — *hangugeo annae isseoyo?* — "Is there a Korean-language tour?"  
Them: **네, 10시에 시작해요.** — *ne, yeolssi-e sijakaeyo.* — "Yes, it starts at 10 o'clock."  
You: **같이 가도 돼요?** — *gachi gado dwaeyo?* — "Can I join?"

If a guide says something you do not catch, **다시 한번 말해 주세요** (*dasi hanbeon malhae juseyo*), "Please say that again," is always welcome. For a sign you cannot read, **이게 뭐예요?** (*ige mwoyeyo?*), "What is this?", covers statues, artifacts, and anything without an English label.

## What are the common rules you need to know about?

Temples that are still active — not just heritage sites — have extra etiquette. Before you enter a main hall, check for **신발 벗으세요** (*sinbal beoseuseyo*), "Please remove your shoes," posted on the door. If you are unsure whether a space is off-limits, ask: **들어가도 돼요?** (*deureo gado dwaeyo?*), "Can I go in?"

Closing time varies by season. Double-check with **관람 시간이 언제까지예요?** (*gwallan sigani eonjekajiyeyo?*) rather than assuming the schedule you saw online is current.
