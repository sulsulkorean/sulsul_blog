Korean phrases for a jjimjilbang (*jjimjilbang*, 찜질방) turn what looks like a labyrinth of hot rooms, cold pools, and sleeping bodies into one of Seoul's most relaxing nights out. Start at the front desk: **수건 어디 있어요?** (*sugeon eodi isseoyo?*), "Where are the towels?" gets you the cotton uniform and a locker key in under thirty seconds. Then ask **찜질방 어떻게 이용해요?** (*jjimjilbang eotteoke iyonghaeyo?*), "How do I use the jjimjilbang?" and the staff will walk you through every sauna room.

> - Get your towel set: **수건 어디 있어요?** (*sugeon eodi isseoyo?*), "Where are the towels?"
> - Understand the layout: **찜질방 어떻게 이용해요?** (*jjimjilbang eotteoke iyonghaeyo?*), "How do I use the jjimjilbang?"
> - Ask about the hottest room: **황토방 어디예요?** (*hwangtobang eodiyeyo?*), "Where is the clay sauna room?"
> - Find the cold pool: **냉탕 어디 있어요?** (*naengtang eodi isseoyo?*), "Where is the cold bath?"
> - Check closing time: **몇 시까지 해요?** (*myeot sikaji haeyo?*), "How late are you open?"

## What do you say at the front desk?

Pay the entrance fee and hand over your shoes; you will get a numbered locker key and a set of cotton shorts and a T-shirt. The locker room is split by gender so confirm with **남자 탈의실 어디예요?** (*namja taryisil eodiyeyo?*), "Where is the men's changing room?" or **여자 탈의실 어디예요?** (*yeoja taryisil eodiyeyo?*), "Where is the women's changing room?" The communal sleeping area, snack bar, and sauna rooms are gender-neutral once you are changed.

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Men's changing room | 남자 탈의실 어디예요? (*namja taryisil eodiyeyo?*) |
| Women's changing room | 여자 탈의실 어디예요? (*yeoja taryisil eodiyeyo?*) |
| Lock your things | 라커 어떻게 잠가요? (*rakeo eotteoke jamgayo?*) |
| Add food to your account | 카드에 추가해 주세요 (*kadeu-e chugahae juseyo*) |

## How do you navigate the sauna rooms?

A jjimjilbang usually has several themed rooms — a clay room (**황토방**, *hwangtobang*), a salt room (**소금방**, *sogeumbang*), and an ice room (**얼음방**, *eoreumbang*) — each kept at a different temperature. Signs are in Korean but there is always a thermometer on the door. When you cannot tell which door leads where, ask:

You: **황토방 어디예요?** — *hwangtobang eodiyeyo?* — "Where is the clay sauna room?"  
Them: **저쪽이에요.** — *jeojjogieyo.* — "It is over that way."  
You: **얼마나 있으면 돼요?** — *eolmana isseumyeon dwaeyo?* — "How long should I stay in?"  
Them: **십오 분 정도요.** — *sibo bun jeongdoyo.* — "About fifteen minutes."

**얼마나 있으면 돼요?** (*eolmana isseumyeon dwaeyo?*) is a great general phrase for any timed situation — use it for the hot tub, the steam room, or the charcoal sauna. For more bathroom-direction phrases, the post on [how to ask where the restroom is in Korean](/ask-restroom-korean) uses the same **어디 있어요?** (*eodi isseoyo?*) pattern.

## Can you order food inside?

Most jjimjilbangs have a snack bar that serves **식혜** (*sikhye*, sweet rice drink), **계란** (*gyeran*, boiled eggs), and ramen. You link everything to your locker number so there is no wallet to carry. To order:

**라면 하나 주세요** (*ramyeon hana juseyo*), "One ramen, please" — then give your locker number when asked.

**맥주 있어요?** (*maekju isseoyo?*) asks "Do you have beer?" — many jjimjilbangs do sell it in the snack area.

If you want to stay overnight — and many people do, spread across the big sleeping mat area — confirm: **오늘 밤 자고 가도 돼요?** (*oneul bam jago gado dwaeyo?*), "Can I sleep here tonight?" The night rate is usually a small extra charge added to your locker account.
