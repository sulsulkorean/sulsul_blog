When Soompi and the Korea Tourism Organization reported the spike in Jeju Island visitors after the 2025 tvN drama 폭싹 속았수다 (*When Life Gives You Tangerines*, starring IU and Park Bo-gum), it was clear that K-drama tourism had become its own travel category. Fans were not just watching — they were showing up at filming spots and needing Korean to talk to the people there.

> - Check if it is the right spot: **여기가 맞아요?** (*yeogiga majayo?*), "Is this the right place?"
> - Ask if a drama was filmed here: **여기서 드라마 찍었어요?** (*yeogiseo deurama jjigeosseoyo?*), "Was the drama filmed here?"
> - Ask where a scene was shot: **이 장면이 어디서 찍었어요?** (*i jangmyeoni eodiseo jjigeosseoyo?*), "Where was this scene filmed?"
> - Ask someone to take your photo: **사진 찍어 주실 수 있어요?** (*sajin jjigeo jusil su isseoyo?*), "Could you take a photo of me?"

## How do you ask if you are at the right filming spot?

K-drama filming locations are often unmarked. The map on your phone gets you close, but the last fifty meters need a local. Use **여기가 맞아요?** (*yeogiga majayo?*) — "Is this the right place?" — as a yes/no opener. Locals who recognize the drama usually answer with a nod and point, which is enough.

You: **저기요, 여기서 드라마 찍었어요?** — *jeogiyo, yeogiseo deurama jjigeosseoyo?* — "Excuse me, was the drama filmed here?"  
Them: **네, 맞아요. 그 카페 장면이요.** — *ne, majayo. geu kape jangmyeoniyo.* — "Yes, that's right. The cafe scene."  
You: **와, 신기해요! 사진 찍어도 돼요?** — *wa, singihaeyo! sajin jjigeodo dwaeyo?* — "Wow, amazing! Is it okay to take a photo?"

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Check you are at the right spot | 여기가 맞아요? (*yeogiga majayo?*) |
| Ask if a drama was filmed there | 여기서 드라마 찍었어요? (*yeogiseo deurama jjigeosseoyo?*) |
| Ask where a specific scene was | 이 장면이 어디서 찍었어요? (*i jangmyeoni eodiseo jjigeosseoyo?*) |
| Ask someone to take your photo | 사진 찍어 주실 수 있어요? (*sajin jjigeo jusil su isseoyo?*) |
| Ask if the cafe in the drama is near | 여기 카페 있어요? (*yeogi kape isseoyo?*) |

## What do you say to a local at a filming location?

If a local stops to chat once they see your camera or drama reference, you can build on the opener. **어느 드라마예요?** (*eoneu deuramayeyo?*) — "Which drama is it?" — shows curiosity and keeps the exchange going. Name the drama title directly: **폭싹 속았수다요** (*poksak soakassudayo*), "It is When Life Gives You Tangerines." Most Koreans recognize the phrase by sound.

## How do you ask someone to take your photo at a filming spot?

**사진 찍어 주실 수 있어요?** (*sajin jjigeo jusil su isseoyo?*) is the polite full form — use it with strangers. Among friends or younger locals, **사진 찍어줘요** (*sajin jjigeojwoyo*) is softer and faster. After the shot, close with **감사합니다** (*gamsahamnida*), "Thank you," or **잘 나왔어요!** (*jal nawasseoyo!*), "It came out well!"
