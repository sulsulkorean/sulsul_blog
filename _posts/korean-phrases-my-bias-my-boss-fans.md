tvN's **My Bias, My Boss** (최애의 사원, *choeaeui sawon*) premiered August 3, 2026, following a K-pop fan who ends up working at her idol's management company. The drama made the word **최애** (*choeae*) — your absolute favorite member — a centerpiece of every episode. Whether you are watching with Korean friends or meeting another fan for the first time, these are the phrases that open the conversation.

> - Ask if someone has a bias: **최애 있어요?** (*choeae isseoyo?*), "Do you have a bias?"
> - Find out who it is: **최애 누구예요?** (*choeae nuguyeyo?*), "Who is your bias?"
> - Say you are a fan: **팬이에요** (*panieyo*), "I am a fan."
> - Suggest watching together: **같이 봐요** (*gachi bwayo*), "Let us watch together."
> - React to a moment: **대박이에요!** (*daebagieyo!*), "This is amazing!"

## What does 최애 actually mean, and when do you use it?

**최애** (*choeae*) is short for 최고로 애정하는 (*choegoreo aejeonganeun*) — "the one I love the most." In K-pop fan culture it means your single favorite member of a group, the one whose parts you rewind, whose solo activities you track, and whose merchandise fills your shelf. Korean fans say it casually in conversation and type it constantly in fan chats.

If you want to tell someone yours, say:

**제 최애는 [name]이에요** — *je choeaeneun [name]ieyo* — "My bias is [name]."

To ask theirs right back: **최애 누구예요?**

| Fan term (*romanization*) | What it means |
|---|---|
| **최애** (*choeae*) | Your number-one favorite member (bias) |
| **입덕** (*ipdeok*) | Falling into the fandom for the first time |
| **덕질** (*deokjil*) | Actively stanning — buying albums, streaming, attending events |
| **직캠** (*jikkaem*) | Fan-filmed focus cut of one member on stage |

## How do you tell a Korean fan you follow the same group?

When you spot another fan — maybe someone with a lightstick on their bag or a photocard on their phone case — the fastest opener is:

**저도 팬이에요!** — *jeodo panieyo!* — "I am a fan too!"

From there, **최애 있어요?** gets the real conversation going. If you are both fans of different members within the same group, expect a lot of **진짜요?** (*jinjjayo?*) — "Really?" — back and forth.

## How do you suggest watching or listening together?

If you want to listen together — on a call, in person, or synced on a streaming countdown — use:

**같이 봐요** — *gachi bwayo* — "Let us watch together."

For audio: swap 봐요 for **들어요** (*deureoyo*) — "Let us listen." When a great moment hits, **대박이에요** (*daebagieyo*) gets an immediate reaction from any Korean fan nearby.

You: **최애 있어요?** — *choeae isseoyo?* — "Do you have a bias?"  
Them: **있어요! 최근 컴백 보셨어요?** — *isseoyo! choegeun keombaek boshyeosseoyo?* — "Yes! Did you see the recent comeback?"  
You: **당연하죠! 같이 봐요 다음 무대.** — *dangyeonhajyo! gachi bwayo daeum mudae.* — "Of course! Let's watch the next stage together."

## Is 최애 only used for K-pop, or can you use it for dramas too?

**최애** works across all Korean entertainment. Drama fans say **최애 드라마** (*choeae deurama*) — "my all-time favorite drama" — just as naturally as K-pop fans say it for a group member. You will also hear **최애 배우** (*choeae baeu*), "favorite actor." Learn the word once and you will spot it everywhere.
