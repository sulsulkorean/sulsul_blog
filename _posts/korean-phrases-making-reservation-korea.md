Making a reservation in Korean takes one opener and three follow-ups. Say **예약하고 싶어요** (*yeyak hago sipeoyo*), "I'd like to make a reservation," then give the date, time, and party size. The staff at most Korean restaurants and guesthouses expects those three details in that exact order — once you know the pattern, the call becomes routine.

> - Open: **예약하고 싶어요** (*yeyak hago sipeoyo*) — "I'd like to make a reservation."
> - Party size: **두 명이요** (*du myeongyo*) — "Two people."
> - Confirm: **예약됐어요?** (*yeyakdwaesseoyo?*) — "Is the reservation confirmed?"
> - Cancel: **예약 취소하고 싶어요** (*yeyak chwiso hago sipeoyo*) — "I'd like to cancel."

## How do you open a reservation call in Korean?

Start with **예약하고 싶어요** (*yeyak hago sipeoyo*). The staff will ask for the date, time, and your name. Answer in short bursts — one piece of information per sentence is easier to process over the phone.

Date and time:

**내일 저녁 7시에 예약하고 싶어요.** — *naeil jeonyeok ilgopsie yeyak hago sipeoyo.* — "I'd like to reserve for tomorrow evening at 7."

Party size:

**세 명이요.** — *se myeongyo.* — "Three people."

Your name:

**이름은 김지수예요.** — *ireumeun gimjisueyeo.* — "My name is Gim Jisu."

## What do you say when the staff asks follow-up questions?

Two questions come back almost every time:

| Staff asks | Your answer |
|---|---|
| 몇 명이에요? (*myeot myeongieyo?*) "How many people?" | 두 명이요 (*du myeongyo*) "Two." |
| 성함이 어떻게 되세요? (*seonghami eotteoke doeseyo?*) "Your name?" | 이름은 __ 이에요 (*ireumeun __ ieyo*) "My name is __." |
| 몇 시에 오실 건가요? (*myeot sie osil geongayo?*) "What time?" | 7시에 갈게요 (*ilgopsie galgeyo*) "I'll come at 7." |
| 실내 자리 원하세요? (*sillae jari wonhaseyo?*) "Indoor seating?" | 네, 실내로 주세요 (*ne, sillaero juseyo*) "Yes, inside please." |

If you did not catch a question, ask the staff to repeat:

**다시 한번 말씀해 주세요.** — *dasi hanbeon malsseum hae juseyo.* — "Please say that one more time."

You: **예약하고 싶어요. 내일 저녁 7시, 두 명이요.**  
— *yeyak hago sipeoyo. naeil jeonyeok ilgopsie, du myeongyo.*  
— "I'd like to reserve — tomorrow evening at 7, two people."  
Them: **성함이 어떻게 되세요?**  
— *seonghami eotteoke doeseyo?*  
— "May I have your name?"  
You: **박민준이요. 예약됐어요?**  
— *bakminjunyo. yeyakdwaesseoyo?*  
— "Bak Minjun. Is the reservation confirmed?"

## How do you change or cancel a reservation in Korean?

To reschedule, say the old time first, then the new one:

**예약을 바꾸고 싶어요.** — *yeyakeul bakkugo sipeoyo.* — "I'd like to change the reservation."

To cancel entirely:

**예약 취소하고 싶어요.** — *yeyak chwiso hago sipeoyo.* — "I'd like to cancel the reservation."

Give your name and the original date so the staff can pull up the booking quickly.

## Why reservation Korean matters before you arrive in Korea

Popular Korean restaurants fill up fast and many take reservations by phone, KakaoTalk, or Naver Place — not only walk-ins. If you wait until you are at the door, seats for the barbecue spot you planned all week may already be gone.
