Korean phrases at a bookstore in Korea start with Pattern 03 from the SULSUL workbook — **있어요?** (*isseoyo?*), "Do you have…?" The key line is **이 책 있어요?** (*i chaek isseoyo?*), "Do you have this book?" Show the title on your phone if the pronunciation feels uncertain. Pattern 22 — **어디 있어요?** (*eodi isseoyo?*), "Where is it?" — gets you from the information desk to the right shelf without wandering every floor.

> - Ask for a title: **이 책 있어요?** (*i chaek isseoyo?*), "Do you have this book?"
> - Find the section: **어디 있어요?** (*eodi isseoyo?*), "Where is it?"
> - Which floor: **몇 층이에요?** (*myeot cheung-ieyo?*), "Which floor is it on?"
> - Ask the price: **얼마예요?** (*eolmayeyo?*), "How much is it?"

## How do you find a book at a Korean bookstore?

Large chains like **교보문고** (*Gyobo Mungo*, Kyobo Books) and Yes24 Offline have touch-screen kiosks on the ground floor. The search field takes Korean characters — not English — so copy the Korean title or author name from your phone before you arrive. Once the kiosk gives you a shelf code, confirm with a staff member.

You: **이 책 어디 있어요? 3층이에요?** — *i chaek eodi isseoyo? samcheung-ieyo?* — "Where is this book? Is it on the third floor?"  
Them: **아니요, 2층 인문학 코너예요.** — *aniyo, icheung inmunhak koneyeyo.* — "No, it's in the humanities section on the second floor."  
You: **감사해요. 엘리베이터 어디 있어요?** — *gamsahaeyo. ellibeiteo eodi isseoyo?* — "Thank you. Where is the elevator?"

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Find a title | 이 책 있어요? (*i chaek isseoyo?*) |
| Ask the floor | 몇 층이에요? (*myeot cheung-ieyo?*) |
| Find a section | 어디 있어요? (*eodi isseoyo?*) |
| Find the elevator | 엘리베이터 어디 있어요? (*ellibeiteo eodi isseoyo?*) |

## What do you say at the checkout or gift-wrap counter?

Most major bookstores offer free **선물 포장** (*seonmul pojang*, gift wrapping) when you ask at the cashier. The same Pattern 23 — **아/어 주세요** (*a/eo juseyo*), "please do this for me" — that works at the tailor and dry cleaner applies here.

You: **이거 계산할게요. 선물 포장 해 주세요.** — *igeo gyesanhalgeyo. seonmul pojang hae juseyo.* — "I'll pay for this. Please gift-wrap it."  
Them: **영수증 드릴까요?** — *yeongsujeung deurilkkayo?* — "Shall I give you a receipt?"  
You: **네, 주세요.** — *ne, juseyo.* — "Yes, please."

If the receipt shows a different total from the cover price, a loyalty card discount was applied quietly. That's normal — no need to correct it.

## What usually goes wrong for travelers at a Korean bookstore?

The most common mistake is typing the book title in English at the kiosk — the system won't find it. Spend thirty seconds copying the Korean title from a search before you walk in.

A second trap is saying **주세요** (*juseyo*) by itself. It means "please give me" but sounds incomplete without the object. Chain it clearly: **이 책 주세요** (*i chaek juseyo*), "Please give me this book," and the clerk knows exactly what to reach for.

If a staff member's reply comes too fast, **천천히 말해 주세요** (*cheoncheonhi malhae juseyo*) — "Please speak slowly" — resets the conversation anywhere.
