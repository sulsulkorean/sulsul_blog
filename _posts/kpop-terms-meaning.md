Most K-pop terms that confuse new fans are not fan slang at all — they are the industry's own words, the ones Korean TV captions, agencies, and news headlines use every week. The short version: a **타이틀곡** (*taiteulgok*) is the title track, a **수록곡** (*surokgok*) is any other song on the album, **음방** (*eumbang*) is a TV music show, **첫방** (*cheotbang*) and **막방** (*makbang*) are the first and last music-show stages of a promotion, an **엔딩요정** (*ending-yojeong*) is the member caught in the final close-up, and **역주행** (*yeokjuhaeng*) is a song climbing back up the charts long after release. Below is each term in the order a comeback actually happens, with a sentence you can say to a Korean fan.

> - **컴백** (*keombaek*) — any new release and promotion, even if the group never left.
> - **활동** (*hwaldong*) — the promotion period. 이번 활동 = "this promotion."
> - **타이틀곡** (*taiteulgok*) — the title track, the song with the music video.
> - **수록곡** (*surokgok*) — the other songs on the album (what English fans call b-sides).
> - **음방** (*eumbang*) — short for 음악 방송, a TV music show.
> - **막방** (*makbang*) — the last music-show stage of a promotion.
> - **엔딩요정** (*ending-yojeong*) — "ending fairy," the close-up at the end of a stage.
> - **역주행** (*yeokjuhaeng*) — "driving the wrong way": a song charting again later.

## Why English-speaking fans mix these words up

English fandom borrowed a few of these terms and dropped the rest, so you see "comeback" and "b-side" everywhere, but almost never 막방 or 역주행. Meanwhile, Korean fans use the full set without thinking — the words are printed on screen during music shows and in every article about a release.

Two things make them easy once you see the pattern. First, Koreans shorten long phrases to two syllables: 음악 방송 becomes **음방**, 마지막 방송 becomes **막방**, 첫 방송 becomes **첫방**. Second, many terms are borrowed English words said the Korean way: 컴백, 타이틀, 쇼케이스, 엔딩. If you can sound out Hangul, you can already guess half of them.

## Before the release: 소속사, 연습생, and 데뷔

Every K-pop career starts with three words:

- **소속사** (*sosoksa*) — the agency a singer belongs to. 소속 means "belonging," 사 means "company."
- **연습생** (*yeonseupsaeng*) — a trainee. 연습 = practice, 생 = student.
- **데뷔** (*debwi*) — debut. **데뷔조** (*debwijo*) is the lineup of trainees picked to debut together.

You will hear them in sentences like **연습생 기간이 길었대요** (*yeonseupsaeng gigani gireotdaeyo*, "I heard their trainee period was long") or **소속사가 어디예요?** (*sosoksaga eodiyeyo?*, "Which agency are they with?").

Before an album drops, a group may release a **선공개곡** (*seongonggaegok*), a pre-release single. 선 = ahead, 공개 = release, 곡 = song. Then comes the **쇼케이스** (*syokeiseu*), the showcase where the group performs the new songs for the first time.

## The album itself: 타이틀곡, 수록곡, and 음원

The **타이틀곡** is the song the group promotes on TV and films the music video for. Everything else on the album is a **수록곡** — literally "a song recorded on it." Korean fans often love a 수록곡 more than the title track and will say so:

- **저는 타이틀곡보다 수록곡이 더 좋아요.** (*jeoneun taiteulgokboda surokgogi deo joayo.*) — "I like the b-sides more than the title track."
- **3번 트랙 진짜 띵곡이에요.** (*sam-beon teuraek jinjja ttinggogieyo.*) — "Track 3 is a total banger." 띵곡 is slang for 명곡, a great song; the ㅁ is swapped for ㄸ because the letters look alike.

When people talk about streaming and downloads, the word is **음원** (*eumwon*) — the digital track, as opposed to the physical **앨범** (*aelbeom*). "The song did well on streaming" is **음원 성적이 좋아요** (*eumwon seongjeogi joayo*).

## Music show season: 음방, 첫방, 막방

![K-pop fans cheering in the crowd during a music show stage](/assets/blog/scenes/concert.jpg)

A promotion lives on the weekly music shows, and the vocabulary follows the calendar.

- **첫방** (*cheotbang*) — the first music-show stage of a new song. Fans say **첫방 봤어요?** (*cheotbang bwasseoyo?*, "Did you see the first stage?").
- **음방** (*eumbang*) — the music shows in general. **음방 뛰다** (*eumbang ttwida*, literally "run music shows") means to do the music-show circuit.
- **사녹** (*sanok*) — short for 사전 녹화, a pre-recorded stage filmed before the live broadcast. The opposite is **생방** (*saengbang*), a live broadcast.
- **막방** (*makbang*) — the last music-show stage of that promotion. After 막방, the 활동 is over.

A quick warning: 막방 sounds a lot like **먹방** (*meokbang*), the eating-show genre. One vowel apart — 마 versus 머 — so say it clearly or someone will think the group is eating on stage.

## The moments fans clip: 엔딩요정, 직캠, 1위 앵콜

These are the words you will see in video titles the morning after a show.

- **엔딩요정** (*ending-yojeong*) — at the end of a performance, the camera holds on one member for a few seconds. That member is the "ending fairy," and their expression usually goes viral.
- **직캠** (*jikkaem*) — a fancam focused on one member. 직 comes from 직접, "directly."
- **1위** (*irwi*) — first place on a music show. **1위 했어요!** (*irwi haesseoyo!*) — "They won first place!"
- **앵콜 무대** (*aengkol mudae*) — the encore stage performed after a win, often messy, emotional, and full of crying.

## Chart talk: 차트인, 줄 세우기, 역주행

Korean fans follow charts closely, and three terms come up constantly:

- **차트인** (*chateu-in*) — entering the chart. **차트인 했어요** = "It made the chart."
- **줄 세우기** (*jul seugi*) — "lining up": when every track on an album fills the chart in a row.
- **역주행** (*yeokjuhaeng*) — a song that rises again months or years after release, often because an old performance video suddenly gets attention. It is the word for a comeback the group did not plan.

## Using the terms in a conversation

Here is how two friends might talk the week a group's promotion ends.

> **A:** 어제 막방이었지? (*eoje makbang-iyeotji?*) — "Yesterday was their last stage, right?"
> **B:** 응, 엔딩요정 진짜 웃겼어. (*eung, ending-yojeong jinjja utgyeosseo.*) — "Yeah, the ending fairy was so funny."
> **A:** 이번 활동 1위 몇 번 했어? (*ibeon hwaldong irwi myeot beon haesseo?*) — "How many wins did they get this promotion?"
> **B:** 세 번! 근데 나는 수록곡이 더 좋아. (*se beon! geunde naneun surokgogi deo joa.*) — "Three! But I like the b-side more."
> **A:** 그 노래 역주행했으면 좋겠다. (*geu norae yeokjuhaenghaesseumyeon joketda.*) — "I hope that song does a reverse-run."

This is 반말, casual speech between friends. With a Korean fan you have just met, add 요: **막방이었죠?**, **수록곡이 더 좋아요**.

## Where English-speaking fans trip up

1. **Treating 컴백 as "return from a break."** In Korean, a group that released two months ago still has a 컴백 now. It just means new music and a new promotion.
2. **Saying 막방 like 먹방.** Keep the first vowel open: *mak*, not *meok*.
3. **Calling every 수록곡 a "b-side" in Korean.** Koreans do not say 비사이드 much. Use 수록곡.
4. **Writing 엔딩 요정 as two English words.** In Korean it is one compound, 엔딩요정, and it means the moment, not a person's nickname.
5. **Pronouncing 음원 as two hard syllables.** It flows together: *eu-mwon*.
