Seoul's subway system has an impressive lost-and-found record — items left on trains are often collected within one stop and sent to the nearest **분실물 센터** (*bunsilmul senteo*), the lost-and-found center. Get there and say **물건을 잃어버렸어요** (*mulgeoneul ileobeoryeoyo*), "I lost something," and staff will start asking the right questions. Adding what you lost — **지갑을 잃어버렸어요** (*jigabeul ileobeoryeoyo*, "I lost my wallet") or **핸드폰을 잃어버렸어요** (*haendeuponul ileobeoryeoyo*, "I lost my phone") — saves a round of guessing.

> - Report a loss: **물건을 잃어버렸어요** (*mulgeoneul ileobeoryeoyo*), "I lost something."
> - Specify a wallet: **지갑을 잃어버렸어요** (*jigabeul ileobeoryeoyo*), "I lost my wallet."
> - Specify a phone: **핸드폰을 잃어버렸어요** (*haendeuponul ileobeoryeoyo*), "I lost my phone."
> - Name the line: **2호선 안에서요** (*iho-seon aneseoyo*), "Inside Line 2."
> - Ask them to call: **찾으면 연락해 주세요** (*chajumyeon yeollak hae juseyo*), "Please contact me if found."

## Where is the lost-and-found center and how do you get there?

Every Seoul Metro station with a staffed service desk handles initial reports. Major transfer stations — **시청** (*sicheong*), **서울역** (*Seoul-yeok*), **강남** (*Gangnam*) — have a full 분실물 센터 on site. For items lost on a moving train, the central lost-and-found for Seoul Metro Lines 1–8 is at Seoul Station.

You: **분실물 센터 어디예요?** — *bunsilmul senteo eodiyeyo?* — "Where is the lost-and-found center?"  
Them: **저쪽 안내소에 물어보세요.** — *jeojjok annaeso-e mureoboseyo.* — "Please ask at the information desk over there."  
You: **감사합니다.** — *gamsahamnida.* — "Thank you."

If the item just fell off you on the platform, you can also go straight to the nearest ticket booth and ask the same question — staff can radio ahead to the next station.

| Item lost | Say this |
|---|---|
| Wallet | 지갑을 잃어버렸어요 (*jigabeul ileobeoryeoyo*) |
| Phone | 핸드폰을 잃어버렸어요 (*haendeuponul ileobeoryeoyo*) |
| Bag | 가방을 잃어버렸어요 (*gabangeul ileobeoryeoyo*) |
| Umbrella | 우산을 잃어버렸어요 (*usaneul ileobeoryeoyo*) |
| Passport | 여권을 잃어버렸어요 (*yeokkwoneul ileobeoryeoyo*) |

## What questions will staff ask you?

Staff will ask where and when. Two short answers cover both.

You: **물건을 잃어버렸어요. 지갑이에요.** — *mulgeoneul ileobeoryeoyo. jigab-ieyo.* — "I lost something. It is a wallet."  
Them: **어디서 잃어버리셨어요?** — *eodiseo ileobeorisseoyo?* — "Where did you lose it?"  
You: **2호선 열차 안에서요.** — *iho-seon yeolcha aneseoyo.* — "Inside a Line 2 train."

If you do not know the exact line or station, say **모르겠어요** (*moreugesseoyo*), "I am not sure," and show the staff your T-money card — the tap history can help narrow down where you were. They may also ask for your phone number to call you if the item turns up later, so have it ready: **제 번호는...** (*je beonhoneun...*), "My number is..."

## What usually goes wrong when you report a lost item?

The most common mistake is going to the wrong place. Not every station has a full lost-and-found center — smaller stations send you to the nearest major hub. If you lost something on Line 2, go to the Line 2 operations center at City Hall Station rather than the booth at your exit station.

A second trap is waiting too long. Korean subway staff log items quickly, but if your train has already completed its route before you report, the item goes into a central database. File the report at any staffed booth as soon as you realize something is missing — the sooner, the better. For a lost wallet on the street rather than the subway, the phrase **지갑을 잃어버렸어요** works at any police box (**파출소**, *pachulso*) too.
