"Answer" in Korean is **대답** (*dae-dap*) when you mean replying to a person, and **답** (*dap*) when you mean the answer to a question, like on a test or in a message. To answer someone, you **대답하다** (*dae-da-pa-da*). To get the right answer, you find the **정답** (*jeong-dap*). In everyday conversation, though, what you really need is not the word "answer" but the answers themselves: **네** (*ne*, "yes"), **아니요** (*a-ni-yo*, "no"), **맞아요** (*ma-ja-yo*, "that's right"), and **잘 모르겠어요** (*jal mo-reu-ge-sseo-yo*, "I'm not sure").

> - **대답** (*dae-dap*) — an answer or reply to a person. 대답해 주세요, "Please answer."
> - **답** (*dap*) — the answer to a question or problem. 답이 뭐예요?, "What's the answer?"
> - **정답** (*jeong-dap*) — the correct answer. 정답!, "Correct!"
> - **답장** (*dap-jang*) — a reply to a message or letter. 답장 왔어요, "I got a reply."
> - **네 / 아니요** (*ne / a-ni-yo*) — yes / no, the two answers you'll use most.

## 대답 or 답: which "answer" do you mean?

Think of **대답** as the sound of someone responding. A teacher calls a name and the student says "네!" That is a 대답. A friend asks a question and stays silent, and you say **왜 대답 안 해?** (*wae dae-dap an hae?*), "Why aren't you answering?" It is about the act of replying, not about being right.

**답** is the content of the answer. A quiz has a 답. A math problem has a 답. If you ask a hard question and nobody knows, someone might say **답이 없네요** (*da-bi eom-ne-yo*), "There's no answer," which Koreans also use jokingly about a hopeless situation.

Two more words grow from 답 and show up constantly in real life:

- **정답** (*jeong-dap*) — "the right answer." Game shows shout it, and friends say **정답!** when you guess correctly.
- **답장** (*dap-jang*) — "a reply" to a text, email, or letter. **답장 늦어서 죄송해요** (*dap-jang neu-jeo-seo joe-song-hae-yo*), "Sorry for the late reply," is one of the most useful KakaoTalk lines you can learn.

A quick test: if you could replace "answer" with "reply," use 대답 (in speech) or 답장 (in writing). If you could replace it with "solution," use 답.

## How to say yes in Korean

**네** (*ne*) is the polite, everyday yes. It works with strangers, shop staff, teachers, and older people. You'll also hear **예** (*ye*), which is a little more formal and common with older speakers or in service jobs.

But Koreans use 네 for more than agreement. It also means "I'm listening," "got it," or "here I am." When a cashier says your total and you reply 네, you're not saying yes to a question. You're saying "okay." When someone calls your name, 네? with a rising tone means "Yes? What is it?"

To answer a yes-or-no question naturally, repeat the verb after 네. This is how Korean answers sound complete instead of abrupt:

- **A:** 한국 처음이에요? (*han-guk cheo-eu-mi-e-yo?*) — Is this your first time in Korea?
- **B:** **네, 처음이에요.** (*ne, cheo-eu-mi-e-yo*) — Yes, it's my first time.

- **A:** 매운 거 괜찮아요? (*mae-un geo gwaen-cha-na-yo?*) — Is spicy food okay?
- **B:** **네, 괜찮아요.** (*ne, gwaen-cha-na-yo*) — Yes, it's fine.

When you agree that what someone said is correct, add **맞아요** (*ma-ja-yo*): **네, 맞아요**, "Yes, that's right." If a Korean friend says "It's really cold today, isn't it?", the natural reply is **맞아요, 진짜 춥네요** (*ma-ja-yo, jin-jja chum-ne-yo*), "Right, it's really cold."

With close friends, 네 drops to **응** (*eung*) or **어** (*eo*). Keep those for people your age you already talk casually with.

## How to say no in Korean

**아니요** (*a-ni-yo*) is the polite no. In fast speech it often shrinks to **아뇨** (*a-nyo*). Like 네, it sounds much warmer when you add a short follow-up:

- **A:** 봉투 필요하세요? (*bong-tu pi-ryo-ha-se-yo?*) — Do you need a bag?
- **B:** **아니요, 괜찮아요.** (*a-ni-yo, gwaen-cha-na-yo*) — No, I'm okay.

That pair, **아니요, 괜찮아요**, is the smoothest way to decline anything in Korea: a bag, a receipt, a sample, a second helping.

When someone is simply wrong, or when you're brushing off a compliment or a thank-you, use **아니에요** (*a-ni-e-yo*), "It's not," "Not at all." Someone says 감사합니다 and you answer **아니에요~**, the Korean version of "Oh, it's nothing." Someone says your Korean is great and you say **아니에요, 아직 멀었어요** (*a-ni-e-yo, a-jik meo-reo-sseo-yo*), "No, I still have a long way to go."

## The negative-question trap

This is where most English speakers answer wrong, and Koreans will be confused.

In English, you answer based on the fact. In Korean, **네** means "what you said is right" and **아니요** means "what you said is wrong." So when the question itself is negative, the answer flips.

- **A:** 아직 안 먹었어요? (*a-jik an meo-geo-sseo-yo?*) — You haven't eaten yet?
- **B:** **네, 안 먹었어요.** (*ne, an meo-geo-sseo-yo*) — Right, I haven't eaten.
- **B:** **아니요, 먹었어요.** (*a-ni-yo, meo-geo-sseo-yo*) — No, I did eat.

An English speaker wants to say "No, I haven't," but in Korean that becomes **네, 안 먹었어요**. The fix is simple: never answer with 네 or 아니요 alone when the question has **안** or **못** in it. Always repeat the verb, so the meaning is clear even if your yes or no lands the English way.

## When you don't know the answer

Not every question has a yes or no. These are the honest, polite answers for everything in between:

- **잘 모르겠어요** (*jal mo-reu-ge-sseo-yo*) — "I'm not sure." Much softer than the blunt **몰라요** (*mol-la-yo*), "I don't know."
- **글쎄요** (*geul-sse-yo*) — "Well, hmm." Buys you a second, and often hints at a gentle no.
- **아마요** (*a-ma-yo*) — "Probably."
- **다시 한 번 말해 주세요** (*da-si han beon mal-hae ju-se-yo*) — "Please say it one more time." Use it before you answer something you didn't catch.

If a stranger asks you for directions, **죄송해요, 저도 잘 모르겠어요** (*joe-song-hae-yo, jeo-do jal mo-reu-ge-sseo-yo*), "Sorry, I'm not sure either," is the kindest way to say you can't help.

## Common mistakes when answering in Korean

- **Answering with just 네 or 아니요.** It's correct but can sound cold. Add the verb, 괜찮아요, or 맞아요.
- **Using 대답 for a text reply.** You'd say 답장 for messages. 대답 is for spoken replies.
- **Saying 몰라요 to a stranger.** It can sound like "No idea, go away." Reach for 잘 모르겠어요.
- **Saying 응 to someone older.** Casual yes to a shop owner or a friend's parent sounds rude. Stay with 네.
- **Flipping negative questions.** "You don't eat meat?" → **네, 안 먹어요** if you don't.

## Practice answering out loud

Answers have to come fast, so reading them isn't enough. Say each pair until it comes out without thinking: **네, 처음이에요** · **아니요, 괜찮아요** · **네, 맞아요** · **아니에요** · **잘 모르겠어요**. Then practice the trap: have a friend ask "안 먹었어요?" and answer both ways. Once 네 and 아니요 feel automatic, every short conversation in Korea gets easier, from the convenience store counter to a taxi ride.
