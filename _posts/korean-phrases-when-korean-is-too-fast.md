Korean phrases when someone speaks too fast give you a polite pause button. Say **천천히 말해 주세요** (*cheoncheonhi malhae juseyo*), "Please speak slowly," when your brain cannot keep up, or **다시 말해 주세요** (*dasi malhae juseyo*), "Please say it again," when you missed the whole sentence. Pattern 22 in the SULSUL workbook pairs **천천히** (*cheoncheonhi*, slowly) or **다시** (*dasi*, again) with **말해 주세요** (*malhae juseyo*) — the same request ending you use for **포장해 주세요** (*pojanghae juseyo*).

> - **천천히 말해 주세요** (*cheoncheonhi malhae juseyo*) — "Please speak slowly."
> - **다시 말해 주세요** (*dasi malhae juseyo*) — "Please say it again."
> - **잘 못 들었어요** (*jal mot deureosseoyo*) — "I did not hear you well."
> - **잘 모르겠어요** (*jal moreugesseoyo*) — "I do not really understand."

## How do you stop the conversation without freezing?

Open with **죄송하지만** (*joesonghajiman*, "I am sorry, but") — it signals you are listening, not ignoring them. Then use one of the lines above.

You: **죄송하지만, 천천히 말해 주세요.** — *joesonghajiman, cheoncheonhi malhae juseyo.* — "I am sorry, but please speak slowly."  
Them: **아, 네. 이렇게요?** — *a, ne. ireogeyo?* — "Ah, yes. Like this?"  
You: **네, 지금 좋아요.** — *ne, jigeum joayo.* — "Yes, this is good now."

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Speak slowly | 천천히 말해 주세요 (*cheoncheonhi malhae juseyo*) |
| Say it again | 다시 말해 주세요 (*dasi malhae juseyo*) |
| Did not hear | 잘 못 들었어요 (*jal mot deureosseoyo*) |
| Do not understand | 잘 모르겠어요 (*jal moreugesseoyo*) |
| One more time | 한 번 더 말해 주세요 (*han beon deo malhae juseyo*) |

## What do Koreans say when they repeat for you?

Most people naturally slow down and simplify — you do not need to apologize twice. Listen for the same keyword repeated louder, not a whole new explanation.

You: **잘 못 들었어요. 다시 말해 주세요.** — *jal mot deureosseoyo. dasi malhae juseyo.* — "I did not hear you well. Please say it again."  
Them: **2층이에요. 2층.** — *icheung-ieyo. icheung.* — "Second floor. Second floor."  
You: **아, 2층. 감사합니다.** — *a, icheung. gamsahamnida.* — "Ah, second floor. Thank you."

Pattern 21 — **잘 모르겠어요** (*jal moreugesseoyo*) — tells the other person you are stuck without sounding rude. Pair it with Pattern 22 when you need them to retry. For broader help requests, see [how to politely ask for help in Korean](/politely-ask-for-help-in-korean).

## What usually goes wrong?

Learners often nod along when they caught nothing — the other person assumes you understood and speeds up. One honest **잘 모르겠어요** (*jal moreugesseoyo*) resets the pace. Another mistake is saying **몰라요** (*mollayo*, "I don't know") when you mean "I didn't catch that" — **잘 모르겠어요** is softer and fits listening problems.

You: **너무 빨라요.** — *neomu ppallayo.* — "It is too fast."  
Them: **괜찮아요, 천천히 할게요.** — *gwaenchanayo, cheoncheonhi halgeyo.* — "It is fine, I will go slowly."  
You: **감사합니다. 다시 말해 주세요.** — *gamsahamnida. dasi malhae juseyo.* — "Thank you. Please say it again."

A second slip is asking for English immediately. Try Pattern 22 once first — many Koreans will adjust speed and repeat key words without switching languages.
