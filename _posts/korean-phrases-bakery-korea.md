Korean phrases for a **빵집** (*ppangjip*, bakery) pair Pattern 01 — noun plus **주세요** (*juseyo*), "please give me" — with Pattern 02 — verb plus **고 싶어요** (*go sipeoyo*), "I want to." Point at a tray and order with a short phrase, or tell a friend **소금빵 먹고 싶어요** (*sogeumppang meokgo sipeoyo*), "I want to eat a salt bread roll." Most counter staff expect a short order, not a full sentence.

> - Point and order: **이거 주세요** (*igeo juseyo*), "This one, please."
> - Say what you want: **빵 먹고 싶어요** (*ppang meokgo sipeoyo*), "I want to eat bread."
> - Ask for to-go: **포장해 주세요** (*pojanghae juseyo*), "Please wrap it to go."
> - Check if warm: **따뜻한 거 있어요?** (*ttatteuthan geo isseoyo?*), "Do you have a warm one?"
> - Add one more: **하나 더 주세요** (*hana deo juseyo*), "One more, please."

## How do you order at the bakery counter?

Korean bakeries stack **소금빵** (*sogeumppang*), croissants, and filled buns behind glass. Staff watch which tray you point at — say the item plus **주세요** (*juseyo*) or point and add **이거요** (*igeoyo*), "This one," when the name is new to you.

You: **안녕하세요. 이거 주세요.** — *annyeonghaseyo. igeo juseyo.* — "Hello. This one, please."  
Them: **포장이요, 드시고 가세요?** — *pojang-iyo, deusigo gaseyo?* — "To go, or for here?"  
You: **포장해 주세요.** — *pojanghae juseyo.* — "Please wrap it to go."

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| This one, please | 이거 주세요 (*igeo juseyo*) |
| Wrap to go | 포장해 주세요 (*pojanghae juseyo*) |
| Warm item available? | 따뜻한 거 있어요? (*ttatteuthan geo isseoyo?*) |
| One more | 하나 더 주세요 (*hana deo juseyo*) |
| Pay by card | 카드 되요? (*kadeu dwaeyo?*) |

## What if you are choosing with a friend?

Pattern 02 lets you share a craving without building a complex sentence. **크루아상 먹고 싶어요** (*keuruasang meokgo sipeoyo*), "I want to eat a croissant," is enough for your friend to point at the right row.

You: **뭐 먹고 싶어요?** — *mwo meokgo sipeoyo?* — "What do you want to eat?"  
Them: **소금빵요.** — *sogeumppangyo.* — "Salt bread."  
You: **두 개 주세요.** — *du gae juseyo.* — "Two, please."

For a drink to match, **아이스 아메리카노 한 잔 주세요** (*aiseu amerikano han jan juseyo*) from [ordering coffee in Korean](/ordering-coffee-in-korean) fits the same counter rhythm at bakery-cafes.

## What usually goes wrong at a Korean bakery?

Travelers mime "to go" with a bag gesture while the staff already asked **포장이요?** — listening for **포장** (*pojang*, wrap) saves the repeat. A second mistake is assuming every tray is fresh from the oven; **따뜻한 거 있어요?** gets you pointed to the warm rack instead of yesterday's batch.

If you need a bag after paying, **봉투 주세요** (*bongtu juseyo*) from [Korean convenience store phrases](/korean-phrases-convenience-store) works at the same register when bags sit under the counter.
