ATM in Korean is just **에이티엠** (*e-i-ti-em*), the English letters read the Korean way, and it's the word people actually say: **에이티엠 어디 있어요?** (*e-i-ti-em eo-di i-sseo-yo?*), "Where's an ATM?" On signs, bank websites, and the machines themselves you'll also see the formal word **현금인출기** (*hyeon-geum-in-chul-gi*), literally "cash-withdrawal machine," and in bank lobbies the area is labeled **자동화 코너** (*ja-dong-hwa ko-neo*), the "automated corner." Once you're standing at the machine, four screen words do almost all the work: **출금** (withdraw), **조회** (check balance), **비밀번호** (PIN), and **수수료** (fee).

> - **에이티엠 어디 있어요?** (*e-i-ti-em eo-di i-sseo-yo?*) — "Where's an ATM?"
> - **여기 에이티엠 있어요?** (*yeo-gi e-i-ti-em i-sseo-yo?*) — "Is there an ATM here?"
> - **외국 카드 돼요?** (*oe-guk ka-deu dwae-yo?*) — "Do foreign cards work?"
> - **돈 좀 뽑으려고요** (*don jom ppo-beu-ryeo-go-yo*) — "I'm trying to take out some cash."
> - **카드가 안 나와요** (*ka-deu-ga an na-wa-yo*) — "My card won't come out."

## Which word to say: 에이티엠, 현금인출기, or 자동화 코너

Three words point at the same machine, and each one lives in a different place.

**에이티엠** is the spoken word. If you ask a convenience store clerk or a passerby, this is the one they'll understand fastest, and it's the one they'll use back. Say it as four even beats, *e-i-ti-em*, not the clipped English "ay-tee-em."

**현금인출기** is the written word. You'll find it on signage, in map apps, and on the side of the machine. **현금** (*hyeon-geum*) means cash, and **인출** (*in-chul*) means withdrawal. It's worth recognizing, but you rarely need to say it. When it's read aloud, the sounds link up: *hyeon-geu-min-chul-gi*.

**자동화 코너** is what banks call the glass-walled room with the machines, often marked **365** because it stays open after the bank closes. If you're looking for a bank ATM late in the day, that's the sign to scan for.

For searching in a map app, type **ATM** or **현금인출기**. Both work, and the second one pulls up bank branches that don't use the English label.

## Asking where one is, and whether your card works

Finding an ATM is Pattern 04 from the SULSUL workbook: noun + **어디 있어요?** (*eo-di i-sseo-yo?*), "Where is ___?"

- **에이티엠 어디 있어요?** — "Where's an ATM?"
- **은행 어디 있어요?** (*eun-haeng eo-di i-sseo-yo?*) — "Where's a bank?"

Inside a shop or a station, switch to Pattern 03, noun + **있어요?**, "Is there ___?":

- **여기 에이티엠 있어요?** — "Is there an ATM here?"

The bigger question for travelers is whether the machine takes a card from abroad. Not every Korean ATM does. Look for a sticker that says **Global ATM** or **해외카드** (*hae-oe-ka-deu*, "overseas card"). If you can't see one, ask:

- **외국 카드 돼요?** (*oe-guk ka-deu dwae-yo?*) — "Do foreign cards work?"

**돼요?** (*dwae-yo?*) is one of the most useful little words in Korean. It means "does it work / is it possible," and you can attach it to almost anything: **카드 돼요?** (card OK?), **영어 돼요?** (English OK?).

## A real exchange at a convenience store

Many ATMs in Korea sit in the corner of a **편의점** (*pyeo-nui-jeom*), the convenience store. Here's how a quick check with the clerk usually goes.

> **You:** 저기요, 여기 에이티엠 있어요?
> *jeo-gi-yo, yeo-gi e-i-ti-em i-sseo-yo?*
> "Excuse me, is there an ATM here?"
>
> **Clerk:** 네, 저쪽 안에 있어요.
> *ne, jeo-jjok a-ne i-sseo-yo.*
> "Yes, it's in there, over that way."
>
> **You:** 외국 카드 돼요?
> *oe-guk ka-deu dwae-yo?*
> "Do foreign cards work?"
>
> **Clerk:** 음… 해외카드 버튼 있을 거예요. 한번 해 보세요.
> *eum… hae-oe-ka-deu beo-teun i-sseul geo-ye-yo. han-beon hae bo-se-yo.*
> "Hmm… there should be an overseas card button. Give it a try."
>
> **You:** 네, 감사합니다!
> *ne, gam-sa-ham-ni-da!*
> "OK, thank you!"

Notice the clerk's answer, **한번 해 보세요** (*han-beon hae bo-se-yo*), "give it a try." You'll hear it a lot in Korea whenever the honest answer is "probably."

## The words on a Korean ATM screen

Most machines have an **English** or **외국어** (*oe-gu-geo*, "foreign language") button on the first screen. Still, some don't, some switch back to Korean halfway, and some print the receipt only in Korean. These are the words to recognize:

| On the screen | Say it | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 출금 | *chul-geum* | Withdrawal |
| 입금 | *ip-geum* | Deposit |
| 조회 / 잔액 조회 | *jo-hoe / ja-naek jo-hoe* | Balance inquiry |
| 이체 / 송금 | *i-che / song-geum* | Transfer |
| 비밀번호 | *bi-mil-beon-ho* | PIN (literally "secret number") |
| 금액 | *geu-maek* | Amount |
| 수수료 | *su-su-ryo* | Fee |
| 확인 | *hwa-gin* | Confirm / OK |
| 취소 | *chwi-so* | Cancel |
| 정정 | *jeong-jeong* | Clear / correct an entry |
| 명세표 | *myeong-se-pyo* | Transaction slip (receipt) |

Near the end, the machine will often speak, too. **카드를 받으세요** (*ka-deu-reul ba-deu-se-yo*) means "Take your card," and **현금을 받으세요** (*hyeon-geu-meul ba-deu-se-yo*) means "Take your cash." Korean ATMs usually return the card first, so don't walk off when you hear the first beep.

## How Koreans actually say "withdraw money"

The screen says **출금**, but nobody says that in conversation. In everyday speech, people use one of two verbs:

- **돈 뽑다** (*don ppop-da*) — literally "pull out money." This is the casual, everyday one. **돈 좀 뽑으려고요** (*don jom ppo-beu-ryeo-go-yo*), "I'm trying to take out some cash."
- **돈 찾다** (*don chat-da*) — literally "find money," meaning to collect it from your account. **돈 찾으러 가요** (*don cha-jeu-reo ga-yo*), "I'm going to get some cash."

Both are natural with friends, clerks, or hotel staff. **인출하다** (*in-chul-ha-da*) is the formal, written version, the same **인출** you saw in **현금인출기**.

## When something goes wrong

ATM trouble is stressful because you're holding up a line and your card is inside a machine. Keep these short lines ready. Each one works on its own, with no extra grammar:

- **카드가 안 나와요** (*ka-deu-ga an na-wa-yo*) — "My card won't come out."
- **돈이 안 나왔어요** (*do-ni an na-wa-sseo-yo*) — "The money didn't come out."
- **비밀번호가 틀렸대요** (*bi-mil-beon-ho-ga teul-lyeot-dae-yo*) — "It says my PIN is wrong."
- **도와주세요** (*do-wa-ju-se-yo*) — "Please help me."

If the machine is inside a bank, show a staff member the screen and use one of these lines. If it's in a convenience store, the clerk usually can't open it, but there's almost always a phone number on the machine for the operator. Pointing at it and saying **여기 전화해 주실 수 있어요?** (*yeo-gi jeon-hwa-hae ju-sil su i-sseo-yo?*), "Could you call this number for me?" gets help faster than trying to explain.

## Mistakes to avoid

**Mixing up 출금 and 입금.** They look alike and differ by one syllable. **출** means "out," **입** means "in." If you want cash out, you want **출**.

**Pronouncing ATM the English way.** Koreans will usually understand, but *e-i-ti-em* is clearer, especially in a noisy store.

**Saying 현금인출기 in conversation.** It's correct, but it sounds like reading a sign aloud. Stick with **에이티엠** when you talk.

**Leaving before the slip prints.** If you pressed **명세표 출력** (print slip), wait for it. And remember the order: card, then cash.

## Practice it before you need cash

The hard part at an ATM isn't the vocabulary, it's the pressure of someone waiting behind you. Say **에이티엠 어디 있어요?**, **외국 카드 돼요?**, and **카드가 안 나와요** out loud a few times now. Watch the tense **ㅃ** in **뽑다**: it's a tight, popped *pp*, not a soft *b*. Get that one sound right and **돈 좀 뽑으려고요** sounds natural on the first try.
